# Aesthetic judging: a miner's coat skirt while it lies on its back

You are one of three separate aesthetic judges for a small stylised 3D game, Wonder Gather. You did not make what you see.
Judge only what is in the pictures, against the game's own aesthetic guide. Be concrete and honest. A verdict of
"neither is good" is allowed.

## What the body was told to do

Small, one of the three miners: a short, sturdy, child-proportioned figure (not a child) with a blue work coat whose
skirt below the waist is cut into four flaps (a front and a back flap on each leg), grey trousers and brown boots. On
its belt: a lit lantern (yellow glow) and an orange bag. Those are part of the costume, not the thing judged.

It lies on its back on a dirt path. Its right leg stays down. Its left leg is moved into a pose and held still until
its clothes settle. These are poses a body passes through while lying, sitting up and getting up off the ground.

The poses (one sheet each, `pose_0.png` to `pose_6.png`):

|---|---|---|---|

Each sheet has two rows, **A** (top) and **B** (bottom): the same pose drawn two ways. Columns, left to right: seen 47
degrees down from the side of its feet and its left, 47 degrees down from the side of its head and its right, and
straight above (its head toward the bottom of that picture). The pictures carry no marks: they are what a player would
see. One thing to look for: the grey trousers are cut off a little above the coat's hem, so where the coat does not
cover that top edge, it shows as the grey tube's hard end or a dark hollow ring, and the leg can look detached from the
body. Which row is which way changes from sheet to sheet.

## The guide

Read, in the project at `D:\Programs\Unity\Projects\Wonder Gather\The Wanderer\Docs\ArtDirection\`:
`VisualSoul.md`, `TheEssence.md`, `CharacterPractices.md` (whole), and in `TheMiners.md` whatever is about clothing,
the coat, cloth and the body's silhouette (search it; it is long). The game director's standing words on movement and
look: "a little stylised, but reading natural"; every view in the game counts.

## Your lens

One of three, each judge given one (November 14, 2026, the skirt's flaps):

- **The cloth:** you look as a costume maker who knows how heavy wool cloth hangs and lies: what gravity does to a
  skirt panel sewn along its top edge when the wearer lies down, bends a knee, spreads or raises a leg.
- **The guide:** you look as the game's art director: the guide's own words first; does each way keep the miners'
  look and silhouette and the "a little stylised, but reading natural" the director asks for?
- **The player:** you look as a player at the game's usual distance: what you notice first in each picture, whether
  a leg looks broken off or detached or the clothes look broken, and which way you would not notice at all.

A referee then sees the three verdicts and the sheets, and keeps only what is really in the pictures.

## What to answer (plain text, at most 600 words)

1. For each sheet: A or B (or neither) reads more like a real heavy coat's skirt lying on a real body under gravity,
   and why, in one or two lines.
2. Every flaw you can see in either row, with the sheet, row and column: cloth through the body or the ground, cloth
   floating, a shape no cloth could make, a sharp spike or fold, a leg that looks detached, the trousers' cut top edge
   showing.
3. Does either row of any sheet break something in the guide? Name the sheet and row, and quote the guide's words you
   rely on (short).
4. Overall (rows swap from sheet to sheet, so say it by what the rows do, not by letter): the single worst thing left
   in the rows you preferred, and anything that would make you reject both ways.
