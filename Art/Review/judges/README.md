# The judges' briefs

Each file here is what one judge of a movement is told. The method, and
why it is this one, is in
[the judging of movement](../../../Docs/ArtDirection/MotionJudging.md).

| Brief | The judge | Its question |
|---|---|---|
| `body.md` | The body | Where is the weight, and what holds it up? |
| `animator.md` | The animator | Does anything start, stop, or move as nothing with weight does? |
| `sceptic.md` | The sceptic | Where is the fake? |
| `referee.md` | The referee | Which of the judges' findings are really in the frames? |
| `alive.md` | Given to every judge beside its brief (since October 9) | Is it alive? Eight things a living body does |

**How a judge is called.** A separate agent, starting with nothing, is
given: its brief (the whole file) and `alive.md`; the sheet or sheets of frames (their
paths, and how the frames are numbered and timed); what the body was
told to do, in one or two sentences; and the figures from the trace if
there is one. It is *not* told what was changed, what I think of the
movement, or what earlier judges said.

**Rules for whoever calls them.**

- A judge sees a movement once. The next round has new judges.
- The three judges do not see each other's findings. The referee sees
  all three.
- No more than three rounds for one movement. What is still open then is
  written down as open, and shown to Luis so.
- A judge's verdict is not Luis's.
