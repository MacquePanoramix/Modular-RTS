# Wonder Gather — Design Rationale

This file preserves a **small number of high-value reasons**.

It does not replace `Docs/GAME_VISION.md`, which remains the canonical living design source of truth. It exists for a narrower problem:

> a competent future contributor can reproduce a rule while still losing the reason the rule mattered.

Add to this file only when losing the **why** could plausibly make the project become technically correct and spiritually wrong.

---

# The central tension

Wonder Gather lives between two gestures.

**Wonder** looks outward: toward distance, possibility, strange systems, old worlds, unfamiliar creatures, and the feeling that something larger is quietly happening.

**Gather** looks inward: toward small details, warmth, attachment, craftsmanship, people doing things with their hands, and the pleasure of bringing scattered pieces into one coherent place.

The project is strongest when it holds both at once.

A useful image is:

> **one eye on the constellation, one hand in the soil.**

---

## 1. The player should want to watch

**Principle**

Observation is part of the game, not decoration around the game.

**Why**

The player is not only trying to win with a civilization. They are watching something that existed first as an idea acquire bodies, habits, buildings, routes, mistakes, victories, and little histories.

A good system should sometimes make the player stop optimizing simply because what is happening is worth seeing.

**Failure shape**

- close zoom exists, but there is nothing interesting to see;
- units are strategically distinct but visually interchangeable;
- spectacle replaces attachment.

---

## 2. Systems should make stories without pretending to be stories

**Principle**

Prefer coherent rules whose interactions naturally create small, legible events.

**Why**

The project is drawn to emergent worlds: a frightened fighter breaks, a builder chooses an awkward route, a settlement grows through an unusual dependency chain, a body adapts to a slope. These moments are most satisfying when they arise from understandable systems rather than scripted theatrics.

**Failure shape**

- random behavior mistaken for emergence;
- authored "cinematic moments" that have no systemic consequence;
- complexity that produces noise rather than stories.

---

## 3. Physicality exists to make beings feel embodied

**Principle**

Procedural motion and physics serve presence, not physics spectacle.

**Why**

The goal is not to prove that a body can wobble. The goal is to make the player believe that this particular creature occupies space, carries weight, meets terrain, and expresses its nature through movement.

**Failure shape**

- slapstick instability;
- ragdolls because ragdolls are impressive;
- close-up realism that destroys RTS responsiveness;
- physical simulation so dominant that the player stops feeling in command.

---

## 4. Mechanics should carry flavor inside them

**Principle**

Aesthetic and mechanical choice should meet whenever possible.

**Why**

The fantasy is not "I changed movement from 100% to 135%." It is "I made a long-legged, lightly armored, cautious people who travel differently because of what they are."

Appearance should not be an interchangeable skin laid over a spreadsheet.

**Failure shape**

- raw percentages become the final creative language;
- visual choices are mechanically empty;
- different fictional identities resolve to the same play texture.

---

## 5. A civilization is a way of becoming

**Principle**

A faction is not fundamentally a roster. It is a network of possibilities reachable from a beginning.

**Why**

The player authors not only **what exists**, but **how it can come to exist**.

A powerful unit hidden behind a fragile chain tells a different strategic story from the same unit available directly at the starting base.

**Failure shape**

- civilization design collapses into choosing units from a menu;
- progression becomes a mandatory conventional age ladder;
- dependencies are treated as UI clutter rather than part of strategy.

---

## 6. Strange civilizations deserve room to breathe

**Principle**

Prefer warnings and visible consequences over prohibition when coherence permits it.

**Why**

Creativity includes making things that are risky, inefficient, fragile, specialized, or simply odd. The game should not "correct" those designs into normality unless a shared competitive contract truly requires it.

**Failure shape**

- validator becomes a taste police;
- suboptimal becomes synonymous with invalid;
- matchmaking restrictions quietly redefine the entire creative sandbox.

---

## 7. Simple orders, rich interpretation

**Principle**

The player's command vocabulary should stay readable while unit bodies, traits, circumstances, and eventually personalities enrich execution.

**Why**

Wonder Gather wants beings with agency without becoming a cockpit of micro-buttons.

The interesting layer lives between:

**player intention → issued order → unit interpretation → embodied action.**

**Failure shape**

- every interesting behavior requires manual micro;
- units "have personality" by ignoring the player unpredictably;
- perfect obedience removes the sense of inner life.

---

## 8. Slowness is attention, not lethargy

**Principle**

The slower pace exists to make room for noticing.

**Why**

A civilization that is worth watching needs time to be watched.

Construction, travel, preparation, hesitation, and small battles can carry emotional and visual weight only if the player is not constantly fighting an attention emergency.

**Failure shape**

- numbers are slower but interface demands remain frantic;
- waiting has no visual or strategic meaning;
- speed becomes the only way to create tension.

---

## 9. Grandeur and intimacy should coexist

**Principle**

The world should feel larger than the immediate encounter while the immediate encounter remains worth looking at.

**Why**

Wonder Gather is interested in both the civilization-scale view and the individual body.

Grand scale can come from landscape, distance, silhouettes, landmarks, memory, and consequence—not only unit count.

**Failure shape**

- hundreds of units erase individual readability;
- tiny maps make the civilization feel like a board game;
- beautiful scale creates travel that is merely tedious.

---

## 10. A prototype may be true without becoming permanent

**Principle**

Implemented is not the same as Locked.

**Why**

Experimentation is how the project discovers itself. Temporary structures need permission to exist, teach us something, and later disappear.

**Failure shape**

- Supplies becomes the final economy because it already works;
- current performance sliders become the final customization model;
- prototype bounds become accidental balance doctrine;
- one successful biped decides the anatomy architecture forever.

**Wonder Gather shorthand**

> **A prototype may bloom in the meadow without becoming a law of the world.**

---

## 11. Things a player creates become emotionally expensive

**Principle**

Persistence deserves ceremony proportional to what it protects.

**Why**

A camera value is disposable. A civilization a player spent an evening imagining may not be.

Once the game invites authorship, save data becomes part of the creative relationship with the player.

**Failure shape**

- convenient migrations silently reinterpret old factions;
- unknown data is rewritten because the parser can;
- stable identity is treated as implementation trivia.

---

## 12. Evidence and taste illuminate different things

**Principle**

Tests, builds, screenshots, and human playtests answer different questions.

**Why**

A test can prove a foot remained planted. It cannot prove the walk feels beautiful. Luis can love the walk and still miss a persistence bug.

Neither form of judgment should impersonate the other.

**Wonder Gather shorthand**

> **A footprint proves someone stood there. It does not tell us whether the view was worth the walk.**

---

# A final orientation

When two technically reasonable choices are available, prefer the one that better supports:

- worlds that feel alive rather than merely populated;
- systems whose interactions are visible;
- aesthetic meaning embedded in mechanics;
- room for strange player authorship;
- the strategic view and the intimate view at once;
- warmth without sentimentality;
- wonder without nonsense;
- complexity that can still be watched and understood.

That is not a specification.

It is a compass.
