## How it was recorded

### The three choices

- **A, how far the physics goes: a mixture of (a) and (b).** Characters are
  "very stable". Falling is not easy, and the body is not "naturally
  wobbly". But falling is not impossible: in an extreme situation that
  calls for it, a character can trip or fall "in this, like, ragdoll-ish
  way". Luis's examples: running; a sword thrust that is too strong; "a
  really strong force pulling them"; a very weak miner whose limbs give
  out. Luis asked for research into whether the two can be combined.
- **B, the lantern and the mug: (a).** "The items hanging somewhere, like
  that makes physical sense, from the clothes … is actually a good idea."
- **C, the order: approved.** "I like the order that you proposed. So yeah,
  let's go with it." Where the new things below fit is left to Claude: "you
  can choose the order … where you think it fits most".

### New direction

- **Built to be built upon.** "I want to later build upon what we do for
  this high-fi prototype." The final game will have running and sword
  fights; what is made now must be able to grow into them.
- **Stamina.** "Some level of stamina for the muscles and tiredness",
  already in this prototype. Short-term at least ("both short-term and
  maybe, I don't know, maybe just a short-term stamina").
- **The interaction click.** A feature for the final game, wanted in a
  simple form now:
  - the ordinary right-click keeps doing the ordinary RTS thing;
  - a special click on something that can be interacted with shows its
    options beside it, "MMO style", in a small box of text;
  - the options always include a cancel;
  - it is given by a key that is very good to press in an RTS. This is to
    be researched. It would be "the most important" key other than moving
    the map, "maybe even more";
  - **what it is for:** "actions that I want to have in my game that I
    don't know how you would click on them on a usual RTS";
  - **examples for the prototype:** on an accessory, "equip this item" or
    "hold the item"; for the pickaxe, unequip it, "just laying it on the
    ground". Luis is "thinking if there's any other that makes sense".
- **Every action is physical.** "I want all the actions to feel physical
  and real … It's not just the important actions, like mining, movement,
  or resource collecting, or fighting … It needs to be all the actions."
  Unequipping, strapping the pickaxe on the back, putting something in the
  bag: each is seen done by the body, believably and simply, in the
  procedural way, "with real weight to everything".

### Not said, so not assumed

- Luis did not choose the key.
- Luis did not fix the list of interaction options: the examples are
  examples.
- Luis did not say how tiredness shows, or how long it lasts.
- Luis did not say to merge the branch into `main`.
- How Long stands in the game (P1) and the walk's high step stay open.
