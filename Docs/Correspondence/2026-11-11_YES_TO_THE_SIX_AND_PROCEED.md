## What it decides

The six questions, each as recommended:

1. **Roll first, in the first build.** A miner on its back or side rolls onto its front before it gets up. Sitting up
   from the back comes in the second build.
2. **A body too weak to get up** gets as far as it can, rests there, and tries again when it has recovered. Never the old
   animation.
3. **Natural variation** is on by default, with a slider on the panel to set its size by eye.
4. **The build opens with the new way switched on**, the old one a click away, and the panel names which way the last
   getting up used.
5. **The three miners' strengths:** a panel switch "strength by its build" is added; the default stays as today.
6. **The last moment of a rise:** the standing balance keeps its use of up to twice a joint's strength for the first
   build; it is measured, and the capped one is shown beside it later.

With that the design of the motion (layers 2 and 3) is agreed as it was put to him in plain words, and it may be built.

## What he adds

"A miner falling forward should still also be able to get up", though "they do almost always fall back".

The assistant's reading (its own, not Luis's words): the roll-first rule of question 1 is for a body on its back or side.
A body that lands on its front gets up from there without rolling, in the first build too, and it is tested as fully as
the common fall even though it is the rare one (about one fall in ten on the bench): the falls forward are forced in
the test runs, so that they are counted and looked at, not left to chance. "Some of the features need to be further
improved" is taken as his general expectation that the first build is not the end of it, not as a list.
