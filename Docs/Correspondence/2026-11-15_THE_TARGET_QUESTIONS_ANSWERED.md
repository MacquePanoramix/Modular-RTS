## What he was asked

1. Which screen the looks are judged on: (a) the ultrawide he plays on (3440x1440); (b) the laptop's own screen
   (2560x1600); (c) both. Recommended: (a), with one check on the laptop screen at the end of each look.
2. The painted strokes' size: (a) kept the size he knows on any screen; (b) finer and sharper on bigger screens.
   Recommended: (a), with one picture of (b) beside it.
3. How smooth: (a) a steady 60 pictures a second with the best looks; (b) aim higher, giving up some looks.
   Recommended: (a).
4. When the visual work stops and shows him: the busy crowd scene no longer holds a steady 60 on his screen; one new
   look takes more than about a tenth of a picture's time; all the new looks together make a picture more than 30%
   slower than today. The physics line (50 standing bodies taking more than a quarter of a picture's time) is only
   reported until the test of how the physics spreads across the cores exists. Recommended: these lines, revised after
   the first measurement.

## What it decides

- The looks are judged first on his ultrawide. **His new ask:** every look must also work on the common screen sizes,
  since other people will test and play the game. Read as: the common shapes (16:9, 16:10, 21:9) from 1920x1080 up to
  3840x2160, the list taken from Steam's survey and proposed at tier 0. The instruments measure those sizes too.
- The strokes keep the size he knows on any screen; one picture of finer strokes is shown beside them.
- A steady 60 pictures a second at the best looks.
- The stop lines as recommended, revised after the first measurement.
- The unit count stays **Open**: its questions come after the crowd measurement.
