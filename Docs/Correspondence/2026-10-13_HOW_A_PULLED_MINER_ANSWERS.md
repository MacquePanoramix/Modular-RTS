# How a pulled miner answers — October 13, 2026

**From:** Luis (Game Director). Two messages, the afternoon of October
13, on the question left open since October 12: what a pulled miner is
to be held to. Luis had not yet tried the step in the build.

## What was asked

The bar for a pulled miner had been taken from the body as it was
posed, which leans against a sixth of its weight without moving its
feet. A body moved only by its joints cannot do that from rest (about
0.09 of its weight; past that it must step). In the game on October 13:
pulled with 0.08 of its weight it takes a step or two and stands; with
an eighth or more it falls. The question, as first put, was "What is a
pulled miner to be held to?"

## The first message (verbatim)

> Oh also sorry I didn't see. I still need to try and judge the last step but for question 2:
>
> What do you mean with your question? I didn't understand it very well.

The question was put again in plain terms, as three things Luis might
want to see when a miner is pulled:

1. it steps along with the pull and stays on its feet;
2. it holds its ground without moving its feet, as the posed body did
   (which an honest body can do only by first bracing itself);
3. it may fall when pulled hard: pulls do not matter yet.

With a suggestion: the first for gentle pulls, the third for hard ones
for now, until something in the game really pulls a miner.

## The second message (verbatim)

> Yeah I like your suggestion. Like I mentioned before, I don't want anything to be a pre-made animation, and it should not switch from one kind of response to the other at a certain threshold. It should be a smooth transition that follows how a person is expected to really react to pulls. Makes sense? It should also be based on their capabilities of course.

## Held against this, the build of October 13

Written by me; Luis has not seen the build.

- **As Luis says:** nothing in the step is pre-made. A little pull is
  leaned against without a step, more with more lean (in its ankles,
  and its trunk leaning back by degrees); a step comes when leaning
  cannot hold it; a longer step for a harder shove; each miner's joints
  are as strong as that miner is, and Round, with its wide stance,
  stands a sideways shove through that the others step for.
- **Not as Luis says, and to be mended:**
  - **Between leaning and stepping there is a jump.** The smallest
    step it takes is about 22 cm, however little it was needed. A
    person takes a small step for a small need.
  - **The three step on one clock** (leaving the ground to landing in
    0.14 to 0.16 s, whatever the leg). Being measured on October 13: a
    swing that takes longer for a longer leg.
  - Several things in the keeper begin at a line (it steps when where
    its weight is going is 3.5 cm outside its soles; the boot behind is
    lifted when it bears under 12 hundredths of its weight). A step is
    one event and has to begin at some moment, as a person's does; what
    must not jump is how large the answer is. Each such line is to be
    looked at for that.
  - Its tiredness is not in it: a tired miner steps as a fresh one.
