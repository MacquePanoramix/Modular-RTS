# The body's own, not posed; stable; natural and alive — October 9, 2026

**From:** Luis (Game Director), in answer to the report at the end of the
playtest round ([the round's page](../Reviews/2026-10-08_ThePlaytestRound.md)),
which had asked, first of all, whether movements are to be posed or the
body's own
([the table of which is which](../Reviews/2026-10-08_ThePlaytestRound.md#what-is-still-posed-and-what-is-the-bodys-own)).
Luis had not been able to play the build.

## The message (verbatim)

> Yeah I feel I want stability in the body but with it still being the body's own. I don't want it to be posed like that. Also, I think you can research and look for what would be a reasonable time for the miners to lay down. You can also make it dependent on factors such as how strong was the force that pushed it down, it's current stamina and anything else relevant you can think. Also the rest I couldn't open a test to see how your progress was so far, but I liked the comments you were making. Now look I really do want movement to feel natural and alive rather than robotic. That's why it can't be posed and we must do media studies and research of what kind of movements feel natural for certain actions. Also good job with the agent reviews, please continue with that for further steps always also.

## How it was recorded

**The answer to the question** (recorded as a **Direction**, in Luis's
words; it is not Locked, and how it is to be built is not decided by
it):

- **"The body's own", and not posed:** "I want stability in the body but
  with it still being the body's own. I don't want it to be posed like
  that." And again: "it can't be posed".
- **Stable:** "I want stability in the body". This keeps Luis's choice A
  of October 6 ("very stable, never naturally wobbly", able to fall "if
  it's an extreme situation that calls for it") and joins it to the
  body's own movement.
- **Natural and alive, not robotic:** "I really do want movement to feel
  natural and alive rather than robotic."

**What Luis asks to be done:**

| | Luis | |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Research and look for what would be a reasonable time for the miners to lay down", and "make it dependent on factors such as how strong was the force that pushed it down, it's current stamina and anything else relevant you can think" | A fallen miner lay 1.2 s whatever had happened to it |
| 2 | "We must do media studies and research of what kind of movements feel natural for certain actions" | The research of October 8 was of real bodies (physical therapy, gait studies) and of how simulated ones are made. Media (how films and games make a movement read as natural and alive) was hardly in it |
| 3 | "Good job with the agent reviews, please continue with that for further steps always also" | A standing instruction: every further step is reviewed by judges who did not make it |

**What Luis liked.** "I liked the comments you were making"; "good job
with the agent reviews". Nothing is promoted to Locked by that.

**What Luis did not say:**

- **Luis has not played the build:** "the rest I couldn't open a test to
  see how your progress was so far". Nothing of the round is accepted,
  and none of its other questions is answered (the lamplight, Long's two
  pickaxes, the pickaxe hanging from the hand, the walk's slower start
  and the two-second turn back, the mug, the button that pushes a miner
  over).
- **Which movements, and in what order.** The walk, the turn, going down
  for a tool, taking the lantern and standing up are posed today. That
  they are to become the body's own follows from the message; which
  first, and whether S4's carrying waits for them, was not said.
- **How.** A body moved wholly by its own joints and still "never
  naturally wobbly" is the hard part; the message asks for both and
  leaves the way open.
- Luis did not say to merge anything into `main`.
