## What Luis saw, looked at

Recorded the same night (Small, pushed over at 2 m/s; five pictures a
second; the camera going with it).

- **The getting up.** It steps back for half a second and falls. From
  1.4 s to 7 s after the push it lies in a heap and does not change:
  **five and a half seconds in which it looks dead.** From 7 s to
  10.6 s its limbs move: an arm reaches, its legs kick up behind it, it
  rolls ("convulsing"). At 10.8 s it is turned round; at 11.0 to 11.6 s
  it is on its hands and knees and then kneeling; **at 12.0 s it
  stands, a quarter turn from how it faced, four tenths of a second
  after kneeling** ("magical"). Luis's words fit what is in the
  pictures. The states behind them: falling, lying (a time reckoned
  from how hard it fell), gathering (rows of poses its let-go body is
  driven through, found by a search), rising (posed).
- **The shove pressed many times.** Measured, not seen in the pictures
  at first: shoved again and again, a miner went on trying to catch
  itself with steps of up to one and a half times its leg in an eighth
  of a second. **A boot went at up to 41 m/s, and a sole a metre over
  the ground behind it,** in 8 of 9 ways of pressing tried, all three
  miners. **Mended in part.** Kept, because they act only when it is
  already lost: a catch that would need its boot to go further than
  three quarters of its leg is not tried, nor a fourth catch in a
  row; it goes down into the fall instead. With them the quick
  pressing is clean (under 4.5 m/s), and **in 3 or 4 of the 9 ways a
  boot still goes at 9 to 20 m/s.** Three more things were tried that
  night and taken off again, each because it spoilt the step when
  nothing was wrong (the plan, "October 13, the night after Luis
  tried it"). The test of it is kept with its bar, and is not yet
  met.
