## What he was answering, as it was sent

From `Docs/Research/2026-11-13_UnnaturalMovement/catalogue.md`, section 1, in the report's short form:

1. How much slower should a bigger body be? (Like a pendulum, twice as tall about 1.4 times slower; or keeping the
   hands' and feet's speed, twice as tall twice as slow; real animals lie between.)
2. Which camera views count?
3. How long may a get-up take? (He had found it "so long to get up" on October 30.)
4. Should a reaction be exactly as big as the push, or a little bigger? (Viewers forgive a reaction a little bigger
   than the push deserves more readily than a smaller one.)
5. Should the movement be true to people, or a little stylised?

And: "One more choice, for turns: the limit taken from people flags a hip twisted more than 35° over a planted foot
and calls it a fault past 45°, but the walk allows 62°. Both limits stay in until you choose."

## What it settles, and what it leaves to judgment

- **Camera (2): decided by Luis.** Every view the game can show counts, the cinematic camera's included: the game is
  to look beautiful from every angle it can be seen from.
- **Stylised (5): decided by Luis.** A little stylised, as long as it reads natural and fits the game's visual
  philosophy and aesthetic; true people are the guide, stylised where that serves the look.
- **Get-up length (3): decided by Luis as a principle, no number.** What feels natural for each body and situation;
  longer is allowed when the get-up is hard, but a longer get-up must show why (bodily cues of effort that make the
  time read natural), always with a design reason.
- **Size and speed (1): left to research, with his eye if research is not convincing.** Part reality, part what feels
  natural; size should matter. If the research does not settle it, he is shown moving examples to choose from.
- **Reaction size (4): left to judgment.** He did not follow the question; it is to be decided by the design
  principles and explained plainly.
- **The turns' twist limit: left to a full review and judgment**, leaning to the limit taken from people unless there
  is a deeper reason for the other.
- **Agents:** Opus 5.5 leads; a Fable sub-agent may be used, at the thinking level judged right, for a task that
  really calls for it. Quality is never sacrificed; speed where it costs no quality.
