# Review B: step 0's facts at the true turns, and the design

**Verdict: sound with changes.** The record's reading of step 0 at the true turns is right as far as it goes; the proposed unfloored share loop and the summed-only K5 bar must not run as proposed (findings 2, 4, 6).

## Findings

1. **The record compares the ankle's give with what it *asked*, but R1 reads it against what spring and damper *said*; by R1's own measure the shortfall is larger.** Claim: "0.74 to 0.89 of the law's" (Validation.md, "The share under the solver's turns") is gave/asked, with `asked` = k(target - q) - c*omega at the step's start (ArticulationFactsTests.cs 1514-1515). R1 (motion_design.md 711-713) reads gave over k*e - (dt*k + c)*omega_end. Evidence, levers_pgs/step0true lines "FACTS share under turns ... of what it said in the first step": at 14/4 Small 0.443 (law 0.697), Long 0.379 (0.532), Round 0.296 (0.451), i.e. 0.64, 0.71, 0.66 of the law; at 28/4 0.77, 0.84, 0.77; at 60/4 0.90, 0.90, 0.88. Weight: should. Do: state the "said" ratios in the record and in R1, since R1's floor and reading are defined on "said"; the "asked" ratio overstates the share R1 would read.

2. **The standing leg's shares still follow K8's form 1 - (1 - x)^n, but with an x about half to two thirds of the law's: the law's R (1/I of the boot) is the held-parent case, and the standing leg's parent turns.** Claim: solving the nine "said" shares for x (n = 18, 32, 64 turns) gives one x per miner, steady across the turns: Small 0.032, 0.035, 0.034 (law 0.064, ratio 0.50 to 0.55); Long 0.026, 0.030, 0.029 (law 0.041, 0.63 to 0.72); Round 0.019, 0.022, 0.023 (law 0.033, 0.59 to 0.70). (My arithmetic from the logged shares; e.g. x fitted at 14/4 predicts Small at 60/4 0.875 against 0.887 read.) K8's rig (ArticulationFactsTests.cs `Stood`, lines ~546-590: an immovable root, the weight on slides or fixed, so the ankle's parent cannot turn) is exactly the case where R = 1/I_boot. In K4(a)'s rig the root floats and the shin, knee and weight turn, so the drive row's response R includes the parent side's articulated inverse inertia: aR larger, x smaller, share lower, and pose-dependent. This is a reasoned explanation, consistent with every logged count of turns; not proven. So the record's "the ankle gives less than K8's law says" is better read as "K8's law with the wrong R"; the law is not refuted, its input is. Weight: should (it changes what R1's floor is and how it is reckoned). Do: before any rerun, write a bar: the share predicted by x = 1/(1 + a*R_jj), R_jj the joint's diagonal of the articulation's joint-space inverse inertia at that pose (floating root, contacts left out; computed from the articulation, or measured by one probe impulse on the joint with the ground away), lies within 5% of the "said" share in all nine miner-by-turns cases of `TheStandingLegsShareUnderTheSolversTurns`, and within 5% of K8's stiff-ankle readings (where it must reduce to 1/I_boot). If it holds, K8 and R1 say "R is the joint's response in the whole articulation, reckoned each step from the pose", not "1/I, reckoned once when the body is made" (motion_design.md 715, 871-874).

3. **The record's reading of K4(a) at 14 and 4 holds as far as it goes: the early miss is the ankle's give, not the sole tipping; the late fall is the sole.** Evidence (step0true.log, "FACTS fall traced, Small with the making-up"): steps 1 to 8 the ankle gave 14.8, 15.6, 16.5 of 31.4, 34.8, 37.3 asked (0.47, 0.45, 0.44), while the boot tilted only -0.19 to -0.42 degrees and its centre moved under 0.5 mm; the offset grew -0.50, -1.81, -2.59 degrees. Only from about step 20 (tilt -2.1) to 52 (+37.6, centre up 52 mm) does the sole rock. The gravity-off control stood exactly. Weight: note. Do: nothing more for the reading; say in the record that the fall's first half second is the drive's share and its end is the sole, so that R2's "flat" read and R1's making-up are not blamed for each other.

4. **An unfloored share read from driveForce has no fixed point where the give stops growing with the ask, and then ratchets the target to its offset bound.** Claim: R1 writes target = q + (wanted)/s, so said = wanted/s and the reading r = given/said. If the drive's give has a ceiling G below what is wanted (a contact, a limit row, the sole's edge, the parent's mobility), r = G*s/wanted < s at every s, so s <- s + g(r - s) decays geometrically to nothing, and the target runs to eFull + dt*omega_max, i.e. the ankle at full strength on a part that cannot take it. Evidence: Round without the making-up (step0true.log, "fall traced, Round without the making-up", steps 3 to 6): the ankle gave +21.6 N m four steps running while asked rose 68.6, 71.6, 73.3, 74.5 (the share 0.31 to 0.29 and falling), with the boot flat (-2.2 degrees); the "share falls as the ask grows" in the record is this. The cause of the plateau is not known (not a contact tip: the boot is flat). Also, with r independent of s, the update with a one-step lag is a first-order loop, monotone for w*a <= 1 and stable for w*a < 2; the danger is not the lag but r depending on s (dr/ds > 0 as above), and the sign of said flipping (r then meaningless), and small said (r noisy). Weight: blocks (the proposed change, the share "rather than floored at the law's share", must not run without these bounds). Do: find what caps Round's give at 21.6 N m first (log the ankle's limit rows and the knee's told force in the same steps); then set, before the run: (a) a floor s >= 0.5 x the law's share (corrected by finding 2 if that holds; at 14/4 with K8's law: Small 0.35, Long 0.27, Round 0.23; the lowest "said" share read anywhere is 0.284, Round's five-step mean), ceiling 1; (b) the reading as a least-squares share over the last 5 steps, r = sum(given*said)/sum(said^2), clamped to [0, 1.2], so small asks weigh little and a sign change weighs by itself, smoothly, with no threshold (Luis's rule); (c) the weight w further times said^2/(said^2 + T0^2), T0 = 0.05 S (as R1 has), and times a smooth measure of whether give grows with ask over the window (the slope of given on said, over r, clamped 0 to 1), so that a plateau teaches nothing instead of a lower share; (d) rates: a_hold*w <= 0.1 a step (a time constant of at least 10 steps towards held), a_free*w <= 0.5; (e) R1's offset bound unchanged. Better still: let the corrected law of finding 2 give the share each step from the pose and the turns read back, and let the reading learn only a slow residual factor in [0.7, 1.3].

5. **Bars for that run, set before it** (proposed; for whoever builds). On K4(a)'s rig at 14 and 4, each miner, the share read with (a) to (e): (i) K4(a)'s own bar: stands 2 s and every joint within 2 degrees of its goal after 2 s; (ii) the share over the last second within +/-5% of its mean, and its step-to-step change changes sign at most 2 times in that second (no limit cycle); (iii) the share never within 10% of its floor for more than 5 steps running, and the ankle's told force never at its strength for more than 3 steps running, and the target never at its offset bound for more than 5 steps running (no ratchet); (iv) the control, the share frozen at K8's law, falls as step0true did (Small 1.04 s, Long 1.54, Round 1.16 within 0.1 s), so the loop is what holds; (v) R1's own edge case (a sole on an edge, the making-up on, the share read step by step): the boot's tilt amplitude over the last second no larger than over the second after it settles, and K5's energy bar of finding 6 not breached. Weight: should. Do: write these into the facts test's comment before its run.

6. **K5: the summed bar alone can hide a one-step injection; the trips come from the count, so fix the count and keep a per-step guard with a tolerance.** Claim: (1) the count (ArticulationFactsTests.cs 1252-1257, `SweepAt`) multiplies the told force by the mean of the told joint speeds at the two step ends, a trapezoid that lags the implicit solve; R1 itself says the work of a step is the told torque times the joint's actual turn, and the speed the engine tells is not always the motion. The step-by-step reading (step0true.log, "FACTS sweep count, Small at 5 rad/s": beyond -0.0029, +0.0010, +0.0005, +0.0002 in steps 1 to 4) is that lag's signature. (2) The per-step clause divides by what the dampers took in that step, which goes to nothing when a joint's speed turns round; any count error then trips it, which is the likelier reading of the 1e-4 J trips at 5 rad/s, steps 19 to 25, than "rounding" (1e-4 J is about 1e-5 of Small's 2.59 J, far above float rounding of the sum, but tiny). (3) A whole-second sum nets an early gain against a later loss: an injection at step 3 paid back by extra loss at step 10 passes, and that is exactly a one-frame flick, which Luis sees. The summed fraction also grows steeply with speed (0.036, 0.31, 0.52 for Small at 10, 20, 30 rad/s), so the step's own injection is real at speed. Weight: should. Do: set before the run: (a) the count from the joint's position change, work = sum driveForce[d] * dq[d], and the dampers' take = sum min(c*|dq/dt|, S)*|dq|; (b) clause A unchanged (never above 1.0001 of the first step's energy); (c) clause B, prefix: at every step t, sum over steps 1..t of the gain beyond the count <= 0.5 x sum over 1..t of the dampers' take + 2e-4 x the first step's energy (0.5 mJ for Small, 2.5 mJ for Long at 5 rad/s); (d) clause C, per step: the gain beyond the count <= 0.5 x that step's take + 1e-3 x the energy then held (a one-frame flick is orders above that); (e) the whole second <= 0.5 x the dampers' take, as proposed. Run first at 5 and 10 rad/s with the new count as a diagnostic with no bar, to see that the lag is gone (per-step beyond under 1e-4 of the energy held), then the sweep with the bar. Bounds from the sweep only after that.
