# Animator judge: stance sheets (A vs B)

Ranked findings

1. Squat h=0.35 flat, Long A/B (fit 0.49/0.48, poor), Round A/B (0.34), front and back views: arms stick out sideways and upward, straight, like wings or a scarecrow, held where nothing holds them. Long A worst (both arms out wide, one raised). Round A: one arm up beside the head, the other out and down. Not a body's own. Sure. Small B instead has a forearm across the face (hand over eyes), odd and looks like a hiding pose; Small A is calmer and reads more real.
2. Hands and feet D=0.50, Long A/B (fit 0.42): very stooped, spine near horizontal, head below the hips, hips tiny against a huge trunk capsule, legs straight stilts. Poor fit; reads as an inverted V rather than a bear crawl. Back view: arms and legs form four parallel posts. A/B identical. Sure.
3. Sitting up, hands behind, Small (reserve S 0.56) and Round (0.40): the shoulders carry over half their strength, the trunk is propped on straight, locked, near-vertical arms. Reads as a strained pose; Long (0.15) is far more convincing. Back view: head hides the body, arms splay, outward in a V. Fairly sure.
4. Squat h=0.40 toes: arms cross in front of or over the head or face in every body (Small A, Long A, Round A: forearms stacked and pointing forward at head height, like a diver or a boxer's guard). A is theatrical; B hangs the arms down by the shins and reads more like a body that is balancing. B better for Small, Long, Round. Fairly sure.
5. Rising from half-kneel: arms are tucked into the trunk or across it (Small B, Long B, Round B: forearm across the belly), odd symmetry-free but plausible. A hangs the arms at the sides, more natural, but then they are dead and limp. Back foot is a straight rod; the rear leg is a ruler-straight line from hip to toe in Small and Long (side view), which reads stiff. Slight A preference for Small and Long; no difference for Round. Fairly sure.
6. Half-kneel: Long A/B back leg lies out flat, the foot far behind the knee, shin and thigh form an unnaturally long, straight line; front shin vertical, hands empty. Reads acceptably. Small and Round tuck better. Round: the left forearm hangs in front of the chest, stiff. Small A vs B: no difference.
7. Stand: all six nearly identical; arms hang straight, hips capsule shows no taper, knees dead straight, no sway. Reads as a mannequin, not alive. Long fit 0.35, with the legs very slightly knock-kneed in front view. Round A/B: in the side view the left arm is a bent forearm in front of the chest, hand near the chin, looking odd for a plain stand. A and B differ only in small arm angles (B arms a little wider); no difference.
8. Tall kneel: good in all bodies, upright, plumb line over the knees, feet laid behind. Round feet splay outward, boots flare (Round A/B back view). A and B no difference. Reads real.
9. Hands and knees: good, spine low and head hanging, plumb inside. Long and Small fine. Round: head and trunk heavily forward, hands seem under the chin; reads real for weight. A and B nearly the same (B shows elbow loaded). Small and Long: the forward arms are exactly vertical and parallel, a little table-like.
10. Hands and feet D=0.50 Small, Round: acceptable; Small's hips are beside the arms at the same height, a round ball sitting between the arms and head, head lower than hips. Round OK. Plumb sits between hands and feet, correct.

Across stances
- Repetition: every body, A and B, are nearly clones in stand, kneel, hands-and-knees, bear; A vs B differs only in arms. Arms are the only thing varying, and they vary at random: no arm is ever used for balance in a plausible way. The legs and trunk are strictly symmetrical in the front views (stand, tall kneel, hands and knees, bear): perfect symmetry, robotic. The head is always plumb, looking straight ahead or down, never turned toward the intended direction (hands and knees, bear: head hangs, never looks up).
- Joints are all dead straight when loaded (stand knees, bear legs, sit-up arms); real bodies show small bends.
- Same recipe every stance: arms are the leftover, set to whatever the solver leaves.

Solids vs the finished character
- The hip capsule is wider than the trunk and swallows the thighs; this makes squat and kneel views read as a ball, and hides leg angles. Finished characters would show thighs.
- Hands are not drawn (arm rods end blunt), so hands over the face and arms across the body read worse than they would with hands.
- The boot boxes make the laid-toe and feet look like wedges standing up (tall kneel back view, hands and knees back view: boots stick up like planks). A real foot laid flat would not show.
- Head is a sphere with only a dot; the gaze direction is hard to read.
- Round's very thick trunk hides the arms in the side view.
