## A versus B, per stance (summary)
- stand: no difference (B arms slightly more outward in Small; A tucks arms into the hip: B marginally better).
- hands and knees: no difference.
- bear crawl: no difference in bodies; Small B front-foot slightly less planted; no difference.
- squat flat: Small A (arms hanging) better than B (hand on face); Long and Round A/B both poor, B slightly less wide in Long (arms lower).
- squat toes: B better in all three (arms down along the legs; A has arms thrown at the head).
- tall kneel: no difference.
- half-kneel: A slightly better in Long (hand on knee); no difference Small/Round.
- rising from half-kneel: B slightly better in Small/Long/Round (arm crosses belly, reads as a guard); A arms stiff.

## Across stances
- The arms are the weak point: they are mostly unsolved leftovers, free of the weight, taking odd positions (wing arms, arms on the face, forearm raised in front of the chest) while the legs are well solved. A body keeps its arms where they help balance (forward, out for balance in a squat is fine) but they should hang or reach, and not float high and horizontal.
- Repetition: yes, the same few forms recur: hands in front of the face (squat toes, Round stand and half-kneel), a forearm across the trunk, A/B often differ by almost nothing (hands and knees, tall kneel, sit-up), so there is little variety to choose from. Also identical symmetrical legs in every stance (feet side by side, no weight preference) outside the half-kneels; the standing body is perfectly symmetrical and stiff, with no hip shift.
- The posture is a solved geometry, so heads always face forward, with no head turn, no hand preparation; fine for stills.
- Weight: in every stance the plumb falls inside the base (all positive, 12 to 241 mm), but at squat toes the margin is only 12 to 16 mm, so a very precarious balance on the toes with the arms forward as counterweight: plausible for that, the arms forward there is the one place it makes sense.

## Solids make it look wrong, not the finished body
- The hip capsule is huge and swallows the hands and forearms (stand, kneel), so arms seem to go through the body; a real character would have a hip with a smaller visible bulk.
- Round head sphere on a thin neck and the head with no neck makes the sit-up look like head thrown back; a character with a neck and chin would read differently.
- The boxes for boots hide ankle roll (the toe wedge in the kneels looks like a ramp stuck to the shin); toes appear as separate bricks.
- Heavy-looking back-flip of the hand dot tint (tint shows load, not bad shape).

Verdict per body: Small reads as real, with a flaw (arms, face-hand in squats). Long: reads as real in kneels and half-kneels, does not read as real in the flat squat and the bear crawl (fit 0.42-0.49). Round: reads as real with a flaw (raised forearm at the chest, arms on the head in the squat).

Alive check (stills only): breathing, timing, lag, arcs, repetition cannot be seen; the balance by degrees is not visible; effort shown only by the sit-up (arms straight and stiff, S 0.4-0.56) and the squat toes.
