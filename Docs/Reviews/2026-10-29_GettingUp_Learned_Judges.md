**Praised:** the weight is over what touches the ground in every stage seen; the order of stages is the believable one;
the boots stay where they are put (no slide, hop or stutter, nothing through the ground, no part suddenly somewhere it
was not heading); its parts are not on one clock (the hips start 0.2 s before the head and arrive before it; the head

are real balance; C's pike goes a little the other way first (its head lifts, then sinks as the hips rise); the roll is
pushed by a planted foot; B's rise "the most body-like"; all three ease into the stand rather than stopping dead.

## Since round 1

Faster by 1 to 2 s, the boots held up for less long, five of eight bench starts standing where three did. Still there,
and named by all three again: the waits (the crouch before the rise, the boots up while it lies), the dead-still end,
the slowness. New: the rise's pace, creeping while the legs give most of their strength and then at one speed, where
round 1 saw a gathering dip and no snap. What was learnt (the corrections at the second foot, the pike and the roll up)
was not pointed at. One round is left for this movement (at most three).

**Could not be judged at this size:** the roll, the hands, the knees and the steps (0.4 s apart); whether a hand
touches the ground; breath and sway. They asked for 0.08 s sheets of the early stages and close crops.
