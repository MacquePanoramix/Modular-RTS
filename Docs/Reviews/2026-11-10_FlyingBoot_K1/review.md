# The flying boot's diagnosis (the design's K1), reviewed by two agents who did not make it (November 10, 2026)

Two Opus reviewers, read-only: a physicist and sceptic asked to refute the diagnosis from the recorded runs and the engine facts; an engineer asked to check the rules drawn from it against the rest of the design and the code. Their findings as returned; what was changed because of them is in `../../Design/AnyBody/motion_design.md` (K1) and `../../Validation.md` (November 10). Their verdicts are not Luis's.

# physics: holds-with-corrections

The core of K1 survives. The energy count is coded correctly, and its drive sign is confirmed by the damped-only runs. Before each event the count balances to a few joules in the Small courses. In several steps the body gains 270 to 1120 J that nothing is counted to give, while the ground gives the boots no push at all (ledger_base k31-k32, ledger_byspeed k33). Even a worst-case bound on the end-of-step torque estimate does not cover those gains. The bare-leg facts are a fair, force-free test, and they do show that the engine's own step adds energy that grows with speed times step. But several of K1's secondary statements are contradicted by the logs. The game's dampers alone do not hold the leg at 60 rad/s; the springs do. The pushed leg with the design's dampers ran away only at 30 rad/s and held at 60 and 120, which goes against the reason given ('a drive can take no more than its strength'). The 0.7(speed x step)^2 law describes the first step only, not a lasting rate per step. In two courses the direct torques supplied most or much of the energy at the onset. One onset (Round shoved six times, other solver with both guards) is not explained by the bare-leg law, and there the count is not balanced beforehand.

## physics 1 [wrong] motion_design.md:654-655 (K1.3, 'Dampers hold it ... the game's own, to 60 rad/s on a leg left alone')
- claim: Dampers hold the gain while they take more each step than the step adds; the game's own hold to 60 rad/s.
- problem: The game's own dampers (1.2/.5/.05, way 1 'damped only' in Whirled, ArticulationFactsTests.cs:301) without springs do not hold at 60 rad/s. facts_all.log: 'damped only' at 60 gives at most 41.43 times, with drives counted -5774.5 J and +13046.8 J gained beyond that. At 120 it is 2.11 times, +8880 J beyond. Under the other solver (facts_whirl_tgs.log) it is 19.76 times at 60. The leg holds to 60 only when it is sprung as well ('sprung and damped as the game's joints', at most 1.00). With the same dampers, adding the springs turns a 41-times runaway into nothing. So what holds it is not shown to be a damper out-taking the step's gain. It may be the springs keeping the joints near zero, in a narrow set of configurations.
- fix: Write: 'Sprung and damped as the game's joints, the leg left alone holds to 60 rad/s. Damped only, with the same dampers, it runs away at 60 (41 times under the game's solver, 20 under the other). Why the spring matters is not known.' R3's premise (a damper that takes more than the step adds) should be marked untested until a damped-only leg holds.

## physics 2 [wrong] motion_design.md:655-656 (K1.3) and ArticulationFactsTests.cs:366-369
- claim: Pushed besides by a torque straight on the thigh, the leg ran away from 30 rad/s even with this design's dampers: a drive can take no more than its strength.
- problem: facts_all.log, way 4 (the design's dampers 4.5/2.6/2.65, pushed with 100 N m): at 30 rad/s at most 9.33 times (+1840.4 J beyond), at 60 rad/s 1.00, at 120 rad/s 1.06. The design's dampers saturate at 130/4.5 = 29, 87/2.6 = 33 and 61/2.65 = 23 rad/s. So they were more saturated at 60 and 120 than at 30, yet held there. Saturation therefore cannot be the explanation. The game's dampers pushed (way 3) are just as non-monotonic: 1.00 at 30, 12.56 at 60. And under the other solver, pushed at only 5 rad/s, the leg reached 2139.79 times (+6307.1 J beyond; facts_whirl_tgs.log), far below any speed at which the (speed x step)^2 law matters.
- fix: Write: 'Pushed with 100 N m on the thigh, the leg with the design's dampers ran away at 30 rad/s (9 times) but held at 60 and 120; under the other solver it ran away from 5 rad/s. Whether a runaway happens depends on more than the speed, and why is not yet known.' Drop 'a drive can take no more than its strength' as the explanation, or test it directly (for example raise forceLimit tenfold and see whether the 30 rad/s runaway goes).

## physics 3 [unsupported] motion_design.md:650-651 (K1.3, 'Each step adds about 0.7·(speed × step)²')
- claim: Each step adds about 0.7·(speed×step)² of what the leg holds: 3% a step at 10 rad/s, a quarter at 30, four fifths at 60; over a second 1.2 times at 10 rad/s.
- problem: The law fits the FIRST step only. Extrapolating the first-step energies to zero step (facts_all.log: 30 rad/s gives 153.2/129.4/123.4 J at dt .02/.01/.005, so E0 is about 121 J; 60 rad/s gives 911.4/589.3/499.7, so E0 is about 470 J) gives an excess of 0.26 and 0.94, that is 0.73 and 0.65 times (speed x step)^2. That part is sound. As a rate per step, though, it contradicts the same log. 3% a step at 10 rad/s over 50 steps would give 4.4 times (or 2.5 without compounding), but the log shows 1.21. A quarter a step at 30 would give about 10^5, against 1886. Growth over a second is also not smooth in the step or the setup: at 20 rad/s halving the step gives 9.42 against 4.13 at the full step, and turning in one plane gives 449.55 against 4.13. And 'four fifths at 60' is not what the stated law gives (0.7 x 1.2^2 = 1.0), nor what was measured (about 0.94).
- fix: Write: 'The first step after the leg is set turning adds about 0.7·(speed×step)² of what it holds (0.26 at 30 rad/s, about 0.9 at 60). Over the following second the gain is uneven and depends on the course: 1.2 times at 10 rad/s, 4 at 20, 1900 at 30.' Correct 'four fifths' to 'about as much again'.

## checked and sound
- Kinetic energy in the count (OwnBodyTests.cs:841-843 and ArticulationFactsTests.cs:282-284) is right for an ArticulationBody. It uses GetPointVelocity at worldCenterOfMass, and the angular velocity rotated into the principal frame by inverse(rotation*inertiaTensorRotation) with the diagonal inertiaTensor. The potential term uses Physics.gravity, and every link has useGravity true (OwnBody.cs:757).
- The work of the direct torques is right in form. The keeper applies +torque[j] to the part and -torque[j] to its parent (OwnKeeper.cs:1151-1152). torque[] is zeroed every step (line 1075) and only legs j>=Thigh get one. So the power is torque·(w_child - w_parent), as counted (OwnBodyTests.cs:849).
- The end-of-step torque estimate does not overturn K1's conclusion. In ledger_base, bounding each leg joint by gave x jw x 0.02 gives 177, 235, 322 and 412 J at k31-k34, against gains of 504, 1081, 903 and 3344 J. Adding a worst case for the drives (|drive| x jw x 0.02: about 117, 222, 250 and 336 J) still leaves 294, 457, 572 and 748 J, all below the gains. With the loosest bound (gave x (|w_part|+|w_parent|) x 0.02 plus drives), k32 (about 642 J against 1081) and k34 (about 1512 against 3344) still hold. k31 (about 470 against 504) is marginal and k33 (about 978 against 903) fails.
- The drive sign is verified. In the damped-only bare leg, pure dampers are counted -0.8 J at 5 rad/s and -8.1 J at 15, with -0.1 and -0.8 J beyond (facts_all.log). Spherical and hinge drives were both in that leg. All drives of every joint i>=1 (arms, trunk and head included) are summed (OwnBodyTests.cs:846-848).
- Nothing acting on the standing body is left out of the count. The only forces are the nudge (m·nudge, counted as byShove; OwnBody.cs:1062), Pull and Push (not used in the test) and the keeper's torques. No velocity or joint writes happen while it stands (OwnBody.cs:1309 runs only at give-back). Self-collisions are ignored (OwnBody.cs:848). No toes, stops or maxJointVelocity are present in these runs (HingesStop, Anatomical and Steps' TurnsAtMost at their defaults). maxAngularVelocity=20 is set but not enforced: 151 rad/s appears in the trace, and the FACTS wheel turned at 39.72 rad/s with the bound at 2.
- The count balances before the event in the Small courses. ledger_base k19-k29: else -5.0 to +2.7 J. ledger_byspeed k30-k31: -0.8 and +4.0 J. ledger_tgs_both Round shoved eight times k5-k17: -9.3 to +10.9 J.
- '22 degrees at one step, 97 at the next': ledger_base k29 tipped 22, k30 97; ledger_byspeed k31 22, k32 97. The boot bore 1.34 and 1.68 body weights during those steps (bore at k30 and k31) while its lowest corner rose from -5 to 67 mm, which fits a boot pushed through on an edge.

- The strongest support for claim 3 is steps with no ground push on the boots. In ledger_base k31 and k32 (bore at the next line 0.00 0.00) the body gained +460 and +1122 J beyond the count. In ledger_byspeed k33 it gained +270 J. No other part is likely to touch the ground then (hips 0.6-1.9 m/s, standing).
- The bare leg is free of forces when left alone: gravity off on the hips and every link, no colliders (Link adds none), drives with stiffness, damping and forceLimit all zero, and no damping (ArticulationFactsTests.cs:247-273, 298-302). The hand-set inertia tensors satisfy the triangle inequality and use identity inertiaTensorRotation. Setting jointVelocity after one frame and measuring after each Simulate is fair. Ratios are taken against the energy after the first step.

# design: holds-with-corrections

K1's diagnosis matches the recorded runs. Until a boot gives way the energy count balances. Then the body gains hundreds to thousands of joules a step that no joint is counted to give (ledger_base.trace.txt: else +89, +460, +1122, +1148, +3635 J). The torque put straight on the boot did 87 to 105 J of work in a single step, five times the ankle spring's whole 19 J stroke. The rules mostly follow from this, and the stroke arithmetic is right. Five parts need correcting before anyone builds from them. (1) R1's claim that a spring "can give no more than its stroke" holds for one write only: K3 re-anchors the load term at the joint's reading every step, so the real bound is the damper (K5's law). (2) The design never says what K1 means at the hand-over (§2.10): the last part of every rise still runs on the standing balance's direct torques. (3) The K5 speed rule cannot be applied as written, because it never says which turning weight counts. Worked by hand with the reading the facts support, the design's dampers already meet it at Small's top speeds. (4) R2 needs more from GroundTouch than change 7 gives, and it conflicts with §2.4's ramp unless "comes from" is read as "is bounded by". (5) Section 7's plain-words paragraph overstates in three places: the four tries are not four guards on the standing balance, the engine's gain is not "step after step" for damped joints, and the new way "cannot begin" is still untested and leaves out the end of the rise.

## design 1 [wrong] motion_design.md §6.2 K1, R1 (lines 667-670); against §2.3 (lines 313-321) and K3 (lines 699-701)
- claim: A spring giving the same torque lets go as the joint moves: it can give no more than its stroke holds (half of strength times eFull: 19 J for Small's ankle, 40 J for its hip), and it does so inside the engine's own solve.
- problem: The arithmetic is right: ½·61·0.6109 = 18.6 J and ½·130·0.6109 = 39.7 J. But the bound holds only for one target write. K3 has the writer put the target at 'the reading now, plus the shortest turn to the goal, plus τ/k' every step, and §2.3 does the same. So the τ/k part is re-armed from wherever the joint has got to, and over several steps it acts as a feed-forward torque of size τ. That is the same thing K1 blames, only solved implicitly within each step. What really bounds the work across steps is K5's law: with c = τcap/ωmax, the drive's torque along the motion falls to nothing at ωmax(1−S). Within one step the bound is about min(½·τ·eFull, τ·ω·dt). R1 as worded would let a builder think the stroke alone protects them. It does not if τ is still worked out from a sole push that is not there, which is why R2 is also needed.
- fix: Reword R1: "Every joint torque is the drive's (K4). Within a step the drive's torque falls as the joint moves (the engine's implicit solve), so one write gives at most half of strength times eFull (19 J for Small's ankle, 40 J for its hip). Because the writer re-anchors τ/k at each step (K3), what bounds it over many steps is the damper of K5: along the motion the torque falls to nothing at ωmax(1 − S). R1 therefore holds only together with K5's damper and with R2 (τ is never worked out for a push that has no touch)."

## design 5 [unclear] §6.2 K5 addition (lines 713-717)
- claim: Step 0 sets how fast each joint may turn in this controller from the count on the bare leg (its damper taking at least twice what the step adds at that speed).
- problem: A builder cannot apply this. 'What the step adds' is measured for a whole leg set turning in one pattern; the damper is per joint; and the rule never says which turning weight counts. Worked by hand: what the step adds is ≈0.7(ωdt)²·½Iω², and the damper takes cω²dt, so 'at least twice' means c ≥ 0.7·I·ω²·dt. Small's top speeds are 29/34/23 rad/s for hip, knee and ankle (the comment at ArticulationFactsTests.cs:299-301; H ≈ 0.45 m, so 23-35 rad/s, not the 20-30 of H 0.6). The design's dampers are 130/29 = 4.48, 87/34 = 2.56 and 61/23 = 2.65 N m s. With each part's own turning weight about its joint (thigh 0.0131 + 2.38·0.13² = 0.053; shin about the knee 0.027; boot about the ankle 0.006 kg m²) the dampers needed are 0.63, 0.43 and 0.046 N m s, so the rule is met about 7×, 6× and 58×. With everything beyond the joint (hip ≈0.67, knee ≈0.14 kg m²) the hip needs 7.9 N m s and fails (its bound would be 21.8 rad/s), and the knee needs 2.2, met barely. The facts favour the first reading. The game's dampers alone (hip 1.2) are predicted to fail near 40 rad/s on that reading, and did: 1.00-1.04 at 30, 19.8 and 41.4 at 60. The design's dampers held at 30 and 60 with nothing pushing. So on the evidence the design's dampers already satisfy the rule at the physiological top speeds, and what failed was the direct torque. 'Getting up needs no joint at a third of that' is given no source.
