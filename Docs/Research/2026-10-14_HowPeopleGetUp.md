## In short: what makes a get-up read as natural

Across all of it, the same few things come back, from the clinic, the
animation desk, the dojo and the lab:

1. **It does not start at once, and it is not dead before it starts.**
   A real body that has fallen first stops and finds out how it is
   (people are taught to; a boxer uses the count to breathe and find
   himself). What moves first is small: the head, the breath, a hand.
2. **At every moment something holds it up.** It goes from one support
   to the next (side, hands and knees, a foot, both feet), and if it were
   frozen at any moment it would stay there. Simulated bodies that do
   this were the ones that looked human.
3. **Its weight goes somewhere you can see before it goes up.** Before
   every lift the body moves its weight over the limb that will push:
   the anticipation of animation, the "momentum" phase of standing up
   from a chair. A rise with no such move reads as pulled up by a string.
4. **It goes by stages a person would name**, and from different starts
   by different ways, often ending in one shared last stand-up.
5. **It is uneven.** One hand leaves the ground before the other, the
   trunk turns a little, one foot comes first. Strict symmetry reads as
   a machine (or as a very slow, careful person).
6. **Its timing is uneven too.** A heavy body is slow to start and then
   goes; it pauses at the low points; it settles at the top. Even spacing
   reads as floaty, a single quick snap as magic.
7. **Effort is seen in the movement itself.** Viewers read how heavy
   something is from how a body moves, with nothing else to go on: how
   fast it lifts, how far it bends at the hips, how long it dwells.
8. **Nothing stops at once.** The head and arms lag and settle; the
   chest moves with breath; the last thing is a small sway.
9. **Neither too strong nor too weak.** A body much stronger than it
   needs gets up in an abrupt, aggressive way that looks wrong; a body
   too weak and floppy reads as slapstick.
10. **It can fail, and try again**, and a tired or hurt body takes
    longer, rests in between, and favours a side.

Section 7 turns these into what our miners are held to.

## 1. How real people get up

### How long it takes

| Who | From | To standing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults 20 to 50 | lying | median 3.7 s | [Schwickert and others, 2016](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/219346) (October 8) |
| Healthy adults over 60 | lying | median 5.7 s | the same |
| Healthy adults over 65, their own way | on their back | 6.4 s on average (4.3 to 12.5) | [Burton and others, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9958992/) |
| Older adults under 70 | on their front | under 4 s | [Díaz Escobar and others, 2013](https://www.atlantis-press.com/proceedings/erse-13/7462) |
| Older adults over 80 | on their front | over 5 s | the same |
| The same people, taught a "conventional" way (with a box) | on their back | 9.4 s | Burton and others, 2023 |

Leg power went closely with the time in Schwickert's study. In Burton's,
the time went with how fast they walked and how fast they stood up from
a chair: the stronger and more able, the quicker.

### The stages, and the ways

Studies of people over fifty describe **three stages: starting,
moving the weight, and coming upright**
([Bohannon and Lusardi, 2004](https://digitalcommons.sacredheart.edu/pthms_fac/43);
Burton, 2023). And three common ways:

- **Onto a side, sitting on one hip, then half-kneeling** (one knee down,
  the other foot flat ahead), and rising over the front foot;
- **Onto hands and knees and pushing up**, then a foot under, or both
  feet in;
- **Sitting up, then rolling forward over the feet** into a squat, and
  rising from it.

In Burton's 3D motion capture of twenty people over 65 rising from their
backs, 12 sat up first (the quickest), 4 sat on a side and 4 rolled over
(the slowest). From there they went on in many ways: rolled over into a
push-up position, onto one knee, onto both knees, a half-kneel, onto all
fours. Six of the twenty had two ways and preferred one. Thirteen led
with the right side and seven with the left.

From the front, 116 independent older people went through 11 different
sequences, mostly of three positions: **the trunk up on all four limbs,
then a "bear walk" (hands and feet, hips up), a squat or kneeling, then
standing** (Díaz Escobar, 2013, from its summary).

### How a young, able body does it

A study of 30 adults of 22 to 40 rising from the floor, scored by body
region ([Pacific University thesis](https://commons.pacificu.edu/works/publication-dissertation/8evd5-4x010),
using VanSant's categories): most often **the legs went through an
uneven squat, one hand left the floor before the other, and the trunk
began straight and then turned a little to one side.** Asked to go
slowly, people moved more evenly; fast, less evenly. Most changed
something when their speed changed.

In VanSant's scheme the most even pattern (both sides together: push
with both hands, sit straight up, squat) is the "most developed", and is
most often seen in older adolescents and young adults; stronger, more
supple people use the more direct ways, in less time (summarised in
[Duncan and others, 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5968950/)
and the October 8 research). **So there is no one natural get-up:**
able bodies go fairly directly and a little unevenly; less able ones go
through more stages, with more support from the hands.

### How deep the body folds

Rising from the floor, older adults' hips bent to 89 to 97 degrees,
their knees to 118 to 129, and their **ankles moved about 30 degrees
each way** (bent up, and pointed) (Burton, 2023). The ankles are part of
getting up: in a squat or a half-kneel the foot is flat and the shin
leans well forward over it; kneeling, the toes are tucked under.

### The last part: up from a seat or a squat

Standing up from a chair is the most studied piece of it
([Schenkman and others, 1990, and others, as summarised](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8183776)):

1. **The trunk bows forward first**, building forward momentum, before
   the seat is left; the knees are already ahead of the ankles.
2. **The seat is left and that momentum goes into the whole body**,
   forward and up. This is **the least steady moment**, when the weight
   is furthest back over the feet.
3. **Hips and knees straighten**, the ankles push.
4. **It steadies** upright.

Earlier work splits it into a forward lean, an upward push by the legs
and a slowing ([Kralj and others, 1990](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2021.678302/pdf), as cited).
The head goes forward and leads (animation tutorials say the same of
this movement, below).

### After a fall

What people are taught to do after a fall (the
[NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/falls/), and the same in other
health guidance), in my words:

1. **Stay still for a moment** and find out whether anything hurts.
2. **Do not get up quickly.**
3. **Roll onto a side, and push up with the arms onto hands and knees.**
4. Crawl to something steady.
5. **Put one foot flat on the floor, and rise slowly** holding on.
6. Sit and rest before going on.

Only about half of people who fall without being hurt can get up again
by themselves
([NIHR](https://evidence.nihr.ac.uk/?p=45395), from its summary). The
lying itself (how long a fallen body lies before it begins) is in the
research of October 9: a median of 10.5 s for older people who did get
up, and seconds for a fit body that is winded.

### Children

Small children first get up from the floor **through a "bear stance"
(hands and feet on the ground, bottom up) or a squat**, and only later,
as their balance and strength grow, **through a half-kneel**
([Starfish Therapies](https://starfishtherapies.com/2020/07/floor-to-stand));
they stand up from the middle of the floor with nothing to hold at about
two to two and a half
([milestones](https://nesc.k12.sd.us/resources/Gross%20Motor%20Developmental%20Milestones.pdf)).
Therapists set things for them lower than their shoulders so that they
push up rather than pull up. The steps they are helped through: high
kneeling, one foot brought forward, then up
([NHS Grampian leaflet](https://www.rach.scot.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/On-the-Move-2-from-baby-to-toddler.pdf)).

**For our miners this matters more than anything about adults:** Small
and Round are built more like small children (large heads, short limbs)
than like grown people, and a body built so gets up as a toddler does:
hands and feet down, bottom up, then the hands push off.

### Martial arts, the boxing ring, dance

- **The "technical stand-up" of Brazilian jiu-jitsu** gets up from
  sitting with the head protected: one hand posted on the ground behind,
  the opposite foot flat, **the hips lifted off the ground** on that hand
  and foot, the other leg **swung back under in a wide arc**, landing in
  a wide, steady stance, facing what it is getting up for. Its common
  faults are scooting along the ground instead of lifting, and stopping
  half way ([GrappleArts](https://www.grapplearts.com/the-four-variations-of-the-bjj-technical-standup/),
  [Breaking Muscle](https://breakingmuscle.com/brazilian-jiu-jitsu-how-why-to-do-a-technical-stand-up/)).
  A get-up with an intent: it faces the danger, and it is done in one
  flowing movement.
- **The boxing ring:** a fighter who is knocked down may stay down on
  one knee for the count; the count is used **to get his breath and find
  himself** before rising; rising and standing still like a target makes
  the referee stop the fight
  ([FightCamp](https://blog.joinfightcamp.com/training/how-to-recover-from-a-knockdown-in-a-boxing-match/)).
  Down on one knee, waiting, breathing, is a real pose of a body that has
  been hit.
- **Dance:** contemporary "floorwork" goes into and out of the floor by
  spirals of the spine, using the weight going down to come out of the
  floor with momentum, and bearing weight on parts of the body other
  than the feet ([Rambert](https://rambert.org.uk/rambertplus/move/beginners-contemporary-transfer-of-weight-through-floorwork-with-simone/),
  [Bennington](https://www.bennington.edu/node/86636)). A trained body
  gets up as one flowing line, turning as it comes up; it reads as skill,
  not as effort.

## 2. How viewers see a moving body

- **Weight is seen in movement alone.** Shown only lights on a person's
  joints lifting a box, viewers can tell how heavy the box is: the
  movement itself carries it
  ([Runeson and Frykholm's "kinematic specification of dynamics"](https://www.psy.gla.ac.uk/~frank/Talk_folder/Humanoids/tsld013.htm);
  [Bingham, 1987](https://palab.sitehost.iu.edu/Resources/Publications/1980-1989/Bingham1987a.pdf)).
  What changes with the weight is how fast it lifts, how far it bends at
  the hips and how long it lingers; how fast it lifts weighed most with
  the viewers (Shim and Carlton, 1997, as summarised). **So a heavy
  miner and a light one must not get up at the same pace**, and a tired
  one must not either.
- **Natural movement slows in its curves.** A moving point is seen as
  going evenly when its speed falls as its path bends, as a hand's does
  (the "two-thirds power law"); one going round a curve at the same
  speed is seen as uneven, and natural movement is harder to pick out
  than unnatural ([Viviani and Stucchi](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/the-two-thirds-power-law-in-motion-perception/);
  [EPFL](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/220762?ln=fr)).
- **Viewers notice motion that breaks physics**, in a body in flight
  especially when the speed along the ground is wrong, or speeds up
  without cause; they notice it more on a figure that looks human, and
  seeing the body get ready before it moves helps
  ([Reitsma and Pollard, 2003](https://ri.cmu.edu/?p=8709);
  [Reitsma, Andrews and Pollard, 2008](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v27i2pp201-210)).
- **Faces are watched hardest**: odd faces were always disliked, while
  some changes to a body's movement were let pass
  ([Hodgins and others, 2010](https://fis.uni-bamberg.de/handle/uniba/106364), from a summary).
  For a miner seen from a distance, its body is what is read.

## 3. How animators show it (animation and anime)

### The principles

The twelve principles of the Disney animators
([Thomas and Johnston, *The Illusion of Life*, 1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_basic_principles_of_animation))
were written to make drawn figures seem to obey physics. Five of them
are the whole of a good get-up:

- **Anticipation:** a movement is got ready for (a knee bends before a
  jump). Before the body goes up, it goes down, back, or over the limb
  that will push.
- **Follow-through and overlapping action:** nothing stops at once;
  the parts of a body move on their own timings, the loose ones going on
  and coming back.
- **Slow in and slow out:** a body takes time to start and to stop.
- **Arcs:** limbs, head and body move on curves.
- **Timing**, and **secondary action** (a breath, a hand brushing off).

### Weight

Richard Williams (*The Animator's Survival Kit*) makes the point that
weight shows when the animator knows **where the weight is in every
drawing, where it came from and where it goes**; and that a figure is
made supple, and kept solid, by **its joints breaking one after another**
rather than all together ([the publisher's account of the chapter](https://www.faber.co.uk/product/9780571358434-the-animators-survival-kit-flexibility-and-weight/)).

Why animation looks floaty, by animators' own accounts
([several](https://blog.prolificstudio.co/blog/slow-in-and-slow-out-in-animation/),
[guides](https://www.udit.es/en/timing-y-spacing-en-animacion-que-es-y-como-mejora-la-sensacion-de-peso/)):
spacing too even (the same distance in each frame); starts and stops
that do not show the mass (**a heavy thing is reluctant to start, then
opens up**); a movement that just stops instead of settling; no pause.
A hold before an action gives it weight, as spacing does.

### Standing up, and getting up

- From a seat: the weight has to travel, from the seat, down through the
  feet into the ground; a plain in-between from sitting to standing
  looks false because the whole body has to take part; **it leans back
  or down first** (anticipation) and then forward and up
  ([a course on seated to standing](https://www.lynda.com/Animation-tutorials/Animating-character-from-seated-standing-Creating-main-poses/134617/159437-4.html)).
  The head leads, the rest of the body following like a pendulum (an
  animation breakdown found with it).
- Tired or hurt: **decide what is wrong first** (spent from a fight, a
  bad knee, dazed), because that changes everything; a tired body is not
  only slow, it keeps trying to push through; longer holds low down and
  at the top; the head and shoulders lag behind the hips; a hurt body
  favours a side and does not push evenly; the breath shows; a pause on
  one knee between lying and standing
  ([Animation Mentor on tiredness](https://www.animationmentor.com/?p=85006);
  [research on fatigue in simulated motion, Penn](https://upenn-test.atmire.com/signposting/describedby/18ccff88-855f-40fe-a5fd-20d0e060d8c3); others).
- The key poses animators block a floor get-up in †: lying with the
  weight on a hip or the hands; the weight moved towards the hand or the
  side that will push; the weight going over the knees or a foot with the
  hands still taking some; the hips up and the trunk forward over the
  feet; standing; a small settle with the head and arms a little behind.

### Anime

- **Timing, not only drawing, makes weight.** The writer on *Ghost in
  the Shell*'s opening says the heroine's slow, heavy rise from her bed
  gets its weight from **uneven gaps between drawings**: uneven timing
  reads as heavy and clumsy, even timing as purposeful
  ([Full Frontal](https://fullfrontal.moe/sakuga-espresso-1-ghost-in-the-shell/)).
- **Mitsuo Iso's "full limited" animation** (key drawings only, no
  in-betweens) puts the flow of movement and its weight first, which is
  why it is called ultra-realistic; small movements made a little larger
  than life (a shoulder rising with a breath) can read as more real than
  filmed life ([Full Frontal](https://fullfrontal.moe/?p=1057);
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsuo_Iso)).
- **Yutaka Nakamura**, the action animator, says realism in movement is
  a deception of the eye: it starts from the real, cannot be the real,
  and is real enough; and that the eye wanders, so motion it does not
  follow helps the illusion
  ([interview, 2003](https://wavemotioncannon.com/2016/10/05/interview-yutaka-nakamura-web-anime-style-7192003-part-1/)).
  Weight true to size is what he is praised for on *Escaflowne*'s
  machines.
- **Stillness is acting too:** in a scene of *Dennou Coil*, one
  character barely moves, and that reads as what she is going through
  ([Full Frontal](https://fullfrontal.moe/sakuga-expresso-dennou-coil/)).
- **Ghibli:** its realism is said to begin with movement (the laboured
  movement of great heavy creatures), on figures with human proportions
  ([Rarebit](https://rarebit.org/?p=6605)). Not found: a breakdown of a
  Ghibli character getting up.

## 4. How games do it

- **The usual way** (forums and tools for Unreal, Unity, Godot, Golaem):
  the body falls as a rag doll and is left to settle; **when it has
  settled, a recorded get-up is chosen by whether it lies face up or face
  down**; the character's place is moved to the hips so that it does not
  jump; and the rag doll's pose is blended into the clip's first frame
  ([Golaem](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/GOLAEM-Maya/files/tutorials/physics-simulation/glm_ragdoll-recovering-animation.html),
  [Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/smooth-transition-between-ragdoll-and-animation/372228),
  [a Godot guide](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-godot-blend-from-ragdoll-back-to-animation-snapping)).
  Its known faults are the ones Luis saw in ours: the body is dead until
  a clock says it may get up, and the join between lying and the clip
  slides or turns the body with nothing pushing it.
- **Euphoria** (NaturalMotion; *GTA IV*, *Red Dead Redemption 2*)
  simulates the body and something of its nervous system, so that it
  answers differently each time; Rockstar says it was taken further for
  *Red Dead Redemption 2*, for people and animals
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria_(software)),
  [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/rockstar-euphoria-evolved-rdr2/amp/)).
  How its bodies get up is not published (not found).
- **Naughty Dog** added a layer of physics on top of animation in
  *Uncharted 4* for momentum, and found it hard until the physics could
  be driven by the animation predictably
  ([GDC 2017](https://www.gdconf.com/news/see-naughty-dog-deconstruct-uncharted-4s-physics-animation-gdc-2017)).
- **Physics bodies played for laughs.** *Gang Beasts* and *Human: Fall
  Flat* are funny because their bodies are floppy and under-powered and
  what they mean to do comes apart from what they do
  ([Nintendo Life and reviews](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/best-nintendo-switch-wacky-physics-games)).
  **That is the line our miners must stay on the right side of:** a body
  of its own that is too weak, too loose or too slow to commit reads as
  slapstick, not as a body getting up.

## 5. Bodies that get up by their own forces (research)

- **Composable controllers** ([Faloutsos, van de Panne and Terzopoulos, 2001](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2001-siggraph-composable.pdf)):
  a simulated "stuntman" that steps to keep its feet, puts out its arms
  as it falls, and **rises in several ways**, each controller knowing the
  states it can begin from. (The rising controllers' details could not be
  read today.)
- **Rising controllers from simple parts** ([Jones, 2011, thesis](https://open.library.ubc.ca/collections/24/items/1.0052110),
  from its abstract): sit-to-stand, a four-legged animal getting up, and a
  person from their front to standing, all made of the same few parts:
  **following a pose, keeping the body's turn right, and forces asked of
  the limbs as if pushing the body** (what our keeper does standing).
- **Learning to Get Up** ([Tao, Wilson, Gou and van de Panne, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00307);
  read through [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00307)):
  found first with a body much stronger than it should be, then made
  weaker step by step, then slowed. **Strong bodies got up abruptly and
  aggressively, with quick stepping and feet split apart, which looked
  unnatural; at 40 to 60% of the first strength it looked most
  natural.** The slow get-ups were steady enough to be stopped at many
  points and stay. Most ended in one shared last stand-up whatever they
  started from, and **most went over onto their front before standing**
  (one that preferred its back first rolled over when started on its
  front). With an arm and a leg in a cast, the free limbs gave momentum
  and the cast ones balance. They cite a slow, low-effort way older
  people get up (Adams and Tyson, 2000) and online videos of 52 ways of
  getting up.
- **Get-up styles from a few curves** ([Frezzato, Tangri and Andrews, 2022](https://diglib.eg.org:443/handle/10.1111/cgf14636),
  from its summary): a simulated body from its back or front, its style
  set by curves of how it rises (from a motion capture library), working
  on rough and sloping ground.
- **Rising by a plan of where the weight, hands and feet go**
  ([Kenwright, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05056); read through
  [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.05056)): turn onto the
  front or a side and put hands and feet on either side of the body;
  **lift one hand, and move the weight towards the feet over the two feet
  and the other hand; if it is still outside them, put the free hand down
  nearer, and lift the other** (the hands walk back towards the feet);
  once the weight is over the feet, let go with the hands and rise,
  keeping it there. What it holds a rise to: balance throughout, joints
  within their limits, hands and feet not slipping, no contorted or
  overstretched poses; a sway or a look around adds character or
  tiredness. Only slow rises, front or back, level ground.

## 7. What we hold ours to

The research of October 8 set four rules for getting up. They stand,
and these are added (each with where it comes from):

| | Held to | From |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Lying, it is not dead.** It stays down a moment, more for a harder fall; its breath shows, harder after a fall; the first things to move are small (the head, a hand); its strength comes back by degrees and it begins as it can, not when a clock runs out | Luis; the advice after a fall; the boxer's count; animators on tired bodies |
| G2 | **Frozen at any moment, it would stay.** Every stage is held up by what touches the ground | October 8; Tao; Kenwright |
| G3 | **Its weight goes over a limb before that limb lifts it**, and the move is seen: onto the hands before the knees come up, over the front foot before it rises, the trunk forward before the seat leaves the ground | The phases of standing up; anticipation; Williams |
| G4 | **By stages a person would name**: onto its front or a side; hands under its shoulders; pushed up onto hands and knees; one foot forward or both feet in; up over the feet; a settle. From its back it rolls over first. Each miner its own way within that, as its body allows | Bohannon; Burton; Díaz Escobar; the NHS; Tao |
| G5 | **Uneven:** one side leads; one hand leaves the ground before the other; the trunk turns a little | VanSant's young adults; Burton |
| G6 | **About three to six seconds** from its first real move to standing, for a fresh miner; longer tired, or after a harder fall. Uneven timing: slow to start for a heavy body, pauses low down, a settle at the top. No glide at an even pace, no snap | Schwickert; Burton; animators on floatiness; Ghost in the Shell |
| G7 | **Each miner's weight shows in its pace:** Round, the heaviest, reluctant to start and slower to rise; Small quicker; Long, tall, longer in its movements. Not one get-up played on three bodies | Weight seen in movement; the judges of October 9 ("one movement played on two bodies") |
| G8 | **Nothing stops at once:** the head and arms lag and settle; it breathes after; a small sway as it comes to stand | Follow-through; Kenwright; Penn |
| G9 | **Strong enough, and no stronger:** decisive pushes at a person's pace; never abrupt, never floppy | Tao (40 to 60%); the comedy of floppy bodies |
| G10 | **The head leads** the last rise, and the eyes go where it is going | Standing up from a chair; animators |
| G11 | **It faces where its body ended up facing** when it comes up, and turns to its work afterwards, by stepping | The technical stand-up faces what it rises for; what Luis called "magical" in ours was the posed body turning it a quarter round |
| G12 | **It may fail and try again**; a tired or hurt one rests between stages (on its knees, on one knee) | October 8; the boxer on one knee; the NHS |

**What this means for the three miners, with their arms now as strong as
their bodies need** (Luis, October 14):

- **From the front, the plainest human way is open**: hands under the
  shoulders, pushed up onto hands and knees. From there, **Small and
  Round, built more like small children, are likely to come up as a
  toddler does: hands and feet down, bottom up, then a push off the hands
  into a squat** and up from it; **Long, built more like a grown person,
  through a half-kneel, one foot forward and up over it**. That is a
  starting point for each one's search, not a rule: what each finds will
  be judged against G1 to G12.
- **From the back, it rolls onto its front** (or a side) first, a knee
  coming up and across and an arm reaching over, as most people and most
  learned bodies do.
- **These twelve go to the judges** with the pictures, in place of what
  they were given before, and they are what I check before Luis sees it.

## 8. Rising from one knee (added October 14, late)

Asked for before building it (Luis: "You can do more research loops
whenever needed"). The way chosen next is the common adult one: from hands
and knees, one foot forward, then up over that foot.

- **No study of half-kneeling to standing itself was found**; the nearest is
  standing up from a seat, read across to it.
- **The phases** (from sit-to-stand): the weight shifts as the trunk bows
  forward; the knee straightens as the bow ends; the body is lifted and the
  trunk straightens to standing
  ([a review, figure 29](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.01487)). Loaded, people
  bow further and bend their ankles more, and the knee works hardest
  ([children with packs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18187221/)).
- **What each joint does**: hips, knees and ankles all push the body up; the
  knee slows its forward travel while hips and ankles drive it forward
  ([Marquette, 2025](https://epublications.marquette.edu/exsci_fac/253/)).
  People with weak knees bring their weight further forward, so the knee
  has less to do ([knee osteoarthritis, the centre of mass's path](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/the-centre-of-mass-trajectory-is-a-sensitive-and-responsive-measu/)).
- **How it is taught** (exercise instructions, not studies): weight onto the
  front leg, then push up; the front foot far enough ahead that its shin is
  near upright (tucked too close, the knee goes far past the toes and loses
  its strength); the trunk leans a little over the front leg; push through
  the whole foot
  ([Physitrack](https://ca.physitrack.com/home-exercise-video/half-kneeling-to-standing-with-dorsiflexion),
  [Fitwill](https://fitwill.app/exercise/10750/kneeling-to-stand/)).
- **A hand on the front thigh** (my reading, no source): a lever that helps
  the trunk over the front foot and takes work off the front knee; tired or
  weak bodies do it.

**What it adds to ours:** the front foot goes well forward (shin near
upright) before the rise; the weight goes over it, the trunk bowing, before
the back knee leaves the ground (G3); the front leg lifts, the back foot
pushes and then comes up beside it; a weak or tired miner pushes on its
front thigh with a hand.

## 9. Moving the weight off a knee (added October 15)

Asked when the miners, on hands and knees, would not move their weight off
the knee they were about to bring forward.

- **No study of hands and knees itself was found.** The nearest are bodies
  about to lift a foot or reach with an arm.
- **Before a limb lifts, the weight goes onto the limbs that stay**, and it is
  seen: adults reaching from a crouch first move their weight onto the leg
  that stays, more so the less steady they are
  ([crouched reaching](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3688725));
  toddlers about to step tilt their pelvis over the standing leg so the other
  is unloaded just before it swings
  ([advances in the study of anticipatory adjustments](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11726838/)).
- **The limb about to lift pushes first.** Starting to walk, the pressure
  under the feet goes back and towards the leg that will swing, and that push
  sends the body forward and over the leg that stays
  ([Parkinson's and gait initiation, 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2019.00352/full);
  [obesity and gait initiation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24885764/)).
- **It is quick:** the muscles begin about half a second before the step in
  children, and the body moves about 0.3 s after they begin
  ([a review of children's adjustments](https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/anticipatory-postural-adjustments-childrena-systematic-review)).
  A fresh body takes well under a second over it, not two.
- **How the move from hands and knees to one knee is taught** (exercise
  instructions, not studies): arms kept straight, the trunk rises as the knee
  comes forward, the low back kept steady
  ([Functional Movement](https://www.functionalmovement.com/exercises/918/quadruped_to_half-kneeling_transitions);
  [the Prehab Guys](https://library.theprehabguys.com/vimeo-video/quadruped-to-half-kneeling/)).

**What it adds to ours:** the weight goes over the hands and the other knee
before the knee lifts (G3), forward and to the side of the knee that stays;
the knee about to lift may push to start that move rather than go slack at
once; for a fresh miner it takes about half a second, longer tired.
