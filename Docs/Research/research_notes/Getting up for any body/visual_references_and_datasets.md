# Visual references and motion datasets for getting up from the floor (Wonder Gather)

Compiled 2026-10-30. **Evidence boundary, read first.** Every YouTube entry below was checked through its public watch-page data fetched on 2026-10-30: exact title, channel, length, upload date, description and the uploader's chapter markers. **None was watched frame by frame by the researcher.** So start posture, body type, speed, camera angle and cuts are stated only where the title, description or chapters say so; otherwise the entry says *verify on viewing*. Links carrying `&t=` point at an uploader chapter marker: the time is exact as a chapter start, but what is on screen there was not seen. All other timestamps are marked *not identified*. Fit ratings (High / Medium / Low / Anti) are the researcher's judgement **from the description only** and must be re-judged on viewing. **Exception:** seven entries (P1, P22, P27, P28, P29, P32, P33) were also checked against YouTube's public storyboard preview thumbnails (the hover-scrub tiles, one every 1–5 s; no video was downloaded). Timestamps marked *storyboard ≈* are accurate to about one tile. For frame stepping on desktop YouTube, pause, then use `,` and `.` to step one frame back or forward (a standard YouTube shortcut; Endless Reference's video descriptions also carry a frame-by-frame tip, see A6). Direct `.mp4` links (S1, S5) open in the browser's own player.

ID prefixes: **P** real people, **S** science/research, **G** games, **X** anti-references, **A** animation/film, **D** datasets, **T** video-to-motion tools.

## 1. Real people of varied builds and capacities getting up from the floor

### Takeaway
Stable, frame-studiable footage exists for each body class the game needs: weak/older adults (many physiotherapist demonstrations, several with chapter markers), toddlers (paediatric-therapy videos naming the three floor-to-stand routes babies use), clinically weak hips (Gowers' manoeuvre), larger and pregnant bodies, a 6 ft 9 in heavyweight rising from flat on his back (Fury v Wilder I, round 12), people in plate armour, and newborn animals rising for the first time. Dedicated "very tall person" and "firefighter in full kit" get-up footage was not found.

### Inferences
- The sources together show **five distinct human floor-rise routes**:
  - (a) roll to the side → all fours → half-kneel → stand, often with a chair (P1–P8);
  - (b) bear crawl / squat to stand, the toddler routes (P17);
  - (c) Gowers' climb, hands walking up the thighs, for weak hip extensors (P16);
  - (d) technical stand-up: hand and foot base planted, the other leg swept back (P24);
  - (e) spiral or crossed-leg rise without hands (P11, P12).

  These map onto the three miners as starting hypotheses for the Game Director, not decisions:
  - Small / toddler-like (heavy head): (b);
  - Long: (a) or (d);
  - Round: (a) with more side-rolling;
  - weak settings: (c).
- For "like ragdolls without being goofy," the best real models combine **effort and dignity**: Fury (P22), the foal and giraffe (P32–P33), the armoured rises (P27–P28) and the Feldenkrais spiral (P11). Each has struggle or weight without comic overshoot.
- Most physio videos are talk-heavy. Their value is in the demonstration segments, and only Bob & Brad (P1) gives chapter-precise entry points.
- The storyboards show the same ordering across very different bodies:
  - Fury (P22) first sits up, then turns onto a knee, then stands; the low replay shows him rolling toward his front before pushing up.
  - The foal (P32) goes chest-down with head up, then front legs, then a hind-end push, with collapses between tries.
  - The armoured rises (P27) come from lying and from prone.

  Common thread: get the head and trunk up and over a planted base before the legs take the weight, and fail and retry rather than succeed in one smooth motion (inference from ≈2 s tiles; check at full frame rate).

## 2. Research videos: simulated and robotic get-ups

### Takeaway
The most directly relevant research reference is **Tao et al. 2022, "Learning to Get Up."** It learns get-ups from scratch for a strong character, then adapts them to weaker characters and slower speeds, with policies that can be paused mid-rise. Its video is a stable `.mp4` on the project page. HumanUP and HoST (2025, Unitree G1) show real-robot rises from many postures and surfaces. Hämäläinen 2014/2015, Mordatch 2012, PHC, ASE and DReCon have official videos; for several, the get-up segment was not confirmed from metadata.

### Cited Findings
- **S1 — Tao, Wilson, Gou, van de Panne, "Learning to Get Up" (SIGGRAPH 2022).** Three-stage deep RL without motion capture: discover get-ups with a strong character, adapt to weaker characters, then produce slower versions. Policies tend to be statically stable and can be paused at many points mid-rise. Video: a direct `.mp4` (HTTP 200, video/mp4, about 24.9 MB, last modified 2022-08-27). **Fit: Very high** (any-body strength range; slowness). — [Project page](https://tianxintao.github.io/get_up_control/); [video .mp4](https://tianxintao.github.io/get_up_control/figs/video.mp4); [arXiv 2205.00307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00307)
- **S2 — HumanUP, He, Dong, Chen, Gupta, "Learning Getting-Up Policies for Real-World Humanoid Robots" (RSS 2025; Unitree G1).** Clips on the page:
  - supine get-up and prone roll-over on concrete, brick, stone tile, muddy grass and a ~10° grass slope (plus snow for rolling);
  - **Stage I (faster, less safe) vs Stage II (slower, safer)**;
  - motor-temperature comparison with the factory controller;
  - failure cases.

  YouTube 1:55, 2025-02-16. **Fit: High** as a speed-vs-care contrast. — [Project page](https://humanoid-getup.github.io/); [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NPMUlf9soL0); [arXiv 2502.12152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12152v1)
- **S3 — HoST, Huang et al., "Learning Humanoid Standing-up Control across Diverse Postures" (RSS 2025, Best Systems Paper finalist).** Rises:

YouTube 1:53, 2025-02-12, ~104k views. **Fit: High** (rising against furniture and terrain). — [Project page](https://taohuang13.github.io/humanoid-standingup.github.io/); [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yruh-3CFwE4); [code](https://github.com/OpenRobotLab/HoST); [arXiv 2502.08378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08378v1)

### Inferences
- **The clean commercial path to human get-up mocap for Wonder Gather is CMU subjects 140, 139, 77, 111, 113 and 114.** They cover face-down, side and back starts, a careful-ready variant, and **pregnant bodies** (mass moved forward, a natural "different body" sample). Use them as imitation references or training priors, never as a shipped canned get-up (the project rule "never the backup animation").
- LaFAN1's "Fall and get up" set (6 sequences) is excellent but **non-commercial and no-derivatives**: viewing only, no retargeting or training for the game. Watching for reference is not something these licences address; copying, adapting or training is what they restrict (inference, not legal advice).
- AMASS's explicit ban on training networks for commercial use rules out AMASS-derived data (HumanML3D, BABEL, Motion-X) for the ML-Agents get-up trainer.

## 6. Video-to-3D-motion tools and their licences

### Takeaway
Among open-source tools, only **MediaPipe (Apache-2.0)** is clearly commercial-friendly. **FreeMoCap** is **AGPL-3.0** (copyleft). **WHAM** and **4D-Humans** have MIT code but need the **SMPL** body model, which is non-commercial unless licensed through Meshcapade. **GVHMR** and **OpenPose** are non-commercial. Terms for commercial services (Rokoko Vision, DeepMotion, Move, QuickMagic) were not retrieved.

### Inferences
- If the team wants to turn the inventory's real footage into motion for study or reward shaping, MediaPipe (T1) is licence-safe for 3D landmarks, though it gives keypoints rather than a rigged character. SMPL-based tools (T4–T6) give better full-body motion but carry SMPL's non-commercial default.
- **Separately from software licences, the footage itself is copyrighted.** Extracting motion from someone's YouTube video for a commercial game raises rights questions no software licence answers. Footage the team shoots itself (as Endless Reference does) avoids this (inference, not legal advice).
