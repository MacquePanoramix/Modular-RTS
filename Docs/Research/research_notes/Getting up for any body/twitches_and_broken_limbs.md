# Twitchy, snapping, "broken-limb" and one-frame-glitch motion in physically simulated characters (Unity 6 / PhysX ConfigurableJoints / learned PD targets)

Symptom key used throughout: **S1** = very aggressive limb twitches, worse in the "more dynamic" moments; **S2** = arms bent so they look broken; **S3** = a limb flicking "completely out of frame" for about one frame ("clipping and bugging out"). Configuration figures used in the arithmetic come from the assignment: fixed step 0.02 s (50 Hz), decision every 5 steps (10 Hz), outputs eased 20 % per decision, offsets up to ±35° from key poses, Slerp drives capped at joint strength, projection PositionAndRotation 0.02 m / 5°, interpolation on, solverIterations 14, maxDepenetrationVelocity 1.5 m/s, maxAngularVelocity 20 rad/s, rendering uncapped. Research date 2026-10-30. "Unverified" marks a claim I could not confirm from a primary source in this session. Everything under "Inferences" is my reasoning from the cited material, not a measured fact about Wonder Gather.

## 1. RL policy smoothness: causes of bang-bang and high-frequency actions, and cures with measured effects and costs

### Takeaway
Unregularized deep-RL policies routinely learn near-bang-bang, high-frequency actions, because simulated actuators deliver any torque instantly and nothing in the objective charges for changing the action. The best-measured cures act on the objective (CAPS temporal/spatial terms, LCP's gradient penalty, action-rate rewards) or the action space (PD targets), and they cut measured jitter by roughly an order of magnitude for a few to ~10 % of task return; filters bolted on after training behave inconsistently. For this project the likeliest policy-side contributors to S1 are: saturated means under deterministic inference (ML-Agents clamps to ±3 and divides by 3, so a mean beyond ±3 is a pinned ±35° offset), the 10 Hz staircase of target steps (up to 14° per decision) into strong drives, and any mismatch between the easing used in training and at inference.

### Cited Findings
**Causes**
- LCP's authors attribute the jitter of simulation-trained policies to "the simplified dynamics and actuation models used in simulation", where motors can produce the desired torque in any state; this leads to bang-bang-like behaviour, large action differences between timesteps, and torques real actuators cannot produce — [Chen et al. 2024, Learning Smooth Humanoid Locomotion through Lipschitz-Constrained Policies](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11825v2)
- CAPS (Mysore, Mabsout, Mancuso, Saenko, ICRA 2021) names: aggressive near-binary, step-like policies that reach goals as fast as possible (causing oscillation); oscillation learned early acting as a local minimum agents fail to escape; overfitting to complicated rewards in simulated dynamics; and that running networks "at rates much faster than the system dynamics" seems to make them more prone to non-smooth behaviour. They hypothesize, without proof, that PPO and SAC are smoother on their own because of stochasticity — [CAPS paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.06644)
- Seyde et al. (NeurIPS 2021) replace the Gaussian policy with a Bernoulli distribution that only uses the extremes of each action dimension (pure bang-bang) and reach state-of-the-art results on several standard continuous-control benchmarks, i.e. benchmark rewards do not discourage bang-bang control; they note that energy and maintenance costs make bang-bang unattractive on hardware — [Seyde et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02552); [NeurIPS 2021 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2021/poster/26842)
- Unstructured per-step Gaussian exploration produces shaky motion; "Consequences of the resulting shaky behavior are poor exploration, or even damage to the robot" — [Raffin, Kober, Stulp, gSDE, CoRL 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05719)
- A torque-action dog policy showed "unnatural jittery behavior" — [Peng & van de Panne 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.01055)

**ML-Agents specifics that matter for deterministic inference**
- ML-Agents' ActionModel clips continuous outputs with `torch.clamp(continuous_out, -3, 3) / 3` and applies the same to `deterministic_continuous_out`; clipping is disabled when `tanh_squash` is on; the source comment says training-time clipping is done in TorchPolicy and must be repeated before ONNX export — [ml-agents action_model.py (develop)](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Unity-Technologies/ml-agents/develop/ml-agents/mlagents/trainers/torch_entities/action_model.py)
- With `_deterministic` set the action is `dists.continuous.deterministic_sample()` instead of `sample()` — same source. `BehaviorParameters.DeterministicInference` is documented as choosing actions deterministically from the network — [ML-Agents 3.0 BehaviorParameters API](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.ml-agents@3.0/api/Unity.MLAgents.Policies.BehaviorParameters.html). That `deterministic_sample()` returns the Gaussian mean is the usual convention but I did not read ML-Agents' distributions module (unverified).

**Cures and their measured effects**
- CAPS objective: J − λ_T·L_T − λ_S·L_S, with L_T = ‖π(s_t) − π(s_{t+1})‖₂ (temporal) and L_S = ‖π(s) − π(s̄)‖₂, s̄ ~ N(s, σ) (spatial). Smoothness metric Sm = (2/(n·f_s))·Σ M_i·f_i from the FFT of the control signal (amplitude-weighted mean normalized frequency; higher = more high-frequency content) — [CAPS](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.06644)
- CAPS on Gym (Sm×10³; reward): Ant-v2 PPO 6.09 → 1.60, reward 3734.58 → 4256.93; Pendulum DDPG 47.6 → 7.09, reward −145.56 → −188.16 (a real cost); LunarLander PPO 11.4 → 10.0, reward 169.08 → 140.71. Authors: smoother on all tasks, "nominal performance hit" on pendulum and lunar lander — [CAPS](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.06644)
- CAPS on a real quadrotor against the previous RL controller (Neuroflight): Sm 4.3 → 0.16 (≈96 % smoother), motor current 22.87 A → 4.86 A (≈80 % less power), but mean tracking error 5.19 → 9.28 deg/s — [CAPS](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.06644); [project page](https://ai.bu.edu/caps/index.html)
- CAPS on filters: median and EMA filters caused minor overshoot and oscillation in their toy problem; an FIR filter caused total loss of control; naively adding filters during training without state history caused catastrophic failure to learn (breaks the Markov assumption); adding history restored it at the price of much larger inputs — [CAPS](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.06644)
- LCP adds λ_gp·E[‖∇_s log π(a|s)‖²] to PPO (λ_gp = 0.002). Humanoid results (Action Jitter / Task Return): no smoothing 42.19 / 28.87; low-pass filter 7.86 / 24.98; smoothness reward 5.74 / 26.56; LCP 3.21 / 26.03. Energy 42.68 (none) vs 24.57 (LCP). Coefficient sweep: λ 0.005 → jitter 2.10, return 23.92; λ 0.01 → jitter 0.17, return 16.11. Applying the penalty to the whole observation including history was best (3.21 vs 7.16 for current observation only) — [LCP](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11825v2)
- LCP on alternatives: low-pass filters "often dampen or limit exploration", need per-robot effort and are not easily differentiable; smoothness rewards "require careful tuning of weights to balance smooth behavior with task completion" — [LCP](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11825v2)
- A March 2026 follow-up (ALCP, He & Nakamura) uses direction-dependent (anisotropic) Lipschitz bounds and reports better stability and impact robustness with fewer oscillations and lower energy (abstract-level; full paper not read) — [arXiv 2603.16180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16180)
- gSDE: state-dependent exploration with noise resampled every n steps; the sampling interval trades performance against smoothness and allowed training directly on real robots "without loss of performance"; implemented in Stable-Baselines3 — [gSDE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05719); [PMLR version](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v164/raffin22a.html)

**Action space and decision rate**
- Torques vs muscle activations (MTU) vs PD target angles vs target velocities: PD targets were best or near-best, e.g. normalized cumulative reward Biped Walk 0.9524 (PD) vs 0.7662 (torque), Raptor Run 0.9548 vs 0.6141; "PD also learns faster than the other parameterizations for 5 of the 7 motions"; "the PD policies are among the most robust" — [Peng & van de Panne, SCA 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.01055)
- Same paper, query rates 15/30/60/120 Hz: PD and velocity targets "cope more effectively to lower query rates"; torque "degrades more rapidly at lower query rates"; MTU does better at lower and worse at higher rates; "the MTU action is visibly smoother than the other actions", and MTU policies "produce more natural motions" once actuator parameters are optimized — [Peng & van de Panne 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.01055)

### Inferences
- Deterministic inference turns a saturated mean into an exact bang-bang output: a pre-scaling mean beyond ±3 is a pinned ±1, i.e. a full ±35° offset, with none of the training-time noise that blurred it. Combined with Seyde et al.'s evidence that extremes are rewarded, the first measurement to take is the per-joint saturation rate (fraction of decisions with |a| ≥ 0.99) and the mean |Δa| per decision, split by calm vs dynamic moments.
- The 20 %-per-decision easing is a discrete EMA (α = 0.2 at 10 Hz): time constant ≈ 0.1 s / −ln 0.8 ≈ 0.45 s (equivalent corner ≈ 0.3–0.36 Hz). It smooths the level but not the steps: the target still changes as a 10 Hz staircase, and a flip of the asked value from −35° to +35° gives a 14° step held for five physics steps, then 11.2°, 9.0°… Into a strong drive this is a torque impulse every 0.1 s, a plausible 10 Hz "tick" (S1). Applying the same easing per physics step (α_step = 1 − 0.8^(1/5) ≈ 0.044) or ramping linearly between decisions keeps the 0.45 s time constant without steps. Per CAPS, changing the filter after training changes the dynamics the policy expects, so it should be retrained or at least re-validated.
- If the easing is applied only at inference, CAPS predicts overshoot/oscillation; if it was in training but the eased target (or previous action) is not in the observation, the policy faces hidden state (CAPS saw learning failure). Check that each joint's current eased target is observed.
- "Worse in dynamic moments" fits the policy explanation: the largest corrective outputs and largest action changes happen in high-velocity, contact-rich states, which is exactly what LCP's state-gradient penalty and CAPS's spatial term constrain.
- Ranking of retrain options by evidence: (1) CAPS temporal term or LCP penalty (λ of order 0.002) — largest measured jitter cut at ~10 % return cost; (2) action-rate and second-difference (jerk) reward penalties — similar effect, more tuning; (3) keep PD targets (already used) and the 10 Hz rate (evidence that PD copes with low rates); (4) correlated exploration à la gSDE (would need a custom trainer; ML-Agents support unverified); (5) filters only if present in training with their state observed.

### Gaps
- No primary source measures how deterministic vs stochastic inference changes smoothness for PPO; CAPS only hypothesizes.
- ML-Agents' `deterministic_sample()` = mean, and gSDE support in ML-Agents, are unverified.
- LCP gives no PD gains or control frequency and no cutoff for its low-pass baseline, so its filter comparison is not calibrated to a 10 Hz, 0.45 s EMA like this project's.
- No study found that compares zero-order-hold vs interpolated PD targets between decisions for physics characters.

## 2. Control: PD gains vs inertia and time step, stable PD, damping, torque caps tied to human strength, muscle-like lag

### Takeaway
PhysX joint drives are implicit springs (stable at any stiffness, the problem Stable PD solves for explicit simulators), so the danger here is not numerical blow-up but over-strong, saturating actuators on light distal segments: a cap far above human joint torque reaches any target in a few physics steps and reads as snapping. Get-up and muscle research agree: strong characters find solutions but move erratically; progressively weaker and slower characters, state-dependent torque limits, or muscle actuation look human. Full normative human torque tables are paywalled; practitioner caps (DeepMimic) and partial biomechanical numbers are below. Whether Unity's ConfigurableJoint maximumForce is a force or an impulse limit is not documented and should be tested empirically.

### Cited Findings
**PD stability, Stable PD and PhysX drive semantics**
- Tan, Liu & Turk, "Stable Proportional-Derivative Controllers" (IEEE CG&A 31(4):34–44, 2011): computing joint torques from the character's positions and velocities at the next time step allows "arbitrarily high gains, even at large time steps"; conventional high-gain PD forces a much smaller time step to stay stable; SPD is stable even with simple Euler integration — [Tan, Liu & Turk 2011 (PDF)](https://www.cc.gatech.edu/%7Eturk/my_papers/stable_pd.pdf); [IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/5719567/figures) (full text not fetched: certificate error; equations unverified here)
- PhysX D6 drives: force = spring·(targetPosition − position) + damping·(targetVelocity − velocity); "Springs are fully implicit: that is, the force or acceleration is a function of the position and velocity after the solve"; "Physical lag can be reduced by stiffening the spring or supplying a velocity target"; "Acceleration drive is often easier to tune than force drive", and with acceleration springs "the solver will scale the magnitude of the force in accordance with the response of the two bodies" — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html)
- PhysX force limit: "the maximum force the drive can apply (note that this can be an impulse, depending on PxConstraintFlag::eDRIVE_LIMITS_ARE_FORCES)" — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html); [PxD6JointDrive reference](https://docs.nvidia.com/gameworks/content/gameworkslibrary/physx/apireference/files/classPxD6JointDrive.html)
- Unity documents JointDrive.maximumForce only as "Amount of force applied to push the object toward the defined direction" — [Unity 6.5 JointDrive.maximumForce](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.5/script-reference/unityengine/jointdrive/maximumforce.md). ArticulationDrive.forceLimit is documented in N or N·m — [Unity ArticulationDrive.forceLimit](https://docs.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/ArticulationDrive-forceLimit.html). Unity once had an articulation-drive bug where the force limit behaved as an impulse (a limit of 2 held a body that should fall); fixed in 2020.3, 2021.1, 2021.2, 2022.1 — [Unity issue tracker 1370338](https://issuetracker-mig.prd.it.unity3d.com/issues/physics-articulation-drive-force-limit-uses-impulse-values-rather-than-forces)
- "The force generated by actuation is not included in the force reported by the solver" — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html)

**Strength and naturalness**
- Tao, Wilson, Gou & van de Panne, "Learning to Get Up" (SIGGRAPH 2022): existing methods "often generate highly dynamic and erratic get-up motions" "which do not resemble human get-up strategies"; their method first uses "a strong character model, which facilitates the discovery of solution modes", then adapts "to work with progressively weaker versions of the character", then reproduces the weaker get-ups "at much slower speeds" — [arXiv 2205.00307](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00307)
- Jiang, Van Wouwe, De Groote & Liu (SIGGRAPH 2019): joint-actuated characters conventionally use empirically chosen constant torque bounds; transforming constant muscle-activation bounds into nonlinear, state-dependent torque limits (and metabolic energy into a function of torque, angle and velocity) generates "human-like motions comparable to those generated by musculotendon models"; proposed as a more anatomically realistic action space for deep RL; code released — [arXiv 1904.13041](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.13041)
- MTU (muscle) actuation produced visibly smoother actions and torque profiles and more natural motion once tuned — [Peng & van de Panne 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1611.01055)

**Torque caps: practitioner values and human data**
- DeepMimic's humanoid (body masses sum to ≈45 kg): torque limits chest 200, neck 50, hip 200, knee 150, ankle 90, shoulder 100, elbow 60 N·m; masses root 6, chest 14, neck 2, thigh 4.5, shin 3, foot 1, upper arm 1.5, forearm 1, hand 0.5 kg; knee limit −3.14…0 rad, elbow 0…3.14 rad — [DeepMimic humanoid3d.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xbpeng/DeepMimic/master/data/characters/humanoid3d.txt)
- Danneskiold-Samsøe et al. (Acta Physiologica 2009) measured normative isometric and isokinetic strength for all major joint movements, ages 20–80; men are 1.5–2× stronger than women for most muscle groups; men at 75 keep 54–89 % of earlier strength — [DTU Orbit record](https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/isokinetic-and-isometric-muscle-strength-in-a-healthy-population-/). Harbo et al. (Eur J Appl Physiol 2012) model peak isometric/isokinetic torque at knee, ankle, hip, shoulder, elbow and wrist in 178 adults (Biodex) — [Springer Medicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/maximal-isokinetic-and-isometric-muscle-strength-of-major-muscle/21069386). Both tables are paywalled (not retrieved).
- Partial numbers: maximal isometric knee torque at 80° flexion in trained women, extensors 143.5 ± 34.4 N·m and flexors 66.8 ± 13.8 N·m (2024 study; figures from search summary) — [DOAJ record, Arthroscopy, Sports Medicine and Rehabilitation 2024](https://doaj.org/article/6a064fa043ba438795c06424001a0335); men in a leg press: knee 289.5 ± 43.3 N·m, ankle 234.7 ± 56.6 N·m (multi-joint) vs isolated plantar flexion 185.7 ± 27.8 N·m — [BISp record](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201201000314)

**Muscle activation dynamics**
- First-order activation dynamics: activation follows excitation with time constants commonly taken as 10 ms (activation) and 40 ms (deactivation) (Zajac 1989, Winters 1990); Thelen 2003 follows Winters 1995 with different coefficients — [OpenSim: First-Order Activation Dynamics](https://opensimconfluence.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OpenSim/pages/53090590/First-Order+Activation+Dynamics)

### Inferences
- Illustrative magnitude (DeepMimic masses, my assumed segment lengths): an outstretched arm (1.5 + 1 + 0.5 kg at ≈0.14 / 0.40 / 0.60 m from the shoulder) has ≈0.37 kg·m² about the shoulder and ≈9 N·m gravity moment. A 100 N·m cap gives ≈270 rad/s², reaching the project's 20 rad/s clamp in ≈0.07 s (≈4 physics steps). A cap ~10× the static load on a light distal chain makes a snap inevitable whenever the target jumps — which the 10 Hz staircase does.
- If maximumForce reaches PhysX as an impulse limit (flag unset), the effective torque cap would be maximumForce/dt, i.e. 50× at 0.02 s. Unity's docs don't say. A two-minute test resolves it: hold a straight limb horizontal with the cap set just above and just below m·g·r of the limb.
- Damping: critical damping for effective inertia I and stiffness k is c = 2√(k·I) (standard second-order result; no source fetched). With I ≈ 0.37 kg·m² and k = 1000 N·m/rad, f_n ≈ 8.3 Hz — near the 10 Hz decision rate, so the staircase excites it. A natural frequency of roughly 1–3 Hz with damping ratio 0.7–1 is a plausible "human limb" starting point (judgement, to be checked against the project's drives). Because PhysX springs are implicit, high k is stable but stiff; because the cap saturates, behaviour at large errors is constant maximum torque (bang-bang) whatever k is.
- Make response time independent of segment mass: acceleration-mode drives where available (PhysX recommends them; Unity ArticulationDrive offers drive types; whether Unity 6's JointDrive exposes an acceleration mode is unverified), or scale k and c by each joint's effective inertia, so the same offset moves a hand and a thigh at similar speeds.
- Cheap state dependence: cap torque as a function of joint angle (torque–angle curve) and of joint speed (lower torque at high speed, like the Hill force–velocity curve), following Jiang 2019's direction. A velocity-dependent cap acts directly against snapping.
- Activation lags of 10/40 ms are a weak filter at a 10 Hz decision rate (corners ≈ 4–16 Hz); the smoothness of muscle-driven characters comes as much from force–length–velocity and tendon compliance. A first-order lag on drive force or cap (≈40–100 ms) is a cheap "muscle-like" low-pass that, unlike a post-hoc action filter, belongs to the plant and should be present in training.
- Tao 2022's strong → weak → slow curriculum is the direct precedent: if the policy was trained at or above human strength, retraining with progressively lower caps and slower targets is the literature-backed route to natural get-ups.

### Gaps
- SPD equations and gain recommendations not verified (fetch failed).
- No open-access table of human maximal joint torque by joint and sex retrieved (Harbo 2012, Danneskiold-Samsøe 2009 paywalled; NASA-STD-3000 strength tables not checked).
- Unity's angular drive spring units (per radian vs per degree) and the eDRIVE_LIMITS_ARE_FORCES mapping for Unity 6 ConfigurableJoint are unverified.
- Tao 2022's body text (how much weaker/slower; any naturalness measure) not read; the PDF exceeded the fetch limit.

## 3. Joint limits: anatomical ranges and coupling, implementing them in PhysX D6 / Unity ConfigurableJoint, and which violations read as "broken"

### Takeaway
"Broken" reads come from motions a human joint cannot make at all (a hinge bending sideways or hyperextending more than a few degrees, forearm twist past ~80–90°, shoulder rotation outside the range coupled to elevation) and from drives asked for targets beyond the limits. PhysX D6 (which ConfigurableJoint wraps) offers one twist pair limit and one swing cone (symmetric about the joint frame in Unity), hard by default, never projected, jittery when small or highly elliptical, singular at 180° swing. Coupled anatomical limits are therefore best enforced on the targets (script or policy side), with the physics limits as a slightly wider backstop, or through ArticulationBody's per-axis asymmetric limits.

### Cited Findings
**Normative ranges**
- CDC Joint ROM study (Soucie et al.), ages 20–44, mean degrees (female / male): hip flexion 133.8 / 130.4; hip extension 18.1 / 17.4; knee flexion 141.9 / 137.7; knee extension 1.6 / 1.0; ankle dorsiflexion 13.8 / 12.7; plantar flexion 62.1 / 54.6; shoulder flexion 172.0 / 168.8; elbow flexion 150.0 / 144.6; elbow extension 4.7 / 0.8; pronation 82.0 / 76.9; supination 90.6 / 85.0. Ages 45–69 are slightly smaller (male elbow extension −0.7). The page does not define its sign convention or active/passive method — [CDC Joint Range of Motion Study](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/ncbddd/jointrom/index.html)
- Healthy adult elbow flexion ≈130–154° and extension between about −6° and 11° (negative = hyperextension); age, sex and BMI matter — [PMC6555111](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6555111)
- A musculoskeletal shoulder model reproduced ≈180° abduction, 180° flexion and 50° extension, "matching human ranges" — [arXiv 2410.05931](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.05931)

**Coupling**
- Shoulder abduction range depends on humeral rotation: −50° internal rotation gave the smallest range (90°), 80° external rotation the largest (165°) — [Haptic shoulder, arXiv 2409.13905](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.13905)
- Digital-human models treat humeral axial-rotation limits as dependent on elevation plane and elevation (Wang et al. 1998 data) — [US patent 10621384](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10621384); scapulothoracic motion supplies 13–20° (76–94 %) of humeral axial rotation during elevation, correlated with the plane of elevation — [Henry Ford Health scholarly commons](https://scholarlycommons.henryford.com/orthopaedics_articles/410)
- Baerlocher & Boulic, "Parametrization and range of motion of the ball-and-socket joint" (Deformable Avatars, 2001), is the classic graphics treatment of swing–twist parametrization with ROM — [EPFL Infoscience record](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/100909) (content not read). Swing–twist replaces the first two Euler angles with an axis-angle swing that has no longitudinal component, avoiding Euler singularities — [arXiv 2311.10653](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.10653)

**PhysX D6 / Unity ConfigurableJoint limits and drives**
- "If both swing degrees of freedom are limited, a limit cone is generated"; "very small or highly elliptical limit cones may result in solver jitter"; one swing limited with the other locked: max π; with the other free: max π/2; twist is a pair limit with lower and upper bounds; "There is a nasty singularity at π radians (180 degrees) swing", and Euler-type decompositions risk "gimbal lock" at π/2 — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html)
- Limits are hard by default ("motion will simply stop"); soft limits pull back with a spring; "stable limit behavior requires a contactDistance tolerance" — "A larger contact makes the limit less likely to be violated even at high relative velocity" at extra cost; "Limits are not projected" — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html)
- "If any angular degrees of freedom are locked, the SLERP drive parameters are ignored"; SLERP follows the shortest arc but "may cause unintuitive changes in the joint's twist and swing"; the twist-and-swing drive has "a singularity when driven to 180 degrees swing"; "Best results will be achieved when the drive target inputs are consistent with the joint freedom and limit constraints" — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html)
- Unity ConfigurableJoint: the Angular Y limit "regards both the upper and lower angular limits as the same" (same for Z), i.e. symmetric swing; angular contact distance is the tolerance "before the limit is enforced. A larger tolerance violates the limit less often when the object is moving fast"; X has separate low/high limits — [Unity 2019.4 manual, Configurable Joint](https://docs.unity3d.com/es/current/Manual/class-ConfigurableJoint.html) (older manual; I believe the property set is unchanged in Unity 6, unverified)
- Unity 6: "Avoid small Joint angles of Angular Y Limit and Angular Z Limit… the minimum angles should be around 5 to 15 degrees in order to be stable. Instead of using a small angle, try setting the angle to zero. This locks the axis" — [Unity 6 ragdoll stability](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/physics-section/physics-overview/ragdoll/ragdoll-stability)
- ArticulationBody spherical joints: Swing Y, Swing Z and Twist each Locked/Limited/Free, each with its own lower and upper limit — [Unity ArticulationBody manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-ArticulationBody.html)

### Inferences
- Visual "broken" list (anatomy plus the ROM data; no perceptual study found): (1) elbow or knee hyperextension beyond ~5–10° (normal extension ≈0–5°); (2) any sideways (varus/valgus) bend at elbow or knee; (3) forearm twist beyond ~80–90° either way, or twist appearing at the elbow hinge instead of along the forearm; (4) shoulder extension or adduction behind the body beyond ~50–60°, or abduction combined with internal rotation beyond ~90°; (5) hip extension beyond ~20°; (6) elbow or knee flexion beyond ~140–150° (segments interpenetrating); (7) wrist or ankle beyond ~60–70°.
- In this project's convention (bend axis = PhysX twist/X; two swings Y/Z), an elbow's or knee's swings are exactly the impossible directions: sideways bend and axial twist. Leaving them Limited with small symmetric angles is both a broken-look source (S2) and a jitter source (S1) per PhysX and Unity. The catch: locking Y/Z makes PhysX ignore Slerp, so hinges would need angularXDrive with rotationDriveMode X-and-YZ while ball joints (shoulders, hips, spine, neck) keep Slerp.
- Unity's symmetric swing cone can only approximate asymmetric ranges (shoulder flexion ~170° vs extension ~50°; hip flexion ~130° vs extension ~18°) by rotating the joint frame so the cone is centred on the range, or by using the twist pair for the most asymmetric DOF (as this project does for bends).
- The ±35° offsets from key poses can command targets beyond limits. The drive then pushes into a hard limit with up to its full cap: the joint sits at its extreme (S2) and chatters there or is driven past it under impacts, and since limits are not projected nothing pulls it back except the limit itself (S1/S2). Clamp each target (key pose + offset) to the anatomical range minus a 5–10° margin before it reaches the joint; for ball joints, clamp in swing–twist space with a coupled table (twist range as a function of swing direction, reach-cone style); keep the physics limits slightly wider with contactDistance > 0, possibly as damped soft limits for a tissue-like stop.
- Changing physics limits at runtime to emulate coupling risks instant violations and pops when a limit is tightened while the joint is outside it; coupling on targets is safer.
- Keep every joint frame used by limits or drives away from 180° swing (PhysX singularity), e.g. so that anatomical extremes stay within ~120–150° of swing.
- ArticulationBody (reduced coordinates, no stretch, per-axis asymmetric limits) is the in-engine alternative but a large change (per-axis drives, no Slerp); see section 4.

### Gaps
- No source found for "joint sinus cones" (Engin & Tümer) or for a perceptual study of which violations viewers read as "broken".
- Baerlocher & Boulic's coupled-limit formulation not read; reach-cone limits (Wilhelms & Van Gelder) not checked.
- Knee hyperextension norms: CDC gives mean knee extension of 1.0–1.6° with no sign convention; no authoritative hyperextension distribution retrieved.
- Current Unity 6 ConfigurableJoint manual wording (elliptical cone, maximum limit angles) not re-checked.

## 4. PhysX/Unity and rendering pitfalls, and a cross-cutting ranking against the three symptoms

### Takeaway
With this configuration the bodies cannot move a limb "out of frame" in one rendered frame: rotation is clamped to 0.4 rad per physics step, depenetration to 3 cm per step, linear joint stretch to 0.02 m by projection. S3 therefore almost certainly lives in the render path: stale or mismatched interpolation state at mode switches, Quaternion.FromToRotation's undefined axis near opposite directions (and its loss of twist), quaternion sign or normalization, NaNs, or teleports interacting with interpolation. S1 most likely comes from the policy/drive combination (sections 1–2) plus documented solver jitter sources (small or elliptical cones, mass ratios above 10, tight projection, PGS rather than TGS). S2 most likely comes from limit and target design (section 3) plus twist lost in reconstructed bones and skinning.

### Cited Findings
**Projection**
- "Projection is not a physical process and does not preserve momentum or respect collision geometry"; "It is best avoided if practical"; "Very small tolerance values for projection may result in jittering around the joint"; trees are projected toward a chosen root and cycles are broken by dropping edges; "By default projection is disabled"; "Angular projection is implemented only for the cases when two or three angular degrees of freedom are locked"; "Limits are not projected" — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html); same text in the [PhysX 3.4.1 guide mirror](https://documentation.help/NVIDIA-PhysX-SDK-3.4.1/Joints.html)
- Unity suggests projection for "extreme circumstances (such as spawning partially inside a wall or pushed with a large force)" when ragdolls stretch — [Unity 6 ragdoll stability](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/physics-section/physics-overview/ragdoll/ragdoll-stability); Projection Distance is "the distance the joint must move beyond its constraints before the physics engine attempts to snap it back" — [Unity 2019.4 manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/es/current/Manual/class-ConfigurableJoint.html)

**Interpolation and teleports**
- Unity 6 interpolation uses "the pose of the Rigidbody from the previous two physics updates" and "delays the Rigidbody's pose by one physics update"; jitter "can happen when the rate of physics simulation updates… is slower than the application's frame rate"; "When interpolation or extrapolation is enabled, the physics system takes control of the Rigidbody's transform… follow any direct (non-physics) change to the transform with a Physics.SyncTransforms call. Otherwise, Unity ignores any transform change that does not originate from the physics system" — [Unity 6 Rigidbody interpolation](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/physics-section/physics-overview/rigidbody/rigidbody-interpolation)
- Older Unity docs: MovePosition respects interpolation; to teleport, set Rigidbody.position so no intermediate positions are rendered — [Unity 5.5 Rigidbody.MovePosition](https://docs.unity3d.com/550/Documentation/ScriptReference/Rigidbody.MovePosition.html) (older behaviour). An unofficial blog reports a one-frame smear when teleporting an interpolated body, fixed by disabling interpolation for that frame plus Physics.SyncTransforms — [bugnet blog](https://bugnet.io/blog/fix-unity-rigidbody-interpolation-jitter-after-teleport); this conflicts with the old official claim.
- "Never use direct Transform access with Kinematic Rigidbody components connected by Joints to other Rigidbody components", since it skips PhysX's computation of internal velocities — [Unity 6 ragdoll stability](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/physics-section/physics-overview/ragdoll/ragdoll-stability)
- The fixed-timestep pattern renders a blend of previous and current physics state with `alpha = accumulator / dt`; displaying physics "at a time slightly different from the render time" causes stutter — [Fiedler, Fix Your Timestep!](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/)

**Solver, mass ratios, depenetration, settings**
- Unity 6 Solver Type: Temporal Gauss Seidel "offers a better convergence and a better handling of high-mass ratios, minimizes energy introduced when correcting penetrations and improves the resistance of joints to overstretch. It usually helps when you experience some erratic behavior during simulation with the default solver" (PGS is the default); solver iterations are typically used "to reduce the jitter resulting from joints or contacts"; Default Max Angular Speed defaults to 50 rad/s; contact offset defaults to 0.01 and "if set too close to zero, it can cause jitter"; the bounce threshold "also reduces jitter" — [Unity 6 Physics settings](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/unity-editor/editor-settings-reference/comp-manager-group/class-physics-manager)
- Unity 6 ragdoll tips: solver iterations 10–20 for jittering joints; a 2:1 mass ratio is fine, 10:1 "can become jittery"; disabling Enable Preprocessing can stop joints separating or moving erratically when constraints cannot be satisfied; lower maxDepenetrationVelocity so overlapping bodies "exit each other more smoothly"; avoid scale other than 1 — [Unity 6 ragdoll stability](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/physics-section/physics-overview/ragdoll/ragdoll-stability)
- PhysX: "Mass ratios of higher than 10 are best avoided" for a light object constrained between heavy ones; make the lighter body the second actor; mass/inertia scaling speeds convergence but is "fundamentally nonphysical" (momentum not conserved, energy may increase); with mass scaling or infinite inertia use eDISABLE_PREPROCESSING with a small minResponseThreshold — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html)
- NVIDIA described PhysX 4's TGS solver as making jointed and articulated systems such as ragdolls much more robust (via search summary) — [NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/?p=28836)

**Engine version and ArticulationBody**
- Unity 2019.3 upgraded PhysX from 3.4 to 4.x (its release page says 4.1; Unity's later blog says 2019.3 moved to 4.0 and 2020.1 to 4.1, which also introduced ArticulationBody and credits PhysX 4.1's TGS solver for reliable robot arms) — [Unity 2019.3 programmer tools](https://unity.com/en/releases/2019-3/programmer-tools); [Unity blog on articulation bodies](https://unity.com/fr/blog/industry/use-articulation-bodies-to-easily-prototype-industrial-designs-with-realistic-motion-and). Unity's May 2025 physics status lists PhysX 5 only as a possible future custom backend — [Unity Discussions, May 2025](https://discussions.unity.com/t/physics-development-status-and-next-milestones-may-2025/1637988). The Unity 6 settings page names no PhysX version.
- ArticulationBody uses a Featherstone solver in reduced coordinates; each body keeps coordinates relative to its parent along unlocked DOFs only, which prevents unwanted stretch; drive effect = stiffness·(drivePosition − targetPosition) − damping·(driveVelocity − targetVelocity); forceLimit is the maximum force or torque — [ArticulationBody scripting reference (Unity 6)](https://docs-alpha.unity3d.com/ScriptReference/ArticulationBody.html); [ArticulationBody manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/class-ArticulationBody.html)

**Rendering and skinning**
- For exactly opposite vectors a from-to rotation has no unique axis; a library must pick an arbitrary perpendicular (one returned identity, a bug); Unity's FromToRotation docs say nothing about the antiparallel case — [Codea forum thread](https://talk.codea.io/t/quat-fromtorotation-with-opposite-vectors/13912); [Unity Quaternion.FromToRotation](https://docs.unity3d.com/462/Documentation/ScriptReference/Quaternion.FromToRotation.html)
- Swing–twist decomposition is singular at 180° swing — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html)
- Linear blend skinning collapses under twist ("candy wrapper"); dual-quaternion skinning (Kavan et al., I3D 2007 / TOG 2008) removes it at slight extra cost, without rig changes, but can bulge — [Kavan DQS page](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/dq/); [CTU publication page](https://dcgi.fel.cvut.cz/en/publications/2007/kavan-i3d-sdq/)

### Inferences
**Physics bounds from the configuration**
- Rotation per physics step ≤ 20 rad/s × 0.02 s = 0.4 rad ≈ 23°; with interpolation that is ≈8° per rendered frame at 144 fps and ≈19° at 60 fps. Depenetration ≤ 3 cm per step. Linear joint stretch is projected back to 0.02 m. Angular projection (5°) never runs for these joints (all angular DOFs Limited, none Locked), so the 5° setting is inert, and limits are never projected. A limb leaving the frame within one rendered frame is beyond what the bodies can do unless state turns NaN or garbage.

**Likeliest S3 mechanisms (render path)**
1. Stale or wrong "previous" pose at a mode switch, reset or teleport: manual interpolation blends from a buffer filled in another mode, before the teleport, or never initialized (identity/zero), giving one frame halfway to garbage. Typical when the buffer only refreshes while its mode is active.
2. FromToRotation hand-over: as the limb direction nears the opposite of the reference "from" direction, the axis swings wildly, and the result always uses the minimal arc, discarding the physics twist; child bones built on it inherit the flip. A zero-length direction (coincident points) is undefined.
3. Mixed sources: a parent from the interpolated Transform (one step behind) and a child from raw Rigidbody.position/rotation (latest), or a manual blend of already-interpolated Transforms (double interpolation). At 20 rad/s the one-step offset reaches 23°, which reads as S2 or S1; when the source changes at a mode switch, it is a one-frame jump.
4. Quaternion hemisphere: a component-wise lerp of q_prev and q_curr with a negative dot product passes near zero, and normalizing amplifies it into an arbitrary orientation for a frame. Whether Unity's Quaternion.Lerp/Slerp guard against this is unverified; hand-written Vector4-style lerps do not.
5. Interpolation alpha outside [0, 1] (wrong clock, unscaled vs scaled time, sampled in the wrong callback, buffers shifted in Update) → extrapolation.
6. Teleport plus interpolation: transform writes on interpolated bodies are ignored without SyncTransforms (Unity 6 docs); the community reports a one-frame smear.
7. NaN or Inf in any bone (normalizing a zero quaternion, alpha divided by zero, FromToRotation of zero vectors) stretches skinned vertices to infinity for a frame, which looks exactly like a limb "flicking out of frame".

**S1 contributors at solver level**
- Small or elliptical swing cones (hinges with small Y/Z) → solver jitter (PhysX, Unity).
- Mass ratios above 10 between connected parts (e.g. a very light hand or foot on a heavy segment) → jitter; TGS is Unity's documented remedy for erratic behaviour and high mass ratios.
- Tight projection (0.02 m) → "jittering around the joint" per PhysX; projection also ignores collisions, so it can push a limb into the ground, after which 1.5 m/s depenetration lets it out slowly over several frames of visible clipping — a fit for "clipping".
- Limits without contactDistance get violated at high relative velocity in dynamic moments.
- The 20 rad/s clamp on one body but not its neighbours may leave joint error to correct on the next step, a possible twitch in dynamic moments (unverified, no source).

**Cross-cutting ranking (my judgement from the evidence above)**

| Rank | Cause | S1 twitch | S2 broken look | S3 one-frame flick | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Render-path reconstruction: stale/mismatched interpolation buffers at mode switches, FromToRotation flips and twist loss, quaternion sign, NaN | medium | medium-high | high | Unity interpolation docs; FromToRotation antiparallel; physics bounds arithmetic |
| 2 | Policy actions: saturated means under deterministic inference, 10 Hz target staircase, ±35° offsets | high | medium | low | CAPS, LCP, Seyde; ML-Agents clamp |
| 3 | Drive strength and stiffness too high for limb inertia (and possible impulse semantics of the cap) | high | low-medium | low | Tao 2022, Jiang 2019, PhysX drive docs |

### Gaps
- Unity's handling of FromToRotation with antiparallel input, the sign handling of Quaternion.Lerp/Slerp, and how NaN bones propagate through SkinnedMeshRenderer are not documented in the pages found.
- No authoritative Unity 6 statement on teleporting interpolated bodies (old doc vs blog conflict).
- The exact PhysX version in Unity 6 is not stated on the pages accessed (only 2019.3/2020.1 history).
- Whether PhysX 5 removed joint projection was not checked (relevant if Unity changes backend).

## 5. Detection: finding these artifacts automatically, frame by frame, at the shipping frame rate

### Takeaway
Research measures smoothness with action jitter and third-derivative metrics (LCP), FFT-weighted frequency (CAPS), jerk and spectral arc length (motor control), and physical plausibility with penetration, floating and foot-skating metrics (PhysDiff). Games research is moving toward video-level ML glitch detection, but it is immature. For one's own game the most reliable detector is instrumentation of invariants on both clocks: per physics step (actions, saturation, limits, separations, penetrations) and per rendered frame (displacement bounds, bone-length constancy, rendered-vs-simulated difference, NaN). Run it at the frame rates players see: fixed-rate capture changes how many physics steps fall in each frame and hides bugs that only appear on frames without a physics step.

### Cited Findings
- LCP reports Action Jitter, DoF position jitter (third-derivative metric), DoF velocity, energy and base acceleration alongside task return — [LCP](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11825v2)
- CAPS smoothness Sm = (2/(n·f_s))·Σ M_i·f_i over the FFT of the action signal — [CAPS](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.06644)
- PhysDiff: Penetrate = ground to lowest mesh vertex below ground; Float = lowest vertex above ground; 5 mm tolerance; Skate = mean horizontal displacement of foot joints in ground contact across two adjacent frames; Phys-Err = sum of the three — [PhysDiff](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.02500)
- A 2025 motion-generation survey lists jerk for smoothness, plus Footskating Frame Ratio and Foot Sliding Factor — [arXiv 2507.05419](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05419)
- Balasubramanian et al. (JNER 2015): a valid smoothness measure must be dimensionless and monotonic with submovement changes; they propose spectral arc length (SPARC) and argue mean squared jerk is not a valid smoothness measure — [PMC4674971](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4674971); applying SPARC or LDLJ-V directly to acceleration signals is incorrect — [Balasubramanian et al., Frontiers 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bioengineering-and-biotechnology/articles/10.3389/fbioe.2020.558771/pdf)
- Game-video ML: "CLIP meets GamePhysics" retrieves physics bugs zero-shot from 26,954 gameplay videos — [ar5iv 2203.11096](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.11096); PhysGame offers 880 gameplay videos of physics glitches in 12 commonsense categories for video-LLM evaluation — [arXiv 2412.01800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01800v1); patents claim ML detection of animation-frame errors, including physics-rule violations — [USPTO patent PDF](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12699646)
- Unity captureDeltaTime: "Time.time increases at an interval of captureDeltaTime… regardless of real time and the duration of a frame"; it "does not have any affect on Time.unscaledTime" — [Unity Time.captureDeltaTime](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/current/script-reference/unityengine/time/capturedeltatime)
- Unity interpolation lags one physics step, and jitter appears when physics runs slower than the frame rate — [Unity 6 Rigidbody interpolation](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/physics-section/physics-overview/rigidbody/rigidbody-interpolation)
- Drive (actuation) force is not included in the force PhysX reports for a joint — [PhysX 4.1 Joints guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/PhysX/4.1/physx/documentation/PhysXGuide/Manual/Joints.html)

### Inferences
**Per physics step (after the simulation step, e.g. a WaitForFixedUpdate coroutine)**
- Policy: per joint |a| ≥ 0.99 saturation rate; |Δa| per decision; second difference of actions; CAPS-style Sm over a sliding window (10 Hz actions → Nyquist 5 Hz).
- Targets: per-step change of the final target rotation (key pose + eased offset) in degrees; flag steps above a few degrees and key-pose switches that are not blended.
- Drive saturation: since drive torque is not reported, estimate demanded torque k·Δθ − c·ω against the cap; log the fraction of steps at the cap.
- Limits: the joint's actual twist and swing from the bodies' relative rotation compared with the configured limits; count and size of violations (limits are not projected). Anatomy: the same angles compared with the anatomical table (section 3) to count "broken" poses separately from physics-limit violations.
- Separation: anchor distance between connected bodies; count steps above 0.02 m (projection events, i.e. teleports).
- Angular velocity: per body ω, steps at the 20 rad/s clamp, and per-step angular acceleration spikes.
- Contact and plausibility: ground penetration and float (PhysDiff-style, ~5 mm tolerance), self-penetration depth between non-adjacent parts (e.g. via Physics.ComputePenetration; API not re-checked here), foot skate while in contact.

**Per rendered frame (LateUpdate, after every bone write, at the real frame rate)**
- Displacement bound: per bone, rotation between consecutive rendered frames must be ≤ 20 rad/s × frame Δt plus a margin, and translation ≤ a speed bound × Δt. Since the bodies cannot exceed this, any violation is a render-path bug by construction (S3 detector).
- Rendered vs simulated: angle between each drawn bone's world rotation and its physics body's (interpolated) rotation, and the direction error; flag beyond a few degrees. Catches FromToRotation twist loss, stale buffers, mixed sources, wrong alpha.
- Rigidity: drawn bone lengths and parent–child joint distances constant (mixing interpolated and raw sources breaks this).
- Numeric health: NaN/Inf in any bone matrix, quaternion norm far from 1, dot(q_prev, q_curr) < 0 before a lerp, interpolation alpha outside [0, 1], number of physics steps since the last frame.
- Events: tag frames with mode switches, resets/teleports, decisions, contact starts; keep a ring buffer of the last ~2 s of all metrics and dump it (plus a screenshot) when any invariant trips, so one-frame bugs are caught in normal play.

**Testing at the shipping frame rate**
- Steps per rendered frame depend on frame rate. At 144 fps (6.9 ms frames) only ≈35 % of frames contain a physics step and ≈65 % contain none; at 240 fps ≈79 % contain none; at 60 fps ≈17 % contain none; at 30 fps every frame contains 1–2 steps and none contain zero. A capture at 30 fps (captureDeltaTime) can never show a bug that only occurs on zero-step frames (buffers shifted in Update, bones written only when a step happened), while the Game Director's uncapped build hits such frames most of the time.
- Run the detectors in a build with real, variable deltaTime at several caps (30, 60, 144, 240, uncapped) and with injected hitches (a long frame giving 3+ steps, then several zero-step frames), since mode switches that coincide with zero- or multi-step frames are the likeliest S3 triggers.
- Any per-frame smoothing written as `Lerp(x, target, constant)` without Δt scaling behaves differently at 300 fps than at 30 fps; captureDeltaTime also leaves unscaledTime untouched, so code on unscaled time diverges in captures.
- Video ML detectors (CLIP-style retrieval, video LLMs) are useful for triaging large playtest recordings but give no per-frame guarantees; numeric invariants are the primary detector for one's own build.

### Gaps
- No source found that describes industry practice for per-frame invariant checking of physics-driven characters (GDC talks on active ragdolls not retrieved), so the detector list is engineering inference built on research metrics.
- No published thresholds for "visible twitch" in degrees per frame or in jerk units for game characters; thresholds must be calibrated on the project's own footage judged by the Game Director.
- Exact frame-pacing behaviour of Unity 6's player loop with uncapped vsync-off rendering (how many FixedUpdates per frame under hitches; maximumDeltaTime caps) was not re-checked.
