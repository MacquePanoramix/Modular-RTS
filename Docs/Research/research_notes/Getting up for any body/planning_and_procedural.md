# Planning, trajectory optimisation, designed controllers and procedural animation for getting any body up from the floor

Scope: non-learned (or lightly learned) methods that adapt by construction to the body they are given: online planning (sampling and gradient MPC), offline trajectory optimisation per body, designed controllers that generalise across proportions, robotics multi-contact planning with torque feasibility, procedural animation for arbitrary morphologies, hybrids of a body-relative plan plus a physics tracker, and what Unity/PhysX allows. The target is Wonder Gather's player-built units: about 15 rigid parts, PD drives capped at each joint's strength (limb thickness × one strength number), rising only by their own joint torques, at once for any body, natural and grounded, with proportions and strength visibly shaping the rise. Only a few units are down at once, but many units exist.

This builds on the October project notes, which already covered SAMCON and its successors, Al Borno et al. 2017 (domain-of-attraction trees), Faloutsos 2001, Stückler 2006, Kenwright 2023, Jones 2011, the basics of MuJoCo MPC and Tassa 2012, and the Unity multi-scene mechanics ([sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>); [designed-controller notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/designed_controllers.md>); [report](<../../reports/Getting up by its own joints.md>)). Those are referenced here, not re-derived. New in this pass: full-text numbers for Hämäläinen et al. 2014 and 2015 (the October pass could not reach the Aalto server), GPU and feedback sampling MPC from 2024–2026, contact-invariant optimisation on non-human bodies, Lin & Huang's body edits and timings, how Coros et al. 2010 handled proportions, mass and torque limits, Spore's morphology-independent semantics, the actuation-aware feasible region, morphology-driven self-righting, a body-shape-conditioned controller, and Unity articulation vs joint facts.

## 1. Online sampling and optimisation MPC: rollouts × horizon, compute per control step, real-time factors, look, and failure modes

### Takeaway
Online MPC is the one family that adapts to any body by construction, because it simply simulates the body it is given, and the full texts confirm it improvises multi-phase get-ups (plant a hand, pull a foot in, shift weight onto it) from simple costs using joint actuation only. But every published get-up of this kind spent roughly 1,000 simulated character-seconds per wall-second for a single character (Hämäläinen 2014: 25 rollouts × 2 s per frame at about 20 fps on 12 threads; C-PBP 2015: 32 × 1.2 s at 20–30 fps on 6 cores), still ran only "near" or "barely" real time, and its own authors call the motion "stiff" and "noisy" with "unnecessary joint contortions"; newer GPU samplers reach hardware quadrupeds but show no get-up, and plain MPPI is shown to be unstable on humanoids unless the sampling itself uses feedback.

### Cited Findings
**Hämäläinen, Eriksson, Tanskanen, Kyrki, Lehtinen, SIGGRAPH 2014, "Online motion synthesis using sequential Monte Carlo" (full text, 2014)**
- Character: 15 rigid bodies joined by 3-DOF ball joints and 1-DOF hinges (elbows, knees, ankles), with no clavicles, toes or fingers. Physics: ODE 0.12 with its direct "big matrix" LCP solver at a 1/30 s time step — [SMC 2014 preprint](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- Control: a non-uniform spline of 4 control points, each holding 30 target joint angles, 3 torque limits (torso, arms, legs) and 1 time coordinate, so 136 optimised variables. ODE motors are driven by target velocities (q_target − q_current)/Δt within the torque limits. Torque limits were sampled within 50–150 Nm for the lightweight model and 50–200 Nm for the heavier one, so the character can choose to be soft, for example to cushion landings — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- Cost: getting up uses the same objective as balancing (target pose, up-vector, velocity and displacement terms with tolerances). It is evaluated at every frame between 0.5 s and the horizon and the best frame is kept, which gives the optimiser "slack in terms of when to reach the goals". A smoothness term on mean squared acceleration and jerk, including the past two frames, "reduces movement jitter, which could otherwise be a problem in a stochastic sampling system like ours" — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- Compute: "approximately 20 fps with a 1/30s physics time step, N = 25 samples per frame, and a planning horizon of 2 seconds" on an Intel i7-4930K (12 logical cores, 75–80% utilisation). "On a 2012 MacBook Pro laptop with a 2.4GHz processor, the same settings yield 6-10 fps." Using 100 samples "slows the simulation down considerably"; fewer samples or a shorter horizon "yields fully real-time but unreliable results" — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- Get-up behaviour: "online, near-real-time synthesis of complex get up strategies, e.g., planting a hand on the ground, leaning on the hand to allow moving a foot closer, and finally shifting weight on the foot to rise up". The authors contrast this with Tassa et al., whose character rises "in a single bounce, implying rather loose limits on the control torques", and note that Tassa's group later solved springing up "by designing a state machine that explicitly breaks down the task into a sequence of subtasks" (Erez et al. 2013). Their horizon is up to 4 s versus Tassa's 0.5 s — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- Robustness test: 100 spheres, each 3× heavier than the character, thrown from random directions; success means balanced 5 s after the throw. "The most typical [failure] cases are wide splits and lying on the back." The heavier character model "with no changes compared to ST ... yields abysmal success rates at low sample budgets"; it did better only after a "roll away from back" goal was switched on, the horizon raised to 3.5 s, and success measured over 8 s. Adding 3 learned predictions per frame (FLANN, 100k training vectors) improved success at low sample budgets. A 2 s horizon was "a reasonable default, and longer horizons do not produce considerable benefit" — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- Naturalness, as admitted by the authors: "movement is sometimes stiff and has unnecessary joint contortions"; the hands are kept "greedily close to the target pose"; adding shoulder and elbow torque minimisation instead left "the hands hanging limp"; and the target-pose initial guess can make the character "assume the target pose prematurely while still moving. Sometimes this appears almost unphysical" — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- Engine requirements: "the iterative solvers in ODE, Bullet Physics or PhysX engines were not stable enough for active characters except at very small time steps that were not computationally efficient". ODE had to be made "causal" (non-deterministic optimisations removed): "If the simulation is not fully causal, the sampler sometimes forgets a chosen control strategy before it has been completely executed." — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)

**Hämäläinen, Rajamäki, Liu, SIGGRAPH 2015, "Online control of simulated humanoids using particle belief propagation" (C-PBP; full text, 2015)**
- Character: 15 capsules with 3-DOF ball joints except 1-DOF knees, elbows and ankles; 1.75 m and 70 kg. Physics: ODE's direct O(N³) solver at 1/30 s; the iterative solver "requires a time step < 1/100 s for stability in our case". Unity 3D was used only for rendering and skinning; the physics ran in ODE — [C-PBP preprint](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)
- Control per prediction step: 30 motor target angular velocities plus motor force limits for 5 groups (left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg, torso), 35 parameters — [C-PBP](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)
- Settings: N = 32 trajectories per frame and a 1.2 s horizon for all behaviours. The video was captured "with N = 32 on a 6 core desktop PC"; 30 fps needs N ≤ 24, "but the subjective quality of the results is better at N = 32, which decreases movement noise and results in 20-30fps" — [C-PBP](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)
- Parameter study: one test task starts "in a supine T-pose so that it has to both get up and balance" (25 repetitions). "Increasing N and/or K improves robustness and both parameters must be large enough for any chance of success." On the ball task, doubling N raised success from 0.6 to 0.92, and a 2 s horizon "starts increasing movement noise" — [C-PBP](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)
- Limitations: the system is "only barely real-time"; "movement is slightly stiff and noisy". Lowering the sampling variances reduces noise but "also decreases the success rate of recovering from large disturbances" unless N and K grow. Heavy contacts on the ends of the hand capsules "look unnatural", which the authors fixed by "manually overriding the sampled wrist controls" — [C-PBP](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)
- The balancing cost, reused by every behaviour, includes centre-of-mass (COM) speed, COM distance from the line between the feet, pelvis height above the feet versus a target of 1.04 m, a penalty on feet more than 0.8 m apart, pelvis angular speed, and pelvis up-vector error — [C-PBP](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)

**Rajamäki & Hämäläinen, SCA 2017, "Augmenting sampling based controllers with machine learning" (abstract only)**
- A fast nearest-neighbour learner and a slower neural network learn from the noisy planned control of a sampling MPC; the augmented controller "can produce stable gaits within a minute on a 4-core CPU" for 3D bipeds (search summary of the abstract) — [Eurographics Digital Library](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1145/3099564-3099579)

**MuJoCo MPC and Tassa 2012 (October notes, summarised)**
- MJPC predictive sampling for the humanoid uses 10 rollouts × 23 steps; planning updates take 1–20 ms; the humanoid "can be simulated 4000 times faster than realtime on a single CPU thread"; the code is "not production-quality software". Tassa's iLQG get-up ran 7× slower than real time with 4,000 dynamics evaluations per control step, rose "with a single bounce", and failed under ±300 Nm limits in an independent test — [October sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>); [MJPC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541); [Tassa 2012](https://02465material.pages.compute.dtu.dk/02465public/_assets/tassa2012.pdf); [Al Borno et al. 2017](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)
- MJPC evaluated on HumanoidBench (Unitree H1, 2024): iLQG for Stand and Walk with a 0.35 s horizon; sampling for Push with a 1.0 s horizon; MacBook Air M1. "We find that sparse reward functions of HumanoidBench yield undesirable and unrealistic behaviors when optimized." The fix added head height, pelvis-to-feet height, COM velocity, COM over the feet, deviation from a canonical posture, facing direction and control magnitude terms. Average squared joint velocity in Stand fell from 1.49 to 0.01. The Stand episode (2.0 s) took 55 s of total inference with a fixed iteration count, which is not a real-time configuration — [MJPC on HumanoidBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.00342v1)

**GPU and feedback sampling MPC, 2024–2026**
- DIAL-MPC (Xue, Pan, Yi, Qu, Shi; ICRA 2025): 2,048 samples, a 20-step (0.4 s) horizon and 50 Hz control on an RTX 4090 with an i9-13900KF. Walking and jumping on a real Unitree Go2. Crate climbing used 4,096 samples, a 1 s horizon and 4 annealing iterations, about 30 s of computation for a 2 s plan ("1/15 the speed of real world"). The Unitree H1 humanoid appears only in simulation (crate pushing). No get-up or recovery from lying. Walking tracking error 0.024 versus 0.19–0.23 for MPPI; crate-climbing success 9/10 versus 2–3/10 for MPPI. "DIAL-MPC requires fast simulation to generate samples." The paper's own speed-up claims are inconsistent (13.4× in the abstract, 3.9× in the text) — [DIAL-MPC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.15610)
- FS-MPC (Pan, Yi, Zhang, Manchester, Qu, Shi; arXiv 2608.19443, 2026): for "high-dimensional and open-loop unstable dynamical systems, the required number of samples to improve the control sequence will grow exponentially with the horizon". Sampling around an optimised feedback policy fixes this. On a real Unitree H1, MPPI failed to stabilise the robot because of the sampling variance, while FS-MPC "finishes the task thanks to the feedback design"; its gain over iLQR was "only marginal". No get-up task was found in the text — [FS-MPC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19443)
- Reference-free sampling MPC (Schramm et al. 2025): the simulated Unitree G1 needed 60–70 samples and real-time execution was "prevented"; no get-up — [October sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>); [arXiv 2511.19204](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19204v2)
- Whole-body iLQR MPC on MuJoCo with finite-difference derivatives (Zhang, Howell et al., CMU and Google DeepMind; arXiv 2503.04613, 2025): real time on hardware with a controller at about 50 Hz and state estimates at 300–500 Hz, on quadrupeds and "full-sized humanoid bipedal locomotion". MuJoCo's soft contact plus finite differences "transfers well to robot hardware despite obvious model mismatch". Read only via search summaries; the humanoid's identity (H1) comes from secondary summaries; no get-up found — [arXiv 2503.04613](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04613v1)
- Hydrax (V. Kurtz): GPU sampling MPC (predictive sampling, MPPI and others) on MuJoCo MJX with JIT-compiled, vectorised rollouts; its examples include a humanoid standup run with MPPI and online domain randomisation. Read only through a README mirror, not the canonical repository — [Hydrax mirror](https://ithub.global.ssl.fastly.net/based-robotics/hydrax)
- MuJoCo Playground (2025): GPU reinforcement-learning environments where most tasks need "only minutes of training time"; the site lists quadruped and Booster T1 humanoid fall-recovery demos. These are learned policies, and per-task training times were not found (search summary) — [MuJoCo Playground](https://playground.mujoco.org/); [technical report](https://playground.mujoco.org/assets/playground_technical_report.pdf)
- Contact-implicit MPC (Kim et al., IJRR 2025): discovers contact modes online on a 45 kg HOUND quadruped without predefined modes or trajectories; the authors note a broader search can produce "undesired results, such as three-leg walking" (search summary) — [arXiv 2312.08961](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.08961v2)

### Inferences
- **Budget arithmetic (mine, from the published settings).** SMC 2014 simulated up to 25 × 60 = 1,500 steps of 1/30 s per frame. At about 20 fps that is about 30,000 steps per second, or about 1,000 simulated character-seconds per wall-second: roughly 100× real time per logical core at 75–80% utilisation, on a 2013 desktop. C-PBP: 32 × 36 = 1,152 steps per frame at 20–30 fps, about 23,000–35,000 steps per second, or 770–1,150× real time across 6 cores. DIAL-MPC walking: 2,048 × 20 × 50 ≈ 2 million steps per second, about 41,000× real time on a GPU. The project's bench runs one body at roughly 20–35× real time with a game frame between steps ([report](<../../reports/Getting up by its own joints.md>)), which is 30–50× short of a Hämäläinen-class budget for one down unit, before "a few units down at once" multiplies it.
- **Adapts by construction, but only partly.** The rollouts use the actual body, so proportions and strength change what is found without new code. But SMC's heavier body needed more samples, a longer horizon and an extra hand-written goal, and C-PBP's cost contains human constants (pelvis 1.04 m above the feet, feet within 0.8 m). For Wonder Gather every distance and tolerance in the cost would have to be expressed relative to the body itself (its own standing pelvis height, foot length, limb lengths); otherwise a short wide body and a tall thin one are judged against the same human numbers.
- **How strength would enter and show.** As the hard cap per joint group: SMC samples a torque limit per group inside bounds, and the project's cap (limb thickness × strength) would be the upper bound. A weaker body then finds slower, more supported routes, or fails. The difference is visible by construction, but it is mixed with sampling noise, so two identical bodies will also rise differently from try to try. That can blur the "this body rises like this" signature the director wants.
- **Against the director's October 30 faults.** The published systems report stiffness, tremor and noise, "unnecessary joint contortions", snapping to the target pose early, and unnatural fingertip contacts: the same class of fault as "very aggressive twitches with the limbs" and arms bent "like it's broken". Jerk terms, larger N, spline parameterisation and posture costs reduce but do not remove this, and C-PBP shows that lowering noise costs recovery. Online sampling as the primary controller conflicts with the look the director asked for.
- **Failure modes to expect:** lying on the back and wide splits (SMC); myopia on the back (MJPC); exponential sample growth for unstable bodies (FS-MPC); and a non-causal simulator making the sampler "forget" its plan (SMC), which bears directly on PhysX's non-restorable contact state (October notes).
- **Plausible roles.** (a) A short-horizon corrector around a body-relative nominal plan during the risky transfer only, seeded so it never has to discover the whole rise. (b) A selector that scores a handful of candidate strategies by short rollouts (as in Al Borno's tree method). (c) An offline or at-creation discovery tool. All three need a planning model far faster than the bench (Section 6).

### Gaps
- C-PBP's get-up success values (its Figure 10) are only in a figure; the text gives the trend, not the numbers.
- Rajamäki & Hämäläinen 2018 (continuous-control Monte Carlo tree search informed by multiple experts) was not read; whether it includes getting up is unknown.
- Hydrax's humanoid standup and Playground's fall-recovery demos were seen only through mirrors and summaries: no success rates, smoothness data or training times.
- No user study or naturalness rating of any online-MPC get-up was found.
- No modern laptop timing exists for SMC or C-PBP; the 2012 MacBook Pro's 6–10 fps is the only laptop figure.

## 2. Offline trajectory optimisation per body (contact-invariant optimisation, Lin & Huang 2012, Jain, Ye & Liu 2009): time per body, and could a plan be computed at unit creation and then tracked?

### Takeaway
Contact-discovering optimisation finds body-appropriate get-ups for different morphologies without being told how (contact-invariant optimisation rose a wide-torso biped and a short-legged quadruped "with strategies appropriate for each morphology"), but it cost 2–10 minutes per clip in 2012 on simplified physics with infinitely strong limbs, and its output still needs a robust tracker; planning plus a physics filter (Lin & Huang) took about 17 s per rise in 2011 and visibly adapted to bodies with doubled shoulders and longer arms and trunk, but relied on a mocap library and a mocap finish. Seconds-scale per-body optimisation exists only for reduced models with fixed contact templates (Megaro 2015, legged walking), so "compute a plan when the player creates the unit" is realistic as a template-based, reduced-model plan, not as full contact discovery.

### Cited Findings
**Mordatch, Todorov, Popović, SIGGRAPH 2012, "Discovery of complex behaviors through contact-invariant optimization" (CIO; full text, 2012)**
- "The framework is not specific to humans, but can synthesize behaviors for other imagined morphologies." In the results: "We also tested our algorithm with characters of different morphologies, such as a biped with a wide torso and quadruped with short legs. The optimization was successful in getting up, walking and climbing scenarios, with strategies appropriate for each morphology." A trot contact pattern emerged for the quadruped without being specified — [CIO](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- Getting up comes from only a stand task (torso centred between the feet, feet extended, torso vertical) and a lying start; back and stomach starts give different strategies — [CIO](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- "Exactly the same continuation scheme was successful in all of the diverse behaviors"; each stage takes 250–1,000 iterations; "the total time to synthesize each animation clip ranges between 2 to 10 minutes on a quad-core 2.3GHz Intel Xeon machine" — [CIO](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- The physics is simplified (mass concentrated at the root, limbs effectively "infinitely strong", helper contact forces during continuation), so the output is a plan that must be tracked by a separate controller. Its 2015 successor made plans hold on a real robot only by optimising across an ensemble of perturbed models, taking up to 20 min of CPU for a get-up — [October sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>); [Ensemble-CIO](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~todorov/papers/MordatchICRA15.pdf)

**Lin & Huang, The Visual Computer 2012, "Animating rising up from various lying postures and environments" (full text, 2011–2012)**
- Pipeline: a modified RRT-blossom planner in posture space (joint limits, collisions with ground, obstacles and the body itself via sphere-swept volumes) connects the lying posture to the closest key posture in a database of 14 rising motions. "Dynamics filtering" then tracks the smoothed path in ODE 0.11 with velocity-driven control plus virtual actuator control, where an "external" control force "is then converted into internal joint torques" of the limbs in contact — [Lin & Huang 2012](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~wclin/rising/rising_tvcj2012.pdf)
- The physics part ends at sitting or squatting; the method then connects "to a closest squatting-to-standing trajectory in our MOCAP database" — [Lin & Huang 2012](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~wclin/rising/rising_tvcj2012.pdf)
- Time: "The average computational time of motion planning and dynamics filtering with nonoptimized code is about 11.87 seconds and 5.3 seconds, respectively", on a 2.66 GHz Core2 Duo E6750. Planning per example ranged from about 2.6 to 20.5 s in their Table 3; one refinement iteration averages 0.787 s (0.41–4.9 s) — [Lin & Huang 2012](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~wclin/rising/rising_tvcj2012.pdf)
- Body edits: Figure 14 shows "motion variations at the same time frame due to the change of character's body": shoulders twice as wide, arms 1.6× as long, and arms 1.6× with upper body 2.2× as long, with "the mass and inertia of the corresponding body segments ... also changed according to these bone length changes" — [Lin & Huang 2012](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~wclin/rising/rising_tvcj2012.pdf)
- User study: 40 participants (21 with graphics background) rated 25 clips; naturalness 6.29 ± 2.27 for the method against 6.92 ± 2.22 for mocap on a 1–10 scale. Paired comparisons showed both planning and dynamics filtering matter, planning slightly more — [Lin & Huang 2012](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~wclin/rising/rising_tvcj2012.pdf)
- The authors judge contact-state graphs, which "work well on robots", to be "difficult to define ... for human motions rising from various lying postures" — [Lin & Huang 2012](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~wclin/rising/rising_tvcj2012.pdf)

**Jain, Ye & Liu, ACM TOG 2009, "Optimization-based interactive motion synthesis"**
- A responsive character's motion is solved as a constrained optimisation at each time step, with control strategies given as kinematic goals and a synthetic vision sensor — [project page](https://sites.cc.gatech.edu/graphics/projects/Sumit/homepage/projects/phoward/index.html); [SIGGRAPH archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=141568)
- Hämäläinen et al. describe it as "a frame-by-frame QP optimizer augmented with a state machine that breaks movement down into subgoals that can be implemented with a planning horizon of a single frame", with detailed subgoals such as moving a lifted foot so the COM projection lies midway between the feet, which "raises concerns about robustness ... and decreases the probability of creative, emergent movements" — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- No getting-up controller was found in the material read; the conference listing mentions balance, dodge and climb (search summary).

**Seconds-scale optimisation for arbitrary designed bodies**
- Megaro et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2015: an optimisation that produces "stable motions for legged robots of arbitrary designs", using an approximate dynamics model; six creatures with 2, 4 or 5 legs, point or area feet, actuated spines and different proportions. A CMU release says the process takes "a few seconds", where conventional methods need several minutes (press-release figure) — [Disney Research](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2015/11/04/interactive-design-of-3d-printable-robotic-creatures/); [CMU](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/?p=111864)
- Wampler, Popović & Popović, SIGGRAPH 2014: predicts a new animal's gait "from its shape alone" by joint inverse optimisation over a set of animal gaits; described as the first method to synthesise real-world gaits for many new animals with no input other than their shapes (search summary of the project page) — [project page](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/inverse_locomotion)
- Mordatch, de Lasa & Hertzmann, SIGGRAPH 2010: approximates full-body dynamics with a low-dimensional closed-form model so it can re-plan at every time step, then controls the full body to follow; robust over gaps, inclines, stairs, drops and stepping stones without mocap (secondary course summary) — [course summary](https://pages.graphics.cs.wisc.edu/777-S11/2011/01/23/robust-physics-based-locomotion-using-low-dimensional-planning); [SIGGRAPH archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=105693)
- October reference points: SAMCON's get-up reconstruction took 93 s on 80 cores; Al Borno's robust get-up trees took 1–3 desktop days each — [October sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>)

### Inferences
- **Time per body, 2011–2015 hardware:** contact-invariant optimisation 2–10 min per clip; Lin & Huang about 17 s per rise, but only with a mocap library; Megaro "a few seconds" for walking on a reduced model. Modern CPUs are faster, but nothing found shows full contact discovery for a get-up in seconds. So a plan made at unit creation is realistic as (a) a reduced-model, template-based plan (pick among known strategy families and solve their key support states for this body; Section 3), or (b) a background job of minutes. It is computed once per body design and shared by every unit of that design, which suits "many units exist".
- **Morphology visibly changing the strategy** emerges in contact-invariant optimisation without being specified, which is the strongest evidence that a body-driven optimiser makes proportions show in the rise. But its limbs are "infinitely strong" inside the optimiser, so it would not show strength differences. Any per-body plan for Wonder Gather must include each joint's strength cap to make strength visible.
- **A plan is not a controller.** Ensemble-CIO and Al Borno's failed pose trigger (October notes) both say an optimised trajectory must be wrapped in feedback that tolerates being off the plan. Lin & Huang's physics filter is such a tracker, but it tracked one plan per start and finished with mocap, which this project's rule forbids.
- **Whether a few seconds of per-design computation counts as the "training" the director ruled out** ("it shouldn't train a new one that takes a long time in the game after that") is a question for the director, not engineering.

### Gaps
- Whether Jain, Ye & Liu 2009 included a get-up was not confirmed (video not reviewed).
- No 2024–2026 CPU timing for contact-invariant get-up optimisation was found.
- Megaro's "few seconds" comes from a press release; the paper's timing tables were not read.
- Hauser et al. 2008 and Bretl's multi-limbed planners (cited by CIO) were not read in this pass.

## 3. Designed controllers that generalise by construction, multi-contact planning, and static torque feasibility as a strategy chooser

### Takeaway
Coros, Beaudoin & van de Panne 2010 is the strongest evidence that a designed controller works across proportions without retuning: four characters of different proportions, live proportion edits with "instant realization of a suitable controller", only the PD gains scaled by mass (otherwise "large characters ... knees begin to buckle"), real time on one 2010 core; but it covers walking-type skills for bipeds, and torque caps had "no visible impact" there, whereas rising is limited by strength. Robotics supplies the generalising machinery for multi-contact rising: contact-state graphs, posture generators that respect torque limits, and above all the actuation-aware feasible region (Orsolino et al. 2020), computed in under 10 ms per stance, which is exactly the per-body static strength test that could choose kneel versus squat versus more hand use automatically; self-righting energy landscapes add a purely geometric way for body shape to choose the roll.

### Cited Findings
**Coros, Beaudoin & van de Panne, SIGGRAPH 2010, "Generalized biped walking control" (full text, 2010)**
- Abstract: the control "generalizes well across gait parameters, motion styles, character proportions, and a variety of skills. The control is realtime, requires no character-specific or motion-specific tuning, is robust to disturbances, and is simple to compute." It integrates PD tracking, inverted-pendulum foot placement, and Jacobian-transpose gravity and velocity corrections — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); [project page](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/)
- "Figure 5 illustrate[s] the execution of a sequence of commanded gait parameters over four characters of varying proportions and dimensions. No character-specific or style-specific parameter tuning is required." An interface lets a user "interactively edit the dimensions and proportions of a character and immediately see the resulting motions". "Characters can have asymmetric arms and legs, which will introduce asymmetries in the motion even with symmetric controls." The robot character's left arm is 40% longer — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- The one body-dependent setting: "when an edit creates a new character with mass M′, we scale the kp values by M′/M ... Without PD scaling, the first point of failure is typically for large characters whose knees begin to buckle under their weight." — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Strength: "We do not use torque limits in the specific results we show. However, we have experimented with adding torque limits (|τ| ≤ 200 Nm, for hips and knees, |τ| ≤ 100 Nm for all other joints) for our humanoid character and this did not have any visible impact on the motions." — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Inverted-pendulum foot placement "does not require parameter tuning because the relevant parameters are captured by the model" (citing Tsai et al. 2010). "All balance control parameters are kept fixed across all our simulations" — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Cost: ODE at a 0.0005 s step, 37 DOF; "With the exception of a simulated crowd scene, all the simulations run in real time, written as a single-threaded application and run on a 2.4GHz 2-core machine" — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Users with no animation experience created "highly varied characters and motion styles in 5-10 minutes"; style authors included "children as young as 7" — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Limits: "We have not explored extending the method to nonbiped morphologies"; self-intersection between swing and stance legs still occurs; push-recovery style cannot be authored — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- SIMBICON's state-triggered transitions and stance-hip "free variable", virtual model control, and GBWC's "COM moved 40% of the way" trigger are covered in the October notes — [October designed-controller notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/designed_controllers.md>)

**Coros, Karpathy, Jones, Reveret & van de Panne, SIGGRAPH 2011, "Locomotion skills for simulated quadrupeds"**
- One simulated dog performs walk, trot, pace, canter, gallops and leaps plus "sitting, lying down, standing up, and getting up from a fall", compared against a filmed dog — [project page](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2011-TOG-quadruped/index.html)
- The getting-up machinery (pose tracking plus hand-specified virtual forces through planted feet, judged "the least robust ... largely open loop") is described in Jones's 2011 thesis — [October designed-controller notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/designed_controllers.md>); [Jones 2011](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~benjones/ubc_2011_fall_jones_benjamin.pdf)

**Multi-contact planning in robotics**
- Kanehiro et al. (HRP-2/HRP-2P): a contact-state graph searched with A*; the 2007 version matches an arbitrary lying state to the most similar predefined state by Mahalanobis distance, then follows the graph — [October designed-controller notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/designed_controllers.md>); [Kanehiro 2007 record](https://unit.aist.go.jp/jrl-22022/assets/bib/kanehiro_icra_2007.bib)
- Escande, Kheddar & Miossec, Robotics and Autonomous Systems 2013: a contact planner for "underactuated hyper-redundant robots ... for which movement can only be initiated by taking contacts with the environment"; each candidate contact set is checked by a posture generator (a non-linear optimisation) and discarded on failure; the posture generator handles "joint and torque limits" and was tested on HRP-2 and ATLAS (search snippets; full text blocked by a bot check) — [HAL record](https://hal-lirmm.ccsd.cnrs.fr/lirmm-00800829)
- Bretl & Lall 2008: with friction, the statically feasible COM set is a vertical prism over a convex "support region"; using the convex hull of the feet "can be arbitrarily bad" on uneven ground — [October designed-controller notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/designed_controllers.md>); [Bretl & Lall](https://lall.stanford.edu/papers/bretl_eqmcut_ieee_tro_projection_2008_08_01_01/pubdata/entry.pdf)

**Actuation-aware feasible region (Orsolino et al., IEEE T-RO 2020; preprint 2019)**
- Definition: a 2D horizontal convex set of COM projections that are "both statically stable and actuation-consistent". Joint-torque limits become per-limb, pose-dependent wrench polytopes that are intersected with friction pyramids. The region is more conservative than the intersection of the friction-only and actuation-only regions — [Feasible region (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.07999)
- Cost: below 10 ms with four contacts and below 7.5 ms with three for 99.5% of computations (about 15 ms quoted in the introduction); at least 20× faster than the 6D polytope route with three contacts and 50× with four. Used for online COM and foothold planning on HyQ at about 15 Hz (63 ms for 9 candidate footholds) — [Feasible region (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.07999)
- It shrinks with load: under a vertical load growing to −600 N the margin fell from 0.24 m to about 0.06 m and reached zero at about the moment a joint-torque limit was hit; a limb much more retracted than the others also shrinks it — [Feasible region (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.07999)
- Limits: quasi-static only (base velocity near zero); stall torques with no velocity dependence; linearised friction; accurate only near the current configuration; validated mainly on a quadruped, with one non-coplanar HRP-4 example — [Feasible region (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1903.07999)

**Morphology-driven self-righting (Chen Li's lab, Johns Hopkins; search summaries)**
- Self-righting is modelled "as a barrier-crossing transition on a potential energy landscape over its body pitch-roll space", with E = m·g·z_com. Opening the wings changes body shape: it cannot supply enough energy to somersault, but it "reduces the barrier for rolling", and small kinetic energy from leg flailing then completes the roll; transitions are probabilistic — [Othayoth & Li, eLife 2021](https://elifesciences.org/articles/60233.pdf); [RA-L 2020](https://li.me.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020-RAL.pdf)

**Human evidence that strength shifts strategy (from the October report)**
- Children aged 3–6 who rose more hands-free were stronger (grip 6.9 against 4.4 kg) and steadier — [Kuwabara et al. 2013](https://file.scirp.org/Html/31326.html); a slow chair rise needs hip-plus-knee torque of about 1.53 N·m per kg per leg — [Yoshioka et al. 2007](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1929086/); both as cited in the [report](<../../reports/Getting up by its own joints.md>)

### Inferences
- **What "generalises by construction" required in Coros 2010:** every target expressed through the body's own geometry (IK in the limb's own plane, foot placement from an inverted pendulum sized by the body), feedback that reads the body's own state (COM position and velocity), and gains scaled with mass. All three carry over to rising. What does not carry over: walking never needed strength there, whereas rising is limited by strength, so strength has to enter the plan (which stances are possible), not only the tracker.
- **A strategy chooser from static torque feasibility (my design inference, unsourced as a whole).** For each candidate support state in a small library of get-up families (prone: forearms, hands and knees, bear stance, squat, half-kneel; supine: roll to the side, sit with a hand behind), solve the key pose for this body by IK, with contacts at body-relative spots. Compute its feasible region with this body's per-joint caps (milliseconds per stance). Require overlap between consecutive stances' regions for a quasi-static transfer, then rank sequences by margin and effort. Weak legs make the squat's region vanish, so strategies keeping hands on the ground or thigh survive, which matches the human pattern that weaker children use their hands more. Long arms let the bear stance's region overlap the feet's, so walking the hands back works. A heavy head moves the COM forward and favours the bear stance. Where no overlap exists, the transfer has to be dynamic and needs the capture-point gate from the October notes.
- **The energy-landscape step is purely geometric and cheap:** compute the potential-energy barrier for rolling left, right or onto the front for this body's shape and pick the lowest. A round heavy trunk and a long thin one would choose differently, which is a visible, body-driven difference that needs no strength model.
- **Cost and timing:** all of this is milliseconds per stance. It can run once per body design at unit creation and be cached, and again per fall from the actual lying state.

### Gaps
- Escande 2013's timings and torque-limit formulation could not be read (bot-protected server).
- No source applies the feasible region to a whole-body human get-up with knees, forearms and hips as contacts. Adapting it to surface contacts and to PD drives is untested.
- No source tests Coros-style laws on a rising task across proportions.
- The Coros 2011 paper text was not read, so whether the dog's get-up generalised to other bodies is unknown.

## 4. Procedural animation for arbitrary morphologies: Spore, Overgrowth, Rain World, Grow Home, Wobbledogs, and morphology-independent motion

### Takeaway
Spore is the only shipped system built to animate arbitrary player-made bodies: animators author semantics (which body parts, in which body-relative frame: limb length, rest pose, ground, bounding box) and a run-time particle IK specialises each motion to each creature in about 0.2 ms (2008 hardware), with a 90% test pass rate, but it is kinematic, ignores self-collision and lacks a notion of volume. The other procedural games either layer procedural effects on authored key poses (Overgrowth, whose get-up is an authored clip), write custom code per creature (Rain World), or are physics comedy (Wobbledogs); none gets a body up from the floor by its own joints, so what transfers is Spore's semantics layer as a target generator for a physics tracker, not a controller.

### Cited Findings
**Hecker, Raabe, Enslow, DeWeese, Maynard & van Prooijen, SIGGRAPH 2008, "Real-time motion retargeting to highly varied user-created morphologies" (Spore; full text, 2008)**
- Animators work on characters whose skeletons are unknown when the animation is authored. They select bodies by "semantic query": capabilities such as grasper or foot, spatial constraints such as Front/Center/Back relative to the whole character's bounds or to the set of bodies with that capability, and extents such as FrontMost — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)
- "Movement modes" fix the coordinate frame in which a motion is stored. Rest-relative: bodies "move relative to their rest poses". Bounding-box scaling: "Small characters get small curves; large characters get large curves". LimbLength: "large movements on long limbs and small movements on short limbs", where a limb's length is its path length to the nearest spine segment. Ground-relative: "the z-axis is vertical and scaled 0 to 1 from the rest pose to the ground", so every body reaches the ground at 1.0 — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)
- Variants per limb (play on the most suitable grasper) and automatic sagittal mirroring. Gaits: authored parameters for 1–6 feet and procedural ones for 7 or more, normalised foot paths scaled by leg length, duty factor and step trigger per foot, hips moved with the feet; footless creatures crawl on spine "pseudo-feet" — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)
- The particle IK solver is designed for "graceful failure": when goals are outside the workspace, "the character should reach for them instead of mechanically hyperextend". Sub-trees with no animation get a damped pseudo-physical simulation ("Jiggles") — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)
- Cost: "A procedurally animating character with 25 bodies currently takes approximately 0.2ms per frame on a 1.7Ghz Pentium-M", 35% of it in IK. Animations are tested against an ever-changing database of player-made creatures; the "pass rate is ~90%" — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)
- Limits: motions like "hug each other" or "rub your chin" are impossible because "the system has no understanding of volume and boundaries"; intra-character collision is ignored; IK singularities can make animations "twist unrealistically". "Animators will need to be able to express much more complex and subtle semantics to the system before the animations will generalize in way that's competitive with a Maya animation on a single fixed character." — [Hecker et al. 2008](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/edu/year/2011/course/TDA361/Advanced%20Computer%20Graphics/027-hecker%20copy.pdf)
- A later assessment says the Spore system handles a limited set of clearly annotated topologies with predefined mappings (search summary) — [Abdul-Massih et al. 2017](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/bbenes/papers/Abdul17CGF.pdf)

**Overgrowth (Wolfire; David Rosen, GDC 2014, "An Indie Approach to Procedural Animation")**
- The talk shows how simple procedural techniques give fluid, interactive animation from very few keyframes; commentators describe keyed poses with spring-like interpolation and procedural layers on top (secondary commentary; slides and transcript not read) — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-an-indie-approach-to-procedural-animation); [Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2093086); [jMonkeyEngine forum](https://hub.jmonkeyengine.org/t/dynamic-procedural-character-animation-system/29460)
- Its get-up is an authored "stand from front/back" clip blended from the ragdoll's last pose after a recovery timer — [Overgrowth source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WolfireGames/overgrowth/main/Data/Scripts/aschar.as), as read in the [report](<../../reports/Getting up by its own joints.md>)

**Rain World**
- Creatures share base scripts, but the designs "require unique code to form their bodies and handle their unique ways of movement". The slugcat is "two spherical chunks locked to each other at a fixed distance", with limbs and tail as code-driven "cosmetic additions". Jakobsson "never really set out to do procedural animation" — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/exploring-procedural-design-rain-world)
- A GDC 2016 Animation Bootcamp session by Joar Jakobsson and James Therrien covered the animation process (listing only) — [GameAnim listing](https://www.gameanim.com/category/learn/presentations/page/7/)

**Grow Home (Ubisoft Reflections, 2015)**
- It began as a physics programmer's experiment in procedural animation; BUD's simplified body has gaps where the joints would be (Wikipedia); physics moves the joints under gravity while IK pulls them into a stance (secondary article) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_Home); [Alan Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2017/04/17/procedural-animations/)

**Wobbledogs (Animal Uprising)**
- The dogs are "physically simulated all the way down to their guts" and mutate through a gut-flora genetics system (developer store text). A fan guide says extra limbs can make a dog worse at walking. No technical description of the locomotion was found — [store page](https://store.playsum.live/product/974b8a41-2b67-46e4-8a9f-28ee198b22c5/wobbledogs); [Kill Screen](https://www.killscreen.com/wobbledogs-even-ridiculous-sounds/); [fan guide](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/how-to-breed-the-optimal-wobbledog-a-guide-to-simulated-genetic-chaos)

**Morphology-independent motion outside games**
- Wampler et al. 2014 (gait style predicted from shape), contact-invariant optimisation 2012 (strategies per morphology) and Coros et al. 2010 (proportions edited live): see Sections 2 and 3. Won & Lee 2019 (one learned controller across body shapes): see Section 5.

### Inferences
- **Spore's principle carries over to physics.** Author each get-up stage once as semantics in body-relative frames, for example "hand ground-relative 1.0 under the shoulder", "foot at a fraction of leg length ahead of the hip", "trunk pitched until the COM is over the support", then specialise per body at run time by IK and let physics track it. LimbLength and bounding-box scaling are exactly the director's "proportions have a real natural impact", achieved without per-body training.
- **Spore's failure rules bear on the director's "broken arm" fault.** It deliberately reaches toward unreachable goals instead of hyperextending, and its unsolved self-collision and IK singularities "twist unrealistically". A target generator for arbitrary bodies needs explicit joint-comfort limits and self-collision awareness, which Spore lacked.
- **What the game examples show:** procedural animation for many bodies is solved kinematically (Spore) or by hand per creature (Rain World); physics-first procedural bodies in shipped games read as comedy (Wobbledogs). None is a precedent for a grounded, physics-only rise.

### Gaps
- Overgrowth's talk slides and Grow Home's technical details were not available; the descriptions above are secondary.
- No technical source on Wobbledogs' locomotion was found.
- Spore's gait style blending across leg counts was not examined beyond the paper's summary.

## 5. Hybrids: a body-relative plan followed by a physics tracker — evidence across bodies, naturalness, and how the "planned pose then switch" trap is avoided

### Takeaway
The best-supported direction for "any body, natural" combines a plan computed from the body's own geometry and strength (contacts, key support states, strategy) with continuous physics feedback (low-gain PD with gravity compensation, Jacobian-transpose COM and contact forces, momentum-gated releases), optionally with a short sampling corrector; the evidence is partial: Lin & Huang (planner plus physics filter, four body edits, rated 6.29 against mocap's 6.92, mocap finish), contact-invariant optimisation (strategy per morphology, offline), Coros 2010 (walking across proportions), and Won & Lee 2019 (one learned controller across body shapes from 0.35× to 1.8× per segment, walking only, 20.7% of random lower-body bipeds falling). The trap is avoided when plans act as proposals to feedback rather than gates, targets are recomputed from the measured state, progress is triggered by state with overlapping pre- and post-conditions, and several strategies stay alive; no published get-up yet demonstrates this across a continuous space of bodies with a measured success rate.

### Cited Findings
**Evidence that plan-plus-tracker adapts to bodies**
- Lin & Huang 2012: a planned path tracked in physics (velocity-driven control plus virtual actuators mapped to joint torques) produces different motions for bodies with 2× shoulders, 1.6× arms, and 1.6× arms with a 2.2× upper body, mass and inertia scaled; naturalness 6.29 ± 2.27 against mocap's 6.92 ± 2.22; about 11.9 s planning plus 5.3 s filtering per rise on 2011 hardware; final squat-to-stand from mocap — [Lin & Huang 2012](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~wclin/rising/rising_tvcj2012.pdf)
- Coros et al. 2010: a motion generator (targets) tracked by PD with gravity compensation, inverted-pendulum foot placement and Jacobian-transpose velocity control generalises over four characters of differing proportions with only mass-scaled gains — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf)
- Contact-invariant optimisation 2012: plans per morphology with "strategies appropriate for each morphology", which still need tracking in full physics — [CIO](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- Mordatch, de Lasa & Hertzmann 2010: a low-dimensional plan re-made every time step and tracked by the full body (secondary summary) — [course summary](https://pages.graphics.cs.wisc.edu/777-S11/2011/01/23/robust-physics-based-locomotion-using-low-dimensional-planning)
- Kenwright 2023: a point-mass plan that walks the hands back until the COM is over the feet, tracked by PD and IK; qualitative only — [October designed-controller notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/designed_controllers.md>); [Kenwright 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.05056)
- SMC 2014: learned predictions added as extra samples improved success at low budgets — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf); MJPC recommends a learned proposal policy or value function against myopia — [October sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>)

**A learned tracker trained once across bodies (for comparison; Won & Lee, SIGGRAPH Asia 2019, full text)**
- One PPO controller with body-shape parameters: global scale 0.5–1.5 and per-segment scale 0.7–1.2, so a segment ranges from 0.35× to 1.8× (about a 5× ratio); base character 1.7 m, 55 kg, 19 segments. New characters "can be controlled immediately without any re-training process" — [MorphCon paper](https://mrl.snu.ac.kr/publications/ProjectMorphCon/MorphCon.pdf); [project page](https://mrl.snu.ac.kr/publications/ProjectMorphCon/MorphCon.html)
- Cost: "we usually spent 2-4 days to get satisfying results where 50-100M transition tuples were generated" — [MorphCon paper](https://mrl.snu.ac.kr/publications/ProjectMorphCon/MorphCon.pdf)
- Skills: walking-type locomotion following simple sinusoidal reference keyframes (bipeds, quadrupeds; underwater animals on the project page); no getting up — [MorphCon paper](https://mrl.snu.ac.kr/publications/ProjectMorphCon/MorphCon.pdf)
- Robustness over 300 random shapes, falls within 30 s: lower-body biped 20.7% with reward normalisation (54.1% without); quadruped 9.3% (11.1% without) — [MorphCon paper](https://mrl.snu.ac.kr/publications/ProjectMorphCon/MorphCon.pdf)
- Limits: "the representation power of the network-based policy is often insufficient for learning a full spectrum of body shape variations"; some shapes walk only with a dedicated controller; "inherently uncontrollable characters" in the parameter space cause problems — [MorphCon paper](https://mrl.snu.ac.kr/publications/ProjectMorphCon/MorphCon.pdf)

**The "planned pose then switch" trap and how sources avoid it**
- The trap: "The character never exactly reaches the designated target pose and the resulting errors typically make the controllers fail" — [Al Borno et al. 2017](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf), via the [October sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>)
- Variants that still work in narrow settings: Kanehiro 2007 (nearest stored state, then a statically stable robot routine) — [October designed-controller notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/designed_controllers.md>); Lin & Huang (nearest database posture, then mocap) — [Lin & Huang 2012](https://people.cs.nycu.edu.tw/~wclin/rising/rising_tvcj2012.pdf)
- A target pose used as a prior in sampling makes the character "assume the target pose prematurely while still moving. Sometimes this appears almost unphysical" — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- Ways out found in the sources: re-planning from the measured state every frame (MPC) — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf); targets computed from the measured state, such as inverted-pendulum foot placement — [GBWC paper](http://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-gbwc/paper.pdf); state-triggered progress with matching pre- and post-conditions and failure monitors (Faloutsos, Jones, Coros) — [October designed-controller notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/designed_controllers.md>); many branches chosen by short rollouts (Al Borno) — [October sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>)

### Inferences
- **An architecture these findings support (my synthesis, untested):**
  1. *At unit creation, once per body design:* specialise the body-relative get-up stages (Section 4) to this body by IK; score each stage's static feasibility with this body's joint caps (Section 3); compute roll barriers from the body's shape; rank strategy families; cache a small table (key support states, margins, which transfers must be dynamic).
  2. *At each fall:* pick the strategy from the actual lying state and the cached table; regenerate targets every frame from the measured state (COM, support polygon, contact loads), never as a fixed pose to reach.
  3. *Tracker:* PD drives with gravity compensation and gains scaled by mass and strength (Coros scaled by mass; strength caps stay the joint limits); Jacobian-transpose COM forces and contact-force sharing; capture-point gates for releases with weights ramped, not switched (the director's October 13 rule); a failure monitor that backs off and retries.
  4. *Optional:* a short sampling corrector for the one dynamic transfer, running in a fast planning model (Section 6).
- **Where proportions and strength show:** in which strategy wins (stances that exist for this body), where hands and feet land (body-relative frames), how long each supported phase lasts (a weaker body needs more margin and moves slower), and how the trunk and head counterbalance. All of these come from the body's own numbers, not from a body-specific clip.
- **Evidence quality:** no hybrid get-up across many bodies with a success rate exists. The closest are Lin & Huang (four edits, mocap finish), contact-invariant optimisation (two non-human bodies, offline), Coros 2010 (walking) and Won & Lee (walking, learned). The hybrid is the best-supported direction, not a demonstrated solution.
- **Naturalness levers the sources point to:** low-gain compliant tracking made possible by gravity compensation (Coros), smoothness and jerk costs (SMC), smooth body-scaled targets, and keeping sampling noise out of the primary loop (C-PBP, SMC).

### Gaps
- No published get-up generalises across a continuous space of body proportions and strengths with a measured success rate.
- No naturalness study compares a planned-plus-tracked get-up with an MPC or learned one.
- Won & Lee report fall rates only for walking; nothing shows whether shape-conditioned learning extends to getting up.

## 6. Unity/PhysX feasibility: planning scenes and Simulate cost, ArticulationBody vs ConfigurableJoint, the MuJoCo Unity plug-in as a planning model, determinism

### Takeaway
Rollouts in Unity are mechanically possible (scene-local physics stepped by script), but no official cost figures exist; one user measured a second physics scene adding about 0.5 ms and suspected the scenes step one after another, and the project's own 20–35× real time per body is 30–50× short of what Hämäläinen-class planning used per character, while the 2014–2015 sampling-MPC authors rejected iterative solvers (PhysX among them, at that time) as unstable for active characters except at tiny steps, and needed a deterministic simulator. MuJoCo is Apache-2.0 source with a Unity plug-in that runs MuJoCo inside Unity and could act as a fast, deterministic planning twin of each player body, but the plug-in requires bodies and joints to be rebuilt in MuJoCo terms, lacks some features, and its documentation states neither licence nor maintenance status; separately, Unity's manual says maximal-coordinate joints (such as ConfigurableJoint) only satisfy constraints if the solver converges, which can produce "stuttery and unrealistic motion", whereas articulations make locked axes unbreakable.

### Cited Findings
**Multiple physics scenes and Simulate cost**
- A Unity forum user measured about 1.2 ms of frame time with one physics scene and about 1.7 ms with two, and suspected the scenes are stepped sequentially; no official answer on parallel scenes appeared in the results — [Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/multiscene-physics-parallelization/1694162)
- Extra scenes are stepped manually; Unity's 2018.3 post suggests invisible physics scenes for trajectory prediction and notes they can be simulated at any frequency — [Unity 2018.3 physics blog](https://blog.unity.com/cn/technology/physics-changes-in-unity-2018-3-beta); queries such as raycasts must use the scene's own methods — [Unity Learn](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/multi-scene-physics)
- `LocalPhysicsMode`, fixed-step `PhysicsScene.Simulate`, `FixedUpdate` not running during manual steps, ArticulationBody state get/set, PhysX same-scene-only determinism, non-serialisable contact state, and Enhanced Determinism are documented in the October notes — [October sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>); [PhysX determinism](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.1/docs/RigidBodyDynamics.html); [Isaac Sim PhysX limitations](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.1.0/physics/physics_resources.html)
- PhysX version: Unity moved to PhysX 4.1 in 2019.3 (per a search summary of Unity's beta notes), and forum users report 4.x in 2021.2; Unity 6's exact PhysX version was not confirmed — [Unity forum](https://forum.unity.com/threads/how-soon-physx-5-0-support-will-be-added-to-unity.797652/)

**ArticulationBody vs regular joints (ConfigurableJoint)**
- Unity 6 manual: in an articulation, "All locked degrees of freedom ... are unbreakable and unstretchable by design. To achieve this, the physics engine uses the reduced coordinate space". With regular joints "the physics engine enforces the maximal coordinate space, which only guarantees the satisfaction of constraints if the solver is able to converge after a set of iterations", and in kinematic chains it "might encounter unsolvable scenarios and leave certain constraints unsatisfied. This might result in stuttery and unrealistic motion." Articulations have one root, no kinematic loops, and a maximum hierarchy depth of 64; ragdolls are among their listed uses — [Unity manual: physics articulations](https://docs.unity.com/en-us/engine/6000.0/manual/physics-section/physics-overview/articulations-section/physics-articulations)
- Ragdoll stability guidance for joints: enable projection; raise solver iterations to 10–20 (and velocity iterations similarly for bounce); avoid mass ratios of about 10× between connected bodies; keep angular limits at least 5–15° or lock the axis; do not set transforms of jointed kinematic bodies directly (search summary of Unity's page) — [Unity manual: ragdoll stability](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/RagdollStability.html)
- Cost: PhysX calls articulations "more expensive than regular joints" — [PhysX best practices](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.1/docs/BestPractices.html); a Unity post says the older maximal-coordinate physics was "more performant for general use cases" but sacrificed accuracy — [Unity blog](https://blog.unity.com/es/industry/use-articulation-bodies-to-easily-prototype-industrial-designs-with-realistic-motion-and); an older NVIDIA forum reply says articulations are not GPU-accelerated — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/articulations-and-cuda/38258). No benchmark of per-step cost for either joint type was found.

**What sampling-MPC authors needed from an engine**
- Iterative solvers in "ODE, Bullet Physics or PhysX engines were not stable enough for active characters except at very small time steps that were not computationally efficient" (2014); a non-causal simulator makes the sampler forget its chosen strategy — [SMC 2014](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/OnlineSMC/preprint.pdf)
- C-PBP's iterative solver needed steps below 1/100 s; the direct solver ran at 1/30 s — [C-PBP](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)

**MuJoCo as a planning model inside Unity**
- The plug-in lets the Unity Editor and runtime use MuJoCo (Windows, macOS, Linux, with a native library); MJCF elements map one-to-one to Unity components; each `MjScene.FixedUpdate()` calls `mj_step` and syncs GameObjects; adding or removing MuJoCo components recreates the whole scene, which "can be expensive"; it is not a drop-in replacement: "Rigidbody, ArticulationBody and Joint configurations still need to be recreated manually"; MuJoCo plugins, mjSpec editing and some sensors are unsupported; skewed capsules cannot collide; an external process can drive the simulation. The page says nothing about licence, maintenance or running several models for planning — [MuJoCo Unity plug-in docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/unity.html)
- MuJoCo's source code is under the Apache License 2.0 (documentation CC BY 4.0), and the repository lists "C# bindings and Unity plug-in" (search summary of the README) — [MuJoCo GitHub](https://github.com/deepmind/mujoco)
- Speed: MJPC states the humanoid "can be simulated 4000 times faster than realtime on a single CPU thread" — [MJPC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541); MuJoCo ships a `testspeed` tool, and DeepMind's published humanoid benchmark figures are only in an image — [MuJoCo samples](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/programming/samples.html); [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/open-sourcing-mujoco/)
- Planning on a model that differs from the plant has worked: Tassa deliberately planned with a different model from the simulated plant — [October sampling notes](<../Getting up by its own joints/sampling_mpc_optimisation.md>); MuJoCo iLQR transferred to real robots "despite obvious model mismatch" — [arXiv 2503.04613](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04613v1); DIAL-MPC tolerated a 2 kg base-mass mismatch — [DIAL-MPC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2409.15610)

### Inferences
- **Planning in PhysX:** at 20–35× real time per body on the bench, against about 1,000× per down unit for Hämäläinen-class sampling, online sampling in PhysX is not viable even for one unit on a laptop unless a clean measurement comes out far better than the bench implies. A minimal MJPC-style corrector (about 50–100× by the October estimate) is borderline for one unit and only during the brief risky transfer.
- **A MuJoCo planning twin:** at MJPC's 4,000× per thread (not verified for a 15-part body), a 1,000× budget is about a quarter of a core per down unit, so "a few units down" fits in about one core. Costs: an MJCF twin generated for each body design at creation (feasible, since bodies are procedurally defined); accepting model mismatch, which MPC tolerates by re-planning; and the plug-in's unknowns (licence wording, maintenance, Unity 6 support). It would also supply the deterministic, "causal" stepping that SMC needed and PhysX cannot guarantee for restored states.
- **Joint type and the October 30 glitches:** maximal-coordinate ConfigurableJoints can leave constraints unsatisfied under stress (Unity manual), a plausible contributor to "twitch like for one millisecond completely out of frame ... clipping and bugging out the limb". That is unproven and needs frame-by-frame capture. The documented mitigations are projection, more solver iterations, limited mass ratios, wider angular limits, or articulations with unbreakable locked axes. The October notes describe the miner as an ArticulationBody, while this brief describes ConfigurableJoints, so which one the current body uses should be checked first.
- **Cost per joint type** must be measured in the project: about 15 bodies, both joint types, 1 to N clones per scene, with and without the game frame.

### Gaps
- No Unity 6 benchmark exists for `PhysicsScene.Simulate` per scene, per articulation, or per ConfigurableJoint ragdoll.
- Unity 6's PhysX version, and whether its TGS solver and articulations overturn the 2014 verdict on iterative solvers for active characters, are unconfirmed.
- The MuJoCo Unity plug-in's licence file, maintenance status and Unity 6 compatibility are not stated on its documentation page.
- No text-form single-thread steps-per-second figure for a MuJoCo humanoid was found; the 4,000× figure is MJPC's claim.
