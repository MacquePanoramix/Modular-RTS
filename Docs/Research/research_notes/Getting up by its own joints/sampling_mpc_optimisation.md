# Sampling-based control, online MPC and trajectory optimisation for a simulated character getting up

Scope: SAMCON and its successors, online predictive control (MuJoCo MPC, Tassa et al. 2012, Hämäläinen et al. 2014/2015, MPPI/CEM), contact-invariant optimisation and trajectory optimisation with feedback (including Domain-of-Attraction expansion), and whether any of it can run in Unity 6 PhysX ArticulationBody, either in real time or offline. The project context is a 40 kg, 13-part ArticulationBody miner. Its current open-loop key-pose sequence, found by a CEM-like search, stands in 1 to 8 of 36 tries, and the count changes from run to run.

Reading notes: I read these papers in full text (PDF to text): SAMCON 2010, Improved SAMCON 2015, Guided Learning of Control Graphs 2016, Learning to Schedule Control Fragments 2017, MuJoCo MPC 2022, Tassa/Erez/Todorov 2012, Mordatch/Todorov/Popović 2012 (CIO), and Al Borno/van de Panne/Fiume 2017 (DOA expansion). The Aalto server hosting Hämäläinen et al. 2014 and 2015 refused every connection (TLS/empty reply), so for those two papers **only the abstracts were readable**, plus what other papers report about them (chiefly the DOA expansion paper, which reimplemented the 2014 method and measured it). Ensemble-CIO (2015) was read only through a search engine's summary of its PDF, because the direct link redirects. Terrain Runner (2012) and Ding et al. (2015) were read only from their project pages.

## 1. SAMCON and its successors: how do they get robustness, and do any of them get up?

### Takeaway
SAMCON itself produces **open-loop** controls: PD target offsets sampled every 0.1 s and kept along the best path. The authors say plainly that these are not robust to perturbations, and its get-up was its hardest case. That is the same family as the project's current key-pose search, and it fails in the same way. Robustness came later from two additions. The first was **time-indexed linear feedback per 0.1 s control fragment, learned by regression over many SAMCON solutions in an iterated "guided" loop** (Liu, van de Panne, Yin 2016). That graph includes prone and supine get-up skills, run in real time. The second was a learned scheduler that reorders fragments (Liu & Hodgins 2017).

### Cited Findings
**SAMCON (Liu, Yin, van de Panne, Shao, Xu, SIGGRAPH 2010)**
- Method: control is a target pose for PD servos. At each 0.1 s "sampling time step" the algorithm draws nSample random offsets to the target pose within per-DoF windows, simulates each forward, scores the end state against the reference motion, keeps nSave samples (not just the best, to be "more far-sighted" and robust to local minima), and finally backtracks the lowest-cost path. Simulation time step 0.0005 s in ODE 0.11; friction 0.8 — [SAMCON 2010 PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- Reconstructed motions include walk, run, sideways/forward/backward rolls, a "get-up" (3.5 s, 40 iterations, 93 s reconstruction) and a "kip-up" (6.6 s, 184 s) on an 80-core cluster with nSample = 1400 and nSave = 200. Cost weights were the same set for most motions, and the get-up used extra contact scales on shoulders, elbows and wrists — [SAMCON 2010](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- **Get-up was the hardest case:** "the character fails to stand up two thirds of the time with nSample = 1400 and nSave = 200 … the failure rate decreases to one fourth if we use nSample = 4000 and nSave = 400." The mocap subject "just made it." These figures are offline reconstruction success rates, not run-time robustness — [SAMCON 2010](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- **Open-loop, not robust:** "our reconstructed controls are open-loop in nature … When starting from a state not on the canonical trajectory … the virtual character is unlikely to follow [it]." The discussion adds that the controls "are not robust with respect to external perturbations or environmental changes", and that dense state-to-action policies are "likely difficult because of the existence of a myriad of local minima caused by many fast-changing contacts" — [SAMCON 2010](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)
- **Its interactive demo cheats by the project's rule.** After a push it switches to the nearest "cut point" of another controller and "kinematically blend[s] the end state … into the new canonical trajectory". Otherwise a semi-ragdoll falls, and "PD control is then used to drive the character close to the canonical trajectory of the chosen get-up controller" — [SAMCON 2010](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf)

**Improved SAMCON (Liu, Yin, Guo, Eurographics 2015)**
- Adds sample-distribution adaptation (a modified CMA-ES that learns from the elite samples of failed trials), a sliding window, and averaging of elite samples to reduce noise. **The output is still open-loop control** — [Improved SAMCON PDF](http://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/libinliu/Samcon2/Samcon2_EG.pdf)
- "Sampling-based methods are inherently noisy." Smoothing the control trajectory afterwards "results in physically invalid controls", and the sampling noise causes "excessive vibration and jerkiness in the simulated motion", which averaging reduces. Example cost: the 54 s "Southern Fist" with Ns = 2000 took 188 trials in 3 hours on a cluster — [Improved SAMCON](http://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/libinliu/Samcon2/Samcon2_EG.pdf)
- Future work named there: "building closed-loop controls on top of the output" — [Improved SAMCON](http://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/libinliu/Samcon2/Samcon2_EG.pdf)

**Terrain Runner (Liu, Yin, van de Panne, Guo, SIGGRAPH Asia 2012)** (project page only)
- Learns "reduced-order linear feedback control laws" around one mocap example per skill (run, jump, speed-vault, drop-roll). It then parameterises them by continuation (an RBF predictor-corrector) and composes transitions, with a step-based online planner — [Terrain Runner project page](http://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/libinliu/Parkour/Parkour.html)
- Related: Ding, Liu, van de Panne, Yin (SCA 2015) learn low-dimensional linear output-feedback matrices by policy search, for balancing, walking, running, rolling, kicks and ball hitting. Getting up is not listed — [Reduced-order feedback project page](https://cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2015-SCA-reducedOrder/index.html)

**Guided Learning of Control Graphs (Liu, van de Panne, Yin, ACM TOG 2016)**
- Each skill is cut into control fragments of about 0.1 s. Each fragment has an affine feedback policy: at the start of the fragment it maps a fixed set of state features to a constant offset on the reference PD targets, and PD servos then produce the torques. "Linear feedback control is applied once at the beginning of each control fragment" — [Control Graphs PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf)
- Learning: SAMCON serves as a "control oracle" producing state-action pairs, and linear regression gives the policy. Regression on plain SAMCON results fails, because the policy drives the character into states far from the data. So **guided SAMCON** starts each new search from the current policy (an EM-like loop). "None of the motion skills can acquire robust feedback policies with only one iteration"; up to 20 iterations were used — [Control Graphs](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf)
- **Get-up results:** the Bollywood graph includes "Prone Get Up" and "Supine Get Up", and the gymnastics graph a "Kip Up" and "Get Up". The "rising skills … will be automatically executed when the character falls". After a fall, "a ragdoll controller that tracks the starting pose of the target rising skill" runs until the pose difference is small enough. Then the rising skill is activated. Falls are detected when the magnitude of the feedback action exceeds a threshold — [Control Graphs](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf)
- Robustness: "a random walk on the graph can always succeed when no perturbations are applied". Two characters repeatedly collide, fall and get up in real time, and four characters are shown simulated simultaneously. The paper concludes that "sequences of linear feedback policies … do well at controlling many skills, including … rising skills" — [Control Graphs](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf)
- Push robustness is measured only for cyclic skills, for example a dance-spin surviving 50 N and 150 N pushes of 0.2 s after 20 iterations. No get-up success rate is reported — [Control Graphs](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf)
- Cost: ODE 0.12 with implicit damping, single-threaded at 10x real time on an Intel Core i5 2.67 GHz. The open-loop SAMCON pass took "as few as ten minutes" for get-up and kip-up. Guided learning takes up to about 1 hour per second of skill, and roughly 1 to 2 hours per second of reference including manual work, on a 20-core cluster. The two graphs (40 s of reference) took about two full days — [Control Graphs](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf)
- Limits stated by the authors: policies are time-indexed, which makes them "less flexible", and they assume no multi-modal responses. Different runs give different policies, which suggests a low-rank structure. A fragment length of 0.05 to 0.2 s works, while longer fragments become "insufficiently flexible" — [Control Graphs](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf)

**Learning to Schedule Control Fragments (Liu & Hodgins, ACM TOG 2017)**
- A deep Q-network reorders SAMCON/guided-learning control fragments at run time, for ball walking, bongo board, skateboarding, push recovery and breakdancing. The simulation pipeline runs at 30x real time on a desktop — [Scheduler PDF](http://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/libinliu/Scheduler/SchedulerTOG.pdf)
- After a breakdance fall, "the character struggles for a few seconds before it is able to stand up … This sequence of actions is not natural looking." The fix was 35 sitting-up fragments added to a hierarchical scheduler, so the character "can sit up immediately after falling" — [Scheduler](http://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/libinliu/Scheduler/SchedulerTOG.pdf)
- The bongo-board feet were pinned to the board with a joint. That is a hidden constraint, which the project's rule would forbid — [Scheduler](http://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/libinliu/Scheduler/SchedulerTOG.pdf)

### Inferences
- The project's current method (a CEM search over key-pose parameters, scored over many starts, executed open-loop with some feedback) is close to SAMCON 2010 plus CMA-style adaptation (Improved SAMCON). This literature says that family yields small domains of attraction. Chaotic counts from try to try are the expected symptom, not a tuning accident.
- The proven route from SAMCON to robustness is to **close the loop at roughly 0.1 s granularity**: per-fragment affine feedback on a small set of state features (root orientation and velocity, CoM relative to support, a few joint states), learned by regression from many perturbed sampled solutions, and iterated so that the data comes from states the policy actually visits. The project already has the bench (a fast batch simulator) that this needs.
- The control-graph get-up uses "ragdoll tracks the starting pose, then trigger". The DOA paper (Section 4) reports that this exact heuristic failed with its controllers, because the body never reaches the starting pose exactly. So a learned feedback policy, or a set of start-state-specific controllers, is what makes this work, not the trigger.
- Everything here keeps the body responsible (PD servos at the joints, unactuated root), **except** SAMCON 2010's kinematic blending at transitions and Liu & Hodgins's pinned feet. Those must not be copied.

### Gaps
- No paper in the SAMCON line reports a quantitative success rate for get-up from varied lying states (only reconstruction success, and qualitative demos).
- The Terrain Runner and Ding 2015 PDFs were not read, so how many feedback parameters they learn, and how they regress them, is unconfirmed here.
- The state features used in Control Graphs 2016 were only partly visible in the text extraction.

## 2. Online predictive sampling and MPC: MuJoCo MPC, Tassa 2012, Hämäläinen 2014/2015, MPPI/CEM

### Takeaway
Online MPC can make a simulated humanoid get up from arbitrary lying states in or near real time with only joint actuation, from simple cost functions. MuJoCo MPC's humanoid "stand" task does it, with planning updates of 1 to 20 ms per step on a fast multi-core CPU. But every success depends on (a) a simulator that runs thousands of times faster than real time and (b) generous torques. Tassa's iLQG get-up "gets up with a single bounce", and an independent group reports both Tassa's method and Hämäläinen's SMC failing from some prone and supine postures under realistic torque limits. Naturalness is not evaluated in these papers, and cost shaping is everything.

### Cited Findings
**MuJoCo MPC (Howell, Gileadi, Tunyasuvunakool, Zakka, Erez, Tassa, arXiv 2212.00541, 2022)**
- Planners: iLQG (a Gauss-Newton DDP that gives time-varying linear feedback; "Equivalently, iLQR"), gradient descent on spline parameters (Pontryagin), and **Predictive Sampling**. All are in C++ with multi-threaded parallel rollouts and an asynchronous planner/agent design — [MJPC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541)
- Predictive Sampling: a nominal action sequence is held as spline parameters. Each iteration evaluates N candidates (the nominal plus N−1 Gaussian perturbations with fixed σ), clamps them to the control limits, and keeps the best. The paper calls it "MPPI with infinite temperature", "CEM with a non-adaptive distribution" or "trivial random search", and presents it "not because it is good, but because it is not good", as a lower-bound baseline that "turned out to be surprisingly competitive" — [MJPC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541)
- Humanoid settings for Predictive Sampling (Table 1): P = 3, N = 10, T = 23, σ = 0.125. Read via ar5iv, these are 10 rollouts per iteration, a horizon of 23 steps and noise 0.125 — [MJPC on ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.00541)
- **Humanoid get-up:** the 27-DoF DeepMind Control Suite humanoid with 21 actuators "can be initialised on the floor and quickly stands in a manner that is robust to large disturbances. If a sufficiently large disturbance knocks the humanoid onto the floor, the system will stand back up." The A1 quadruped "is also capable of rolling off its back and standing up" — [MJPC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541)
- Humanoid cost (6 terms): lateral CoM over the mean of the feet (cosh, weight 100); torso over CoM laterally (smooth-abs, 1); head height minus feet height versus a target (smooth-abs, 100); lateral CoM velocity (smooth-abs, 10); joint velocity (quadratic, 0.01); control effort (quadratic, 0.025) — [MJPC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541)
- Compute: "the total planning time for a single update is between 1 and 20 milliseconds" for all examples. The humanoid "can be simulated 4000 times faster than realtime on a single CPU thread". The machines were a 72-core Xeon Gold 6154 workstation and an M1 Pro laptop — [MJPC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.00541)
- Why crude sampling works in MPC: warm-starting amortises optimisation over steps, and "it usually makes no sense to take more than one step of optimisation". MPC is "more like surfing than mountain climbing … The goal is not to find the minimum, but to remain in the basin-of-attraction" — [MJPC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541)
- Stated limits: "one can only control what can be simulated much faster than real-time". Predictive control "is myopic and cannot see past the fixed horizon". Suggested remedies are a learned proposal policy rolled out alongside the samples, or a learned value function at the end of the rollout. The repository says MJPC is "not production-quality software" — [MJPC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541); [MJPC GitHub](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_mpc)
- The repository ships humanoid tasks `interact`, `stand`, `tracking` and `walk` — [MJPC humanoid tasks](https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco_mpc/tree/main/mjpc/tasks/humanoid)

**Tassa, Erez, Todorov, IROS 2012, "Synthesis and stabilization of complex behaviors through online trajectory optimization"**
- iLQG MPC running on MuJoCo (built for this purpose). A 22-DoF humanoid, 1.6 m and 55 kg, gets up "from an arbitrary pose on the ground" and recovers from large disturbances, computed "only 7 x slower than real time, on a standard PC" — [Tassa 2012 PDF](https://02465material.pages.compute.dtu.dk/02465public/_assets/tassa2012.pdf)
- Cost: horizontal CoM to the mean of the feet, torso to CoM horizontally, torso height versus 1.3 m above the feet (all smooth-abs), and a quadratic penalty on CoM velocity. The control cost is a cosh-shaped soft limit. "It was not difficult to find a cost which would promote getting-up." The horizon is 500 ms: shorter degraded performance, and longer gave no qualitative gain — [Tassa 2012](https://02465material.pages.compute.dtu.dk/02465public/_assets/tassa2012.pdf)
- The plant and the planner model deliberately differ: a 2 ms plant step against an 8 ms controller step, and stiffer contact in the plant. "Performance was qualitatively similar for wide range of parameters". The sequences shown "were not exceptional or carefully selected" — [Tassa 2012](https://02465material.pages.compute.dtu.dk/02465public/_assets/tassa2012.pdf)
- The cost driver is finite-difference derivatives. For 20 DoF and a 50-step horizon that is "4,000 dynamics evaluations per time step", so "the physics engine must run at least 4,000 faster than real-time" — [Tassa 2012](https://02465material.pages.compute.dtu.dk/02465public/_assets/tassa2012.pdf)
- Independent critique: Tassa's character "gets up with a single bounce, implying that large torques are at play", and "the character fails to get up with the iLQG method when more conservative torques limits are used, e.g., ±300 Nm" — [Al Borno et al. 2017, DOA expansion](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)

**Hämäläinen, Eriksson, Tanskanen, Kyrki, Lehtinen, SIGGRAPH 2014, "Online motion synthesis using sequential Monte Carlo"** (abstract only; full text unreachable)
- MPC that, each frame, plans control-parameter spline trajectories a few seconds ahead with sequential Monte Carlo. A multimodal tree-based sampler explores several near-term strategies at once, and the strategies are evaluated in parallel in a physics engine. A 36-DOF human balances, dodges projectiles and "improvise[s] a get up strategy if forced to lose balance", with no mocap, state machines or offline precomputation — [SIGGRAPH history abstract](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=103531)
- The released source code contains only the sampler and an inverted-pendulum example, and the author's page says there are "no Unity examples yet" — [Hämäläinen publications page](https://users.aalto.fi/~hamalap5/publications.html)
- Independent measurement: Al Borno et al. reimplemented the SMC method (Algorithm 1) with PD control, noting that "their actuation approach … is specific to the Open Dynamics Engine". Steering random lying states into the domain of a get-up controller, SMC "succeeds 9, 4, and 2 times out of 10 trials" for increasingly hard start sets (β = 2, 6, 8). They add that SMC "fails to have the character get up under certain prone and supine postures" — [DOA expansion](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)

**Hämäläinen, Rajamäki, Liu, SIGGRAPH 2015, "Online control of simulated humanoids using particle belief propagation" (C-PBP)** (abstract only)
- Gradient-free multimodal sampling plus a Markov Random Field factorisation, for "simultaneous path finding and smoothing". Behaviours: balancing, recovery "from both small and extreme disturbances", reaching, balancing on and juggling a ball, and steerable locomotion, all from simple cost functions without offline training, mocap or state machines. **Getting up is not named in the abstract** — [SIGGRAPH history abstract](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=112721)
- "Near real-time (20–30 fps)" for a humanoid with 30 actuated DoF and 6 unactuated root DoF. This comes from a search-engine snippet of the preprint, which I could not open — [C-PBP preprint (unreachable)](https://mediatech.aalto.fi/publications/graphics/C-PBP/preprint.pdf)
- Follow-up: Rajamäki & Hämäläinen (SCA 2017) add nearest-neighbour and neural-network learners trained on the noisy MPC output. The abstract (via search) reports stable gaits in under a minute on a 4-core CPU — [Eurographics DL record](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1145/3099564-3099579)

**Recent MPPI-style sampling MPC**
- Reference-free sampling MPC (Schramm et al., arXiv 2511.19204, 2025): MPPI with diffusion-style annealing (from DIAL-MPC) and Hermite splines. On a 16-core Mac Studio M3 CPU, quadruped updates take about 20 to 30 ms with 30 samples and 3 iterations. The simulated Unitree G1 humanoid needed 60 to 70 samples, and real-time execution was "prevented". **No get-up**, and motions are "not yet optimized for smoothness, efficiency, or robustness" — [Schramm et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19204v2)
- I found no sampling-MPC paper reporting humanoid get-up on hardware, or with success rates across start states.

### Inferences
- **Body responsibility:** MJPC, Tassa and Hämäläinen all act through joint actuators only and are compatible with the rule in principle. The real risk is that the optimiser finds "a single bounce" or a fling that is physically valid but uses unrealistically large or fast torques. With the miner's realistic strength caps, both critiqued methods failed on some postures.
- **Naturalness** depends on the cost. The MJPC humanoid cost has no posture or style terms, only CoM, torso, head height, velocity and effort. Expect energetic, opportunistic and sometimes strange get-ups unless effort, smoothness, joint-limit and "use the arms before the legs" style terms are added. Predictive sampling is noisy by construction, as SAMCON 2015 also found, so control smoothing in spline space, or low-pass drive targets, will be needed.
- **Real-time budget, as a formula:** rollout steps per replan = N × H. MJPC humanoid-like settings are N = 10 and H = 23. A 0.5 s horizon at a 50 Hz physics step is H = 25, so N × H = 250 articulation steps per replan. Replanning at 10 to 20 Hz then needs 2,500 to 5,000 articulation steps per second of game time for one character, which is 50x to 100x real time. MJPC gets away with this because MuJoCo steps its 27-DoF humanoid 4,000x faster than real time on one thread. Whether Unity PhysX can do 50x to 100x for one 13-link articulation while the game also runs is the deciding unknown (see Section 3).
- **Myopia matters for getting up from the back.** The cost improves only once the trunk rises, so a short horizon can stall in a lying local minimum. That is why MJPC recommends value functions or proposal policies. The project's existing key-pose sequence could serve as the **nominal/proposal plan**: MPC perturbs the next 0.3 to 0.6 s of drive targets around it and re-plans every 0.05 to 0.1 s. This combines the offline search with online feedback.
- iLQG needs finite-difference derivatives, about 2 × state dimension rollouts per time step. For a 13-part articulation, with roughly 30 to 40 joint DoF plus a 6-DoF root and velocities, that is far beyond what a game can spend in PhysX. Predictive sampling or CEM/MPPI is the realistic online choice.

### Gaps
- Full-text details of Hämäläinen 2014/2015 were not accessible: sample counts, horizon, cores, frame rate for 2014, and any get-up success rates.
- MJPC reports no get-up success rate across start states, no torque-limit sensitivity, and no naturalness assessment. The humanoid's actuator gear and limits were not checked.
- No published rollout throughput was found for a 13-link articulation in any engine except MuJoCo's 4,000x for a 27-DoF humanoid.

## 3. Can such rollouts be done in Unity PhysX (separate physics scenes, cloning ArticulationBody state, determinism, cost)?

### Takeaway
Mechanically, yes. Unity supports scene-local 3D physics scenes stepped manually with `PhysicsScene.Simulate(fixedStep)`, and ArticulationBody exposes whole-hierarchy get/set for joint positions, velocities, forces and drive targets, plus `TeleportRoot` and root velocities. But a clone is only an **approximation** of the live body. PhysX keeps internal contact state that cannot be serialized, so restoring an in-contact state mid-run is documented as non-deterministic. PhysX determinism also holds only for identical scenes with identical insertion order. No published benchmark of ArticulationBody step cost was found, so the project must measure it before choosing online MPC.

### Cited Findings
- Independent physics scenes must be created through scripting, and each "cannot auto-simulate". Documented uses include instantiating physics scenes to "predict GameObject collisions and trajectories", isolating "a very detailed character in its own physics Scene", and creating "pre-populated physics Scenes to be able to entirely destroy and reload them in order to improve determinism" — [Unity Manual: Multi-scene physics](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/physics-multi-scene.html)
- `LocalPhysicsMode.Physics3D`: "A local 3D physics Scene will be created and owned by the Scene". Its lifetime follows the Unity scene — [Unity ScriptReference: LocalPhysicsMode](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/SceneManagement.LocalPhysicsMode.html)
- `PhysicsScene.Simulate(step)`: only components in that scene are affected. "To achieve more deterministic physics results, you should pass a fixed step value". "Running the simulation during physics callbacks will always fail". The doc example steps non-default scenes manually in `FixedUpdate` — [Unity ScriptReference: PhysicsScene.Simulate](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/PhysicsScene.Simulate.html)
- `FixedUpdate` logic does not run during `PhysicsScene.Simulate`. Prediction code must call the controller logic itself after each simulate step, or the prediction diverges from the live game (Unity forum, via search summary) — [Unity forum thread](https://forum.unity.com/threads/executing-fixedupdate-logic-during-physicsscene-simulate.732755/)
- ArticulationBody state API: `GetJointPositions/SetJointPositions`, `GetJointVelocities/SetJointVelocities`, `GetJointForces/SetJointForces`, `GetDriveTargets/SetDriveTargets` and `Get/SetDriveTargetVelocities` for "the entire hierarchy". There are also per-body `jointPosition`, `jointVelocity`, `jointAcceleration` and `jointForce` in reduced coordinates, `linearVelocity`/`angularVelocity`, `TeleportRoot`, `GetDofStartIndices`, `Sleep/WakeUp`, and `solverIterations`/`solverVelocityIterations` — [Unity ScriptReference: ArticulationBody](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/ScriptReference/ArticulationBody.html)
- PhysX determinism: "PhysX provides limited deterministic simulation". Results are identical between runs only for "the exact same scene" (same actors inserted in the same order, same time-stepping, same PhysX release, same platform). It is "not influenced by the number of worker threads", but "can change if actors are inserted in a different order", and adding actors, even ones that never touch, can change behaviour. `eENABLE_ENHANCED_DETERMINISM` makes an island's simulation independent of other islands when insertion order is deterministic, and "sacrifices some performance" — [PhysX 5.4.1 docs: Rigid Body Dynamics](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.1/docs/RigidBodyDynamics.html)
- Unity exposes this as "Enable Enhanced Determinism": "Simulation in the scene is consistent regardless the actors present", at some performance cost. Simulation Mode "Script" runs physics only when a script calls `Physics.Simulate`. For articulations, one- and two-directional friction require the TGS solver — [Unity Manual: Physics settings](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/class-PhysicsManager.html)
- Restoring state: "Replaying a simulation from an in-contact simulation state saved out in the middle of a simulation run … can be nondeterministic", because "the PhysX SDK is using internal contact state that can persist over multiple simulation steps" which "cannot currently be serialized". The workaround is to "Restart simulations from the beginning" (NVIDIA Isaac Sim / Omniverse limitations table) — [Isaac Sim PhysX limitations](https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/5.1.0/physics/physics_resources.html)
- PhysX articulations are "much better at simulating articulated objects" but "more expensive than regular joints" — [PhysX 5.4.1 Best Practices](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.1/docs/BestPractices.html)
- Throughput references from other engines: MuJoCo steps a 27-DoF humanoid 4,000x faster than real time on one thread — [MJPC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541). ODE ran the SAMCON-era humanoid at about real time with a 0.5 ms step — [SAMCON 2010](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf). ODE with implicit damping ran it at 10x real time single-threaded — [Control Graphs](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf)
- No published benchmark of Unity ArticulationBody steps per second, or CPU cost per articulation, was found (search returned only unrelated Mecanim and robotics-simulator comparisons) — [search: HumanoidBench reports MuJoCo 1000+ FPS on one CPU](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.10506)
- Project fact: the existing `GetUpSearch` and benches already switch `Physics.simulationMode` to `Script` and step physics themselves — [Docs/Technical/UnityProjectContext.md, lines ~2036–2041 and ~2095–2102](../../../Technical/UnityProjectContext.md)

### Inferences
- **A workable Unity design for online rollouts:** keep one planning scene (`LocalPhysicsMode.Physics3D`) with a copy of the local ground colliders and N clones of the miner articulation. Place them far apart, or on layers that ignore each other, so one `Simulate` call advances all N rollouts and PhysX can solve the separate islands in parallel. Each replan: `TeleportRoot` each clone to the live root pose, set the root linear and angular velocity, `SetJointPositions/Velocities` (and drive targets), then step H times. Before each step, write that candidate's drive targets, calling the same controller code the live body uses, because `FixedUpdate` will not run. Then score.
- **The clone will not match the live body exactly.** Contact caches, solver warm-start and friction anchors are not copied, and inserting clones changes the scene. So rollouts predict approximately. Online MPC tolerates this, since Tassa deliberately planned with a different model and MJPC reports robustness to model mismatch, because it re-plans from the measured state every few tens of milliseconds. **Offline searches that score a fixed plan once are hurt by it more.**
- **On the project's chaotic counts:** the PhysX determinism conditions (insertion order, other actors present, release and platform) plus non-restorable contact state mean that "same code, different counts" can come partly from the simulation setup, not only from the controller. Turning on Enhanced Determinism, and building each bench try from a freshly instantiated scene in a fixed order, would separate controller chaos from setup non-determinism. The flip side: a controller that only works in a bit-exact replay is not robust. MPC-style re-planning, or feedback, is the cure either way.
- **The measurement that decides online MPC:** time `PhysicsScene.Simulate(0.02)` for 1, 8, 16 and 32 isolated miner clones on a target-spec CPU (the bench may already imply it: wall time per try divided by simulated seconds). If one 13-link step costs about c ms, a predictive-sampling MPC with N = 10, H = 25 at 10 Hz replanning costs about 2,500·c ms of CPU per game-second. For example (illustrative, not measured), c = 0.05 ms gives 125 ms per second, about 12% of a core per character.
- Unity's scripting API is generally main-thread bound, so rollouts would run inside the main thread's frame budget unless physics worker threads parallelise the islands. Doing the rollouts asynchronously over several frames, MJPC-style with the agent and planner decoupled, is the safer architecture: keep executing the current plan while the next one is being computed.

### Gaps
- No measured per-step cost for a 13-link ArticulationBody in Unity 6 was found anywhere. It must be measured in the project.
- I did not confirm whether `GetJointPositions`/`SetJointPositions` include the floating root's 6 DoF in Unity, or whether the root state must be set separately (the docs list `TeleportRoot` and root velocities separately). This needs a test.
- I did not confirm which PhysX version Unity 6 ships. The determinism quotes are from PhysX 5.4.1 documentation and may differ in detail.
- Whether `PhysicsScene.Simulate` can be called off the main thread, or for several scenes concurrently, is undocumented in what I read.
- No Unity example of MPC or sampling control of an ArticulationBody character was found (Hämäläinen's page explicitly has none).

## 4. Contact-invariant optimisation, trajectory optimisation plus feedback, and robustness to different start states

### Takeaway
CIO (Mordatch, Todorov, Popović 2012) discovers get-up strategies from only a "stand" goal and a lying start, giving different strategies for back and stomach. But it optimises a **simplified, non-executable physics** (all mass at the root, massless limbs, helper contact forces during continuation), so its output must be tracked by a separate controller in the real simulator. Its descendant Ensemble-CIO made plans robust enough to run on a real Darwin-OP robot, including getting up, by optimising across an ensemble of perturbed models. For many start states, the strongest result found is **Domain-of-Attraction expansion** (Al Borno, van de Panne, Fiume 2017). It is an offline tree of many trajectory-optimised, PD-tracked get-up branches on a 40.5 kg, 25-DoF character with ±150 Nm torque limits. It succeeded 10, 10 and 9 of 10 times where online SMC managed 9, 4 and 2.

### Cited Findings
**CIO (SIGGRAPH 2012)**
- Synthesises "getting up from the ground, crawling, climbing, moving heavy objects, acrobatics" with no mocap or behaviour-specific knowledge. Auxiliary scalar variables switch each potential contact on or off per movement phase and are optimised with the trajectory — [CIO PDF](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- Get-up: using only the stand task (torso between the feet, feet extended, torso vertical) from a lying start; "different initial poses (such as lying on the back, or on the stomach) result in different getting up strategies" — [CIO](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- **Physics is simplified:** "All mass is assumed to be concentrated at the root bodies". Limbs are massless, with an acceleration cost, and the body is represented as "a single rigid body", which "effectively results in limbs with infinitely strong muscles". The resulting motions can be energy-inefficient ("bent knees for support, having arms extended against gravity, or occasional out of place sitting"). Continuation allows contact forces "from a distance" and stretched limbs early in optimisation. Root helper forces are not allowed — [CIO](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)
- Compute: 2 to 10 minutes per clip on a quad-core 2.3 GHz Xeon. "The style of the motions was not the focus of this work". Global optimisation "could provide more robust exploration … especially for tasks such as getting up that have a wide range of possible and equally good solutions". Applying CIO to full physics was left as future work — [CIO](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf)

**Ensemble-CIO (Mordatch, Lowrey, Todorov, ICRA 2015)** (via a search engine summary of the PDF)
- Executing CIO trajectories on a Darwin-OP "even with local feedback derived from the optimizer, does not result in stable movements". Planning through ensembles of perturbed models made execution succeed: about 90% forward walking, 95% sideways walking and 80% turning. Getting up from the floor had a "similarly high" success rate that was not precisely quantified because the robot broke. Compute was about 5 min of CPU for walking and up to 20 min for getting up; not real-time — [Ensemble-CIO PDF](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~todorov/papers/MordatchICRA15.pdf)

**DOA expansion (Al Borno, van de Panne, Fiume, ACM TOG 2017)**
- Each controller is a time-indexed cubic B-spline of PD targets (knots every 0.1 s, kp = 700 Nm/rad, kd = 1 Nms/rad), optimised by CMA-ES to reach a target state with minimum effort. The method then grows a tree. It samples a state outside the current domain of attraction, connects it by trajectory optimisation to some node already inside, and adds the new branch's states as nodes. "We can build robust controllers by combining many weak ones together" — [DOA PDF](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)
- Get-up start states come from dropping the character from the air in randomised orientations and joint ranges and waiting until it lies on the ground. Three get-up controllers with different strategies were expanded. Initial controllers came from 1 to 3 hand-specified key frames as trajectory-optimisation targets — [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)
- **Character and physics:** MuJoCo, 0.01 s step, friction 1, **torque limits ±150 Nm, mass 40.5 kg, height 1.61 m, 25 DoF** — [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)
- **The project's current heuristic, tested and failed:** "supply a single prone or supine target pose … and wait until the motion comes largely to rest … then apply a fixed, known rising strategy … this heuristic method does not work with our controllers. The character never exactly reaches the designated target pose and the resulting errors typically make the controllers fail." In contrast, "a small tree, e.g., 80 nodes, is sufficient to obtain a robust rising controller that is invariant with respect to the facing direction" — [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)
- Results: the character gets up with the desired facing direction "from a large set of initial states", with strategies including rolling prone to supine (and back) and rotating itself on the ground. Tree sizes were 1,377, 751 and 277 nodes for the three get-up controllers. Trees took "between 1 and 3 days of computation on a desktop computer". DFC (breadth-first dense trees) needed 562 trajectory optimisations against 4,763 for RRT-style growth on one get-up domain — [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)
- **Head-to-head against online MPC:** SMC (their reimplementation of Hämäläinen 2014) succeeded 9, 4 and 2 of 10; after DOA expansion, 10, 10 and 9 of 10 for the same start sets. Their failure was "not performing enough iterations offline or failing to find the node … online". iLQG (MuJoCo) and SMC "fall into local minima" when steering into the standing-twist controller's domain — [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)
- Run time: to choose which branch to use, the method runs V forward rollouts from the current state with the controllers of the V nearest nodes (V typically 50 to 250). "On a single core, Algorithm 3 runs in real-time on average because only a few rollouts are usually required." A hybrid version, with online CMA tracking of a small tree, ran at about 5% of real time on 8 logical cores. A nearest-neighbour classifier replacing the rollouts "required an excessively high number of samples" — [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)
- Stated weaknesses: "Our synthesized motions do not always look natural". The controllers "are all time-indexed, which are known to lack robustness". Converting them to state-based controllers is future work, and LQR rather than PD feedback is suggested — [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)
- Related prior work cited there: Lin & Huang (2012) extend mocap get-ups to new lying postures with a final motion "that has both physics-based and motion-capture-based segments", which is not allowed by the project's rule — [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf)

**A simpler physics-based get-up claim**
- Kenwright (arXiv 2023, single author, not peer-reviewed) tracks continuous CoM, hand and foot references from an inverted-pendulum-like model to make a biped rise "from various lying positions". The abstract gives no timing, success rates, or statement that only joint torques are used (abstract only) — [Kenwright 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05056)

### Inferences
- DOA expansion is the closest published match to the project's problem: a body of the same mass, a realistic torque cap, PD-target splines, many lying start states, a getting-up goal and a demand for robustness across starts. Its recipe maps directly onto the project's bench. The current searched key-pose sequence becomes the root controller. Starts that fail become samples, each connected by a short CMA search (0.2 to 1 s) to any state already known to succeed, so that the tree grows. At run time, the current lying state is matched to nearby nodes by a few short look-ahead rollouts (this needs the planning scene from Section 3) or by a cheaper nearest-neighbour lookup.
- CIO-style discovery is valuable offline for finding strategies, for example how a big-headed, short-armed body might roll to its stomach first. But a CIO trajectory is not a controller. It would need tracking (time-varying LQR, or SAMCON-style sampling plus guided linear feedback), and Ensemble-CIO shows that tracking alone was not enough without robustness built into the plan.
- **"Ensemble" is the cheapest robustness lever for the project's existing search:** score each candidate across perturbed models (strength ±, friction ±, mass and head mass ±, small timing jitter), not only across start states. Ensemble-CIO did this and it carried over to hardware. It also directly targets the try-to-try chaos.

### Gaps
- No paper found applies time-varying LQR to a full-physics get-up trajectory and reports success rates across start states. DOA uses PD-tracked splines and only suggests LQR.
- Ensemble-CIO's get-up details (ensemble size, perturbations, success figure) were read only through a search summary.
- Whether CIO has since been run on full physics for getting up was not checked.

## 5. Reported success rates, naturalness, and compute cost, side by side

### Takeaway
Quantitative get-up success rates are scarce. The best are DOA expansion's 10/10/9 of 10 against SMC's 9/4/2 of 10, and SAMCON's offline reconstruction success of one third to three quarters. Naturalness is almost never measured, and authors repeatedly admit unnatural results without style terms: bounces, jerk from sampling noise, struggling, inefficient postures. Compute splits into two regimes: hours to days offline for robust controllers that are cheap at run time, or 1 to 20 ms per planning step online with a physics engine thousands of times faster than real time.

### Inferences
- The only controlled comparison (DOA against SMC) suggests that **online sampling MPC alone degrades sharply as start states get harder**, while offline-expanded sets of controllers stay robust. A hybrid has the best evidence of covering both: offline-robust controllers plus short online look-ahead to choose and correct between them.
- None of these methods gives natural motion "for free" without reference motion. The mocap-guided SAMCON line looks most human. Cost-only MPC and CIO need explicit style terms (effort, smoothness, joint-limit comfort, preferred contact order). For the project, which has no mocap, the director-approved key poses, or a filmed reference studied as data, are the natural "reference" for SAMCON-style tracking.

### Gaps
- No study reports get-up success under torque caps as low as a 40 kg miner's, apart from DOA's ±150 Nm. No study tests a large, heavy head.
- No perceptual or naturalness study for any physics get-up method was found.

## 6. Method-by-method fit for this project (body responsibility, robustness, naturalness, real-time cost, Unity effort, risks)

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced path keeps the body fully responsible and needs no external force. It combines two parts. Offline: grow robustness around the existing searched key-pose plan, either SAMCON-style guided linear feedback per 0.1 s fragment, or a DOA tree of many PD-spline branches, scored over ensembles of start states **and** perturbed models. Online: optionally a cheap predictive-sampling look-ahead in a separate PhysX scene that chooses or corrects the next 0.3 to 0.6 s. Whether pure online MPC is affordable hinges on measuring the ArticulationBody step cost in Unity.

### Cited Findings
- Joint-actuation-only character control is used in SAMCON (PD servos, unactuated root) — [SAMCON 2010](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf); in Control Graphs (PD on target angles) — [Control Graphs](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2016-TOG-controlGraphs/2016-TOG-controlGraphs.pdf); in DOA (PD splines, ±150 Nm) — [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf); and in MJPC and Tassa (actuator torques) — [MJPC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00541), [Tassa 2012](https://02465material.pages.compute.dtu.dk/02465public/_assets/tassa2012.pdf)
- Rule violations to avoid: kinematic blending at controller switches (SAMCON 2010) — [SAMCON 2010](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~van/papers/2010-TOG-sampControl/2010-TOG-samcon.pdf); pinned feet (Liu & Hodgins) — [Scheduler](http://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/libinliu/Scheduler/SchedulerTOG.pdf); CIO's helper forces and massless limbs during optimisation — [CIO](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~zoran/behavior-discovery.pdf); mocap-segment stitching (Lin & Huang, as described in [DOA](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~mazen/DOA/DOA.pdf))

### Inferences (assessment per method; effort and cost estimates are my judgement, not sourced)
1. **Online predictive sampling MPC (MJPC-style), in a planning PhysicsScene**
   - *Body responsible:* yes, through drive targets and torques only.
   - *Robustness:* good against pushes, but degrades on hard lying states with a short horizon (SMC 9/4/2 of 10 in DOA's test).
   - *Naturalness:* noisy and opportunistic unless effort and smoothness terms are added and the spline is low-pass filtered. There is a risk of fling or bounce if strength caps are generous.
   - *Run-time cost:* about N × H × replan-rate articulation steps (for example 2,500 to 5,000 per game-second per character). Unknown in PhysX until measured; probably fine for one hero miner, not for crowds.
   - *Unity effort:* moderate, about 500 to 1,000 lines of C#: a clone manager, state copy, spline parameters, cost and an async planner. It reuses the existing `Lives`/drive-target code, called manually per step.
   - *Risks:* the clone/contact mismatch, main-thread budget, local minima on the back, and tuning time. Mitigate by seeding the nominal with the existing searched key-pose sequence.
2. **Online CEM/MPPI or SMC/C-PBP (multimodal sampling)**
   - Same profile as item 1, with better exploration (several strategies kept alive) at higher cost (more samples, horizons of about 1 to 2 s).
   - Hämäläinen's published code has no Unity example.
3. **Online iLQG/DDP (Tassa)**
   - *Body responsible:* yes.
   - *Cost:* finite differences need about 2 × state-dimension rollouts per step, so it is impractical in PhysX.
   - *Evidence:* bouncy get-ups, which fail under realistic torque caps. Not recommended.
4. **Offline SAMCON-style sampling plus guided linear feedback (Control Graphs recipe)**
   - *Body responsible:* yes.
   - *Robustness:* the best-demonstrated route from open-loop to closed-loop for contact-rich skills, including get-ups.
   - *Naturalness:* tracks a reference. Here that would be the director's key poses or a studied real get-up as a time-indexed target, smoothed by elite averaging.
   - *Run-time cost:* negligible (one small matrix-vector product per 0.1 s).
   - *Unity effort:* moderate to high, but it reuses the bench. It needs a state-feature vector, regression, and an iterative guided loop (tens of iterations of batches of tries).
   - *Risks:* time-indexing (it fails if the body falls far behind schedule), and a separate controller is needed per get-up family (back, front, side).
5. **Offline DOA-expansion tree (Al Borno 2017)**
   - *Body responsible:* yes.
   - *Robustness:* the strongest published get-up robustness across start states, on a body of the same mass with ±150 Nm caps.
   - *Naturalness:* "not always natural"; a style cost is needed in each connecting optimisation.
   - *Run-time cost:* either a few short look-ahead rollouts in a planning scene ("real-time on average" on one core in MuJoCo), or a nearest-neighbour table (which the authors found sample-hungry).
   - *Offline cost:* 1 to 3 desktop days per tree in MuJoCo, probably more in Unity at a lower simulation speed.
   - *Unity effort:* high (tree storage, a state metric, a CMA connector, run-time selection).
   - *Risks:* offline compute, the state-metric choice, and determinism of node "success" labels (re-test nodes under perturbed models).
6. **CIO and trajectory optimisation, then tracking**
   - *Body responsible:* only after tracking in full physics. The optimisation itself uses non-physical helpers and simplified dynamics.
   - Useful offline to discover strategies, not as the controller.
   - Implementation in C# is heavy (a QP inside the optimisation loop) and not recommended as a first step.
7. **Cheapest immediate improvement to the existing search** (inference from Ensemble-CIO and Improved SAMCON):
   - Score candidates over perturbed models as well as start states.
   - Re-test the best candidates on fresh seeds before accepting them, since CEM can fit to noise.
   - Enable Enhanced Determinism and build each try from a fresh scene in a fixed insertion order, to separate setup non-determinism from controller fragility.
   - Then add closed-loop correction (item 4 or item 1). The literature says an open-loop plan alone will stay fragile.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how much a planning-scene MPC would cost in Unity PhysX. The deciding number must come from the project's own bench.
- No published work tests any of these methods on a stylised body with a large heavy head and short limbs. Whether its strength and proportions allow the human-like strategies found in these papers is unverified.
- Whether online MPC's naturalness can be made acceptable to the Game Director is a feel judgement that no paper can answer. A short prototype with a seeded nominal plan would be the cheapest way to find out.
