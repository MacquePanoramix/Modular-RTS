# Reader notes: the cold-reader review, the acceptance checks and the long study

Source: five files in the package (the fresh-receiver review, the final guide acceptance, the final receiving check, the independent foundation comparison, the foundation case assessment). Written November 16, 2026. Plain words. Nothing is copied. P-numbers refer to `notes_main_guide.md`.

## 1. How the cold-reader (fresh-receiver) review was done

**The question it answered.** Could a competent newcomer, holding only the guide, tell what is true now from what is history or proposal? Could that newcomer start useful work, find who owns each missing fact, handle a blocked dependency without a bypass, and see all the finishing duties?

**What the reader was given.**
- The whole main guide, line by line.
- The reference files it chose to open. It read some in full, some in part, and named which.
- Later, short notices from the author when the guide was revised during the review.

**What it was not given, or did not do.**
- It did not read the other reviewers' reports. This was kept on purpose, so its view stayed its own.
- It touched no project source, ran nothing, edited nothing, contacted nobody and used no helper agents.
- It did not check the published page or the transport of the files. That stayed with the author.

**What it had to disclose about itself.**
- Exactly which bytes it judged (line count and size), so the verdict attaches to one version. A later change to the text needs a recheck of the affected part only.
- Exactly what it read in full, what in part, and what it never opened. It even lists the part of the references it had only looked through for navigation, and says that is not a review.
- Which findings it made alone and which came after the author's notices. Notices count as help it was given, not as its own discoveries. It did note one problem it had already found before the notice arrived.
- A small slip: it once consulted an old wrapper file, and says when and why (only to recover one missing locator, after its own analysis was done).

**What it found that the authors had missed.**
1. An ordering mistake. The guide put a "final handover" step before a "reliability" step, and treated "the old thing was removed" as the whole result of the retirement phase. The phase files showed that the retirement phase also demands a useful cycle after the removal, and that the later steps depend on it. Fixed.
2. A dropped locator. A new optional bundle had its size and check value stated, but the place to get it had been lost when the guide was rewritten. A newcomer would have needed the private history to find it. Fixed.
3. A spacing slip in a number. Trivial.

All three are the kind of thing an author cannot see in their own text: a dependency read the wrong way, and a fact that fell out during a rewrite. The reader found them by checking the guide's claims against the underlying files, not by reading the guide alone.

**The practice, in general words, so another project could run it.**
- Hand a fresh agent the entry text and the supporting files. Hold back the earlier reviews and the author's explanations.
- Ask a plain question: could you start, and what would you do first? Make the reader write that first step down. The reader here did (recover the normal entry, the holds and the owners; do one bounded useful piece; do not start with a full diagnosis).
- Require a statement of exposure: the exact subject, what was read in full and in part, what was not opened, what help was received and when.
- Allow "accept, with nothing required" and allow "low-importance finding only". Do not pay the reviewer for finding something.
- Let the verdict attach to one version. Any later change is rechecked only where it touches.
- Keep the author's duties (publishing, transport, status wording) out of the reader's verdict.

## 2. What the acceptance and receiving checks check, and what they say they do NOT claim

**The final guide acceptance.**
- Checks the guide against a short list of completion goals: the intent is recoverable; the first useful piece of work is concrete; the latest input and lessons are included; no material finding is left; the published copy is correct.
- It checks that the changes made match the stated difference between the old and new text exactly, and that the list of 47 outputs is unchanged in content and order.
- Its verdict is "accept, conditional on delivery checks". The last goal (the published copy) is marked pending, and no earlier receiving result may be relabelled as belonging to the new version.
- It says it is a consistency and integration review by someone who knew the earlier material, not a cold read. It says it wrote the earlier comparison that led to the refinements, and did not write the revision.
- It does NOT claim: that the implementation exists; that the guide was used in real work; that it saves effort; that the reform is qualified; that the stated rejection rules actually reject anything; that every old result was rechecked; that the saved page matches.

**The final receiving check.**
- Reads the whole guide, then checks only the changed parts after each late edit, and says so each time. It records the three hashes it saw.
- Checks that each claim in the guide about current versus proposed things is supported by the files named for it; that the first useful piece of work is an actual build-and-use outcome and not "call a command that does not exist yet"; that a blocked dependency has a stated repair and is not hidden; that the order of phases is right; that the preserved list of outputs equals the original; that all preserved payload files match their recorded sizes and check values.
- It does NOT claim: that the guide was used for real; that the supplier facts, the safe route or the receiver exist; that the whole set of 109 cases, 68 phases and 48 stimuli was reread; that later changed bytes are covered.
- It discloses a small overreach of its own: a file search listed one unrelated instruction file by name. It was not opened. It reports this as a scope slip.

**What all three share (general, and close to P1, P3, P31).** Each verdict names its exact subject. Each lists what was read. Each separates "the document is coherent" from "the thing works in use". Each says which outstanding items are still open. Reading a count or an index is called navigation, not proof.

## 3. Lessons about long-running agent work (the long study)

The long study was a timed measurement run of many samples. Agents ran it for a long time and left it unfinished: about half the samples were recorded passing, many obligations were pending, and no final result was reported. The files are secondhand summaries, so they are weak evidence about what happened. The lessons they draw are still general.

What went wrong, in general terms:
1. **A child process ending was taken for the task ending.** The run's main work could finish while comparison, result derivation and storage of evidence were still to do. Exit with success meant nothing about the whole.
2. **A run was stopped part way and its parts were mixed with a later run.** The authors now insist on a fresh, complete run, no splicing of old and new samples, and a truthful label for a partial run (a diagnostic is a diagnostic, not a measurement).
3. **A measure was not the measure.** A sampled reading was taken for the true peak. The label must match what was actually measured.
4. **A check passed on the wrong thing.** A row could be "pass" while the check behind it had run in the wrong role or had never really executed. Support for a claim was credited that had not been demonstrated.
5. **Storage that "had the bytes" but could not be read by the next step.** An archive held the data, but the later step needed it in another form. Removing the original after a hash-only receipt would have lost it. The fix: prove with a cold read through the real reader, check every needed member and reference, and keep the failed attempts, before anything is deleted.
6. **Work called "independent" competed for the same machine.** Lanes were independent as tasks but shared processor, memory and disk. Heavy side work had to wait.
7. **An author crossed the blind line of their own test.** It was disclosed and separately reviewed. A new agent name is not independence.
8. **The record of an agent said "working" when nothing was running.** An idle message is not a real dispatch. Names and process numbers from old notes are history, not live authority.
9. **A repair made after measuring was filed as if it came before.** It was relabelled truthfully as before collection, not before measurement.
10. **The written record was a summary of conversations, not the transcript.** What was lost could not be rebuilt from the summary. Missing paths in a bounded search do not prove global loss.

What the authors do differently now:
- A named owner is responsible through the whole end: comparison, result, storage, receiving. Only then is it done.
- The terminal point is named before the run starts.
- Evidence of a pass must join: the exact rule, the case, the thing run, the outcome and the saved observation. A schema-valid pass row is not enough.
- A rule written down is not shown to work until it rejected a known-bad case.
- Costs are counted in layers (source, journal, proof, storage, memory, processor), not as one number. Large reported size shows "untested fit", not "unfit".
- They do not carry the study's own special counts into other tasks as general gates.
- Exposure and chronology are stated plainly.

## 4. Transferable ideas not in P1 to P36

**R1. A verdict is tied to one exact version of the subject.** Where: all five files. Guards against: a later edit quietly using an older approval. Cheap version here: name the build or commit a note was checked against.

**R2. Say who received help, and when.** Where: cold-reader review. Guards against: a "fresh" reviewer whose findings were really the author's hints. A reviewer notes what it found before and after being told things. (Related to P22 but adds the timing of disclosure.)

**R3. A late small edit gets a limited recheck that says it is limited.** Where: final receiving check. Guards against: either rereading everything each time, or silently trusting the whole after a tweak. The check names the lines reread and calls it an affected-text check, not a fresh full review.

**R4. The author's own duties stay out of the reviewer's verdict.** Where: cold reader (publishing, status wording left to the author). Guards against: a reviewer vouching for something it did not check.

**R5. A rewrite can drop a fact that the old text had.** Where: cold-reader finding on the lost locator. Guards against: silent loss in a clean-up pass. A reader who compares new text against the old and the files finds it. Matches this project's habit of keeping words verbatim, but adds the check after a rewrite.

**R6. An index or count is navigation, not evidence.** Where: acceptance and receiving checks. (Close to P7; the extra point is that the structure checks, such as "same 47 items in the same order", are named as structure only.)

**R7. A repair made after a measurement is labelled as such.** Where: foundation comparison. Guards against: "before" and "after" blurring when a bar or reader is fixed once results are known. Say when the repair came relative to the result.

**R8. Never splice parts of a stopped run into a new run.** Where: foundation comparison. Guards against: a result that is half old setup and half new. Begin a fresh complete run, or keep the partial labelled partial. Fits this project's repeated test courses.

**R9. A file search can overreach.** Where: receiving check, own disclosure. Guards against: opening or listing things outside the permitted scope while looking for a name. Narrow the search first.

**R10. Missing paths in a bounded search do not prove loss.** Where: foundation files. Guards against: declaring something gone or never done because one search failed. Say "not found here", not "does not exist".

**R11. A source that is a summary of conversation is labelled so.** Where: foundation case assessment. Guards against: a handoff written from memory being treated as a record. Useful for this project's own handoff and Outlook pages.

**R12. "Accept" and "optional polish" are separate.** Where: final acceptance (it says optional wording work does not justify another full cycle). Guards against: an endless review loop. If a flaw shows later in use, fix the affected part and recheck what depends on it; do not restart.

## 5. What argues against ceremony, or was called too heavy

- The cold reader's own note on the guide: it protects the right to find nothing, protects cheap discarded thought, and says ceremony must be in proportion. "No new finding" is a valid result.
- The acceptance check says no new whole-guide review is wanted unless a material change affects it. A later defect should repair its own part, not restart all preparation.
- Both checks say the guide must not create a new registry, a permanent office or a standing report layer. New controls use the existing owners.
- The foundation comparison says its special 9,928-key rules are for that study, not new gates for every task, and that no new universal matrix is warranted. It says to carry only the long-task and cold-reader lessons into the eventual real run.
- It also says the first small work item should not be sold as the full qualification, and that the reviewers should not freeze unrelated work.
- Heavy burden shown by the files themselves: huge text for one document, line-by-line review of hundreds of lines, repeated hash checks and many review cycles before a document was called ready. The reviewers were comfortable with this for a large multi-party project. The files do not say that it was too heavy, and I did not find a reviewer who says a step "added nothing". What I did see: much of the effort ended in "accept, nothing required" and in spacing fixes, and the guide itself still says no field use is shown. For this project's size, the hashes, byte counts and layered receipts do not transfer.
- The authors say plainly that none of the reviews proves the guide helps in real work. That is the honest limit on the whole body of ceremony: it shows coherence, not benefit.
