# Getting up, frame by frame — the references looked at (November 2, 2026)

**Asked by Luis** (October 30): "try to really see the visuals of all the real life cases frame by frame and all the
animation and character ones from video games and see what fits our aesthetic" ([the message](../Correspondence/2026-10-30_THE_GETTING_UP_FOR_THE_WHOLE_GAME.md)).
The research of October 30 ([report](reports/Getting%20up%20for%20any%20body.md), section "Study these rises frame by
frame") listed the references and where in them to look; this page is what was seen when they were stepped through.

**How:** each video opened in the app's browser and its frames drawn into one sheet in the page (seeked frame by frame,
nothing downloaded), every few seconds to find the rise, then every 0.25 to 0.4 s through it. The sheets are in
[Images/GettingUp_2026-11-02/references](../Images/GettingUp_2026-11-02/references/). Times are the video's own.

## What was seen

### A very tall, heavy man, from flat on his back (Tyson Fury, Wilder v Fury I, round 12)

[Every 0.6 s](../Images/GettingUp_2026-11-02/references/fury_rise_every_0.6s.jpg),
[every 0.25 s](../Images/GettingUp_2026-11-02/references/fury_rise_every_0.25s.jpg) (the live broadcast, 0:15 to 0:30).

| Time | What the body does |
|---|---|
| 0:18.0 | Lies flat on his back, arms out, still (dazed) for about four seconds; the camera cuts away |
| 0:23.5 | **The head first**: it lifts, the eyes open |
| 0:23.75 | The trunk curls up from the head down, the arms reaching forward: the sit-up begins with momentum from the arms |
| 0:24.0 to 0:24.25 | Sitting, legs out in front; **already turning** to his right while still rising: the stages overlap |
| 0:24.5 | The right hand reaches down to the floor beside the hip **before** the weight goes onto it |
| 0:24.75 to 0:25.0 | The weight moves over the right hand and knee: side-sitting becomes kneeling |
| 0:25.25 | On both knees, the hands down briefly |
| 0:25.5 to 0:26.25 | The trunk comes upright; the right foot is brought forward: **one knee down, one foot planted** |
| 0:26.5 to 0:27.0 | The trunk leans forward over the front foot, then both legs straighten together |
| 0:27.25 | Standing; a small sway, then steady |

**About 3.75 s** from the head lifting to standing, for a man of 2.06 m and over 110 kg, dazed. No stage is held: each
begins before the last has ended. The order is VanSant's commonest adult pattern (partial roll, asymmetrical,
half-kneel).

### A newborn foal: a weak body with long legs (BBC)

[Every 1.5 s](../Images/GettingUp_2026-11-02/references/foal_every_1.5s.jpg),
[the rise every 0.4 s](../Images/GettingUp_2026-11-02/references/foal_rise_every_0.4s.jpg) (1:21 to 1:29).

| Time | What the body does |
|---|---|
| 1:21.0 to 1:22.6 | Lying on its chest, front legs reaching forward, head up: **gathering**, small shifts, no rise yet |
| 1:23.0 to 1:23.4 | **Front end first**: the front legs push and the chest comes up; the head and neck go forward and up |
| 1:23.8 to 1:24.6 | The hind legs are drawn under and push; the hind end lifts, the legs splayed |
| 1:25.0 to 1:26.6 | Standing on a **very wide base**, the legs splayed and trembling; it does not move its feet for a while |

Earlier tries (from 0:50) collapse back onto the chest and are tried again after a rest. **Weakness shows as gathering
before the push, a wide base, trembling, and tries that fail and are taken up again**, not as slowness alone.

### A man in plate armour, from flat on his back (conncork)

[Every 3 s](../Images/GettingUp_2026-11-02/references/armour_every_3s.jpg),

| Time | What the body does |
|---|---|
| 1:06.6 to 1:07.2 | Lies flat on his back, still |
| 1:07.5 to 1:07.8 | Rolls to his right: the head and the near shoulder lead, the far knee bends and swings over |
| 1:08.1 to 1:08.4 | On hands and knees, the head up and looking ahead |
| 1:08.7 | The right foot comes forward beside the right hand: one knee down, one foot planted |
| 1:09.0 to 1:09.3 | The trunk over the front foot; a hand pushes on the thigh; the legs straighten |
| 1:09.6 to 1:10.5 | Upright, a little crouched, then standing; walks off |

**About 3 s**, heavy and stiff-jointed, calm and competent: the roll to the front, hands and knees, one knee, standing.
No struggle, no flourish. (Also in it, from 2:06: from lying face down, a push-up onto hands and toes, then up; and in
snow, from 2:20.)

### A weak older adult, taught to get up after a fall (Bob & Brad, physical therapists)

[Every 4 s](../Images/GettingUp_2026-11-02/references/weak_adult_staged_every_4s.jpg) (4:58 to 5:50). A demonstration
at a teaching pace, so its order matters, not its times: lying on the back; the **stronger leg drawn up first**; a roll
onto the side; pushing up on the forearm, then the hand; hands and knees; crawling on hands and knees to a table; up from
one knee against the table. Everything that a weak body does to keep **more of itself on the floor for longer**.

### A toddler, alone, from its back (Chicago Pediatric Therapy, "Floor To Stand")

Stepped through every 0.3 s (1:14.5 to 1:22), at the end, "Time to try all by yourself". **Not saved here**: it is a
child; it was looked at for study only.

| Time | What the body does |
|---|---|
| 1:16.0 to 1:17.5 | Lying on its back, arms out |
| 1:17.8 | Rolls onto its front |
| 1:18.1 to 1:19.3 | Up onto **hands and feet** ("the bear"): legs straight, hips high, head down between the arms |
| 1:19.6 to 1:19.9 | Pushes off its hands: the trunk comes up over the feet, the hands leave the floor last |
| 1:20.2 | Nearly upright, knees bent, arms forward for balance |
| 1:20.5 to 1:20.8 | Standing, arms out, a step to steady itself |

**About 2.5 to 3 s** from its back to standing, the route the research predicts for a heavy-headed, short-limbed body
(and near Small's present hands-and-feet stage). It is quick and light, not laboured: a toddler is strong for its
weight. The arms forward and out as it comes up, and the step at the end, are how it balances its big head.

### A physiotherapist's demonstration (Ask Doctor Jo)

Looked at every 8 s only: the same order from the back (onto the side, hands and knees, one knee beside a chair,
standing), and others (sitting, kneeling; from lying face down, a push-up onto hands and knees). A teaching pace.

## What they share, and what it asks of ours

1. **Something leads, and it is small: the head**, then the trunk curls after it. Fury's head lifts a quarter of a second
   before anything else moves; the armoured man's head leads the roll. Ours should begin with the head and a breath,
   not with a whole body moving at once.
2. **The stages overlap.** Fury turns while he is still sitting up; the armoured man's foot comes forward as he reaches
   hands and knees. No able body holds a stance; only the weak one (the foal) gathers, and it gathers *before a push*,
   not after reaching a stance. Our present getting up holds poses; the new one must flow from one support to the next,
   and pause only where the body needs to gather strength (by how little it has to spare).
3. **A hand goes down before the weight goes onto it**, and the weight follows it. Supports are placed, then loaded.
4. **The last push is over a planted foot**, the trunk leaning forward over it, often with a hand on the thigh; both legs
   straighten together. One knee down, one foot planted is the common last stance of the adults seen (Fury, the armour,
   the physiotherapists).
5. **The able take 3 to 4 s; weakness adds stages and tries, not slowness alone**: the weak adult keeps more of itself
   on the floor (hands and knees, crawling to a support); the foal gathers, splays, trembles, fails and tries again.
6. **Nothing is goofy in any of them**, even the dazed boxer and the newborn: the limbs that bear are firm (tone in the
   planted limbs), the free limbs hang or reach with purpose, and nothing flails. What makes a struggle read as a
   struggle and not as comedy is that every try is a sensible try, and the body gathers itself before it.

7. **The build chooses the way, as the research said.** The toddler went up by hands and feet; the tall heavy boxer and
   the armoured man through one knee; the weak adult kept to hands and knees and a support; the long-legged foal front
   first on a wide base.

**A simulated one beside them: "Learning to Get Up" (Tao et al. 2022)**, its video every 8 s, then a slow get-up every
0.4 s (1:38 to 1:46): from its back, the mannequin rolls to its side over about two seconds and comes up to sitting and
kneeling at one steady slowness. At this size its details cannot be judged; what can be seen is what the people above
do not do: it neither gathers before a push nor lets one stage run into the next. Slowed evenly, it reads as slow, not
as weak.
