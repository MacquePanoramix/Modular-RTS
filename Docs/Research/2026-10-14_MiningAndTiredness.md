# Breaking rock, and tiredness: what feels right — research, October 14, 2026

**Asked by Luis** (October 14, after the miners' arms were made as
strong as their bodies need, and they rested about half as often): "Do
a in depth research about mining rocks in media to see what's the ideal
balance that would feel right for the game and make the characters feel
more real due to their tiredness. Reminder tiredness should also
influence strength and precision in case it's not already"
([the message](../Correspondence/2026-10-14_MINING_TIREDNESS_AND_BALANCE.md)).
On the pace of work itself: "You can make what feels right."

**What was read:** how real hand miners, drillers and other heavy
workers paced themselves, tired and rested (history, ergonomics,
physiology); how tiredness changes strength and precision (movement
science); work songs; how games show mining and tiredness (and what
players say about it); how film and animation show mining and heavy
work; and how a tired body reads to a viewer. Nothing was downloaded.
Where only a summary of a source could be read, that is said; what I
know without having read it today is marked †.

## In short

1. **Real heavy hand work is rhythmic, and self-paced.** Its rhythm is
   kept steady (hand drillers trained to keep the same number of blows a
   minute), and its pace is set by the worker so that it can be kept up:
   sung, in chain gangs and prison farms, "almost painfully slowly",
   because it had to last from morning to dusk.
2. **It cannot be done without rest, and the rest is a large share of
   the day.** Work as heavy as shovelling rock (about 7 to 8 kilocalories
   a minute) needs about two fifths of the working time as rest, by the
   standard ergonomics formula. Short pauses taken often (a minute in ten)
   keep fatigue down better than none.
3. **Rest gives strength back fast at first, then slowly.** Much comes
   back in the first one or two minutes; after six minutes of rest from
   exhaustion, about four fifths; nearly all only after a quarter of an
   hour.
4. **Tiredness takes strength and precision.** A tired limb has less to
   give and moves more variably, at the shoulder most; aimed movements
   land less accurately, and the cause found was less of the joint
   stiffening co-contraction, not only less force. People partly make up
   for it by changing how they move.
5. **Games mostly show tiredness as a bar**, and what players dislike is
   work throttled by it (Valheim's mining "tedious"); what they like in
   mining is the blow itself (feedback, debris, impact) and the rhythm.
   Where tiredness is in the body (*Kingdom Come*, *Arma*), it slows,
   weakens and unsteadies.
6. **Film and animation show heavy work as rhythm and song** (Snow
   White's dwarfs, the bellows women of *Princess Mononoke*, the chain
   gang), or as grinding realism (*Germinal*): the rhythm and the toll are
   both what make it read.
7. **A tired body reads by several things at once:** its posture sags,
   its movements slow and get clumsy, its breath shows, it leans on
   things, it rests its weight on its knees; no one gesture alone.

## 1. How real heavy hand work was paced

### Hand drilling and pick work

- **Hand drillers in the American West** (before machine drills) struck
  the steel with a hammer, one man alone with a four-pound hammer
  ("single jack") or two together with six-to-eight-pound hammers
  ("double jack"). In the contests of the 1890s and after, **a usual
  hammering rate was 67 or 68 blows a minute, the best 75 or 76, and
  keeping the same rate minute after minute was what they trained for**;
  the contests lasted 15 minutes, later 10, and drillers came up out of
  the mines two weeks before to get their wind
  ([Colorado Magazine, 1934](https://historycoloradodev.exemplifi.io/sites/default/files/media/document/2018/ColoradoMagazine_v11n3_May1934.pdf);
  [Bisbee](https://www.bisbeeminingandminerals.com/competition-drilling)).
  Those are sprints by trained men. A day's work was slower.
- **Coal hewers** lay on their sides in low seams and picked at the coal
  with a short-handled pick, undercutting it and wedging it down, paid by
  what they produced; a good man got 4 to 6 tons in an 8-9 hour day,
  less in hard coal; at one New Zealand mine the union capped a hewer's
  output to protect the pace ([sources gathered here](https://www.shropshiremines.org.uk/resources/Stories/mner.pdf),
  [ILO](https://webapps.ilo.org/public/libdoc/ilo/P/09602/09602(1931-23-5)667-674.pdf);
  from summaries). No count of pick blows a shift was found.
- **A study of framing hammers** used "a fatiguing pace" of one strike a
  second ([Buchanan and others, 2016](https://ergo.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/901/2020/11/Buchanan-et-al-2016.pdf)).

### Work songs

On the chain gangs and prison farms of the American South (recorded by
Alan Lomax at Parchman Farm in 1947-48), **a leader called and the men
answered, and their tools fell on the beat**: picks swung up together and
struck in unison as a phrase ended, with the breath let out in a grunt.
The songs paced the men, eased the strong and the weak into one pace, and
were sung very slowly because they had to last the day
([Cultural Equity](https://culturalequity.org/resources/lesson-plans/work-eighteen-hammers);
[Logophile on the recordings](https://www.logophile.org/blog/2005/10/10/murderous-home-mississippi-prison-songs-from-the-40s)).
**The breath with the blow, and a pace that lasts, are what make heavy
work look like work that is lived, not performed.**

### Rest, in figures

- **Very heavy work needs much rest.** Heavy work is 5 to 7.5
  kilocalories a minute and more; shovelling rock in mine stopes was
  estimated at about 1.5 litres of oxygen a minute (about 7.5 kcal a
  minute) for a whole shift. By Murrell's formula for rest allowances,
  work at 7.5 kcal a minute over a day needs **about 42% of the time as
  rest**, roughly one minute's rest for each minute and a half of work
  ([onemine](https://onemine.org/documents/factors-affecting-the-mechanical-efficiency-of-men-shovelling-rock-in-stopes);
  [Murrell, as given in NTP 1190](https://www.insst.es/es/documentacion/colecciones-tecnicas/ntp-notas-tecnicas-de-prevencion/36-serie-ntp-numeros-1176-a-1190-ano-2023/ntp-1190-balance-energetico-y-calculo-de-pausas);
  the figure for this work is my working of the formula).
- **Short pauses, often.** Moving boxes, a minute's rest (or stretching)
  every ten minutes cut muscle fatigue markedly against none
  (a University of Alberta study, from summaries); short breaks of under
  ten minutes go with less fatigue in a review of 22 studies (Albulescu
  and others, *PLOS ONE*; from a summary, the paper itself not read).
- **How strength comes back.** Pushing a demolition hammer to
  exhaustion and then resting: strength came back as an exponential, a
  seventh of what was still missing each minute; **from 59% of full it
  was 66% after a minute, 74% after three, 82% after six**, and near full
  only after about a quarter of an hour; how hard it felt fell with it
  ([Yi and others, 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8775642/)).
  Other work finds **much of a muscle's quick force back within one or two
  minutes** (explosive force back to about four fifths within a minute;
  the muscle's fast fuel half refilled in about 20 seconds)
  ([Frontiers, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2021.660099/pdf);
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23230235/)).
- **People who set their own pace tire less than at a pace set for
  them**, for the same work (exercise research, as summarised in the
  same search).

## 2. What tiredness does to strength and precision

- **Strength:** a tired muscle can give less, and less again the longer
  it goes on; in the demolition study, holding a push of a share of one's
  strength lasted a time that falls steeply with that share (pushing 40 N
  lasted under a third as long as 20 N).
- **Precision:** after fatigue, **aimed movements landed less accurately
  and more variably**, and the change went with **less co-contraction**
  (the muscles on both sides of a joint pulling against each other to
  hold it steady) rather than with a lack of force
  ([the role of co-contraction, Montpellier](https://esploro.umontpellier.fr/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-role-of-cocontraction-in-the/99136689309311);
  from its summary). **Variability rose most at the joints
  nearest the trunk** (the shoulder) ([PMC5325574](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5325574/)).
  Dart throwers were less accurate after muscular fatigue; baseball
  pitchers lost speed after any fatigue, and accuracy after throwing
  fatigue. **But people also change how they move to protect the goal**:
  in tracking and in repeated reaching, fatigued people kept what
  mattered steady while the rest of the movement varied more
  ([repetitive goal-directed movements](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18327575/)).
- **So, for a miner:** a tired blow comes down weaker (as ours does
  already), and lands less surely where it was aimed, more so for the
  shoulder than the wrist; and a tired miner partly makes up for it (it
  takes the tool shorter, it slows, it settles before it swings).

## 3. How games show it

- **Tiredness as a budget.** *Stardew Valley*: each tool use costs energy;
  at nothing the farmer is exhausted, moves and swings slower, and at a
  little below that passes out; it is a daily budget, a deliberate brake
  on pace ([wiki](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Exhaustion)).
- **Tiredness that throttles the work.** *Valheim*: each pick swing costs
  stamina; players time their swings to not run out, a copper node takes
  about fourteen seconds, and **many call the stamina gating of mining
  tedious** ([Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/892970/discussions/0/3731827313780376838/?ctp=3)).
- **The pleasure of the blow.** *Deep Rock Galactic*'s mining itself is
  praised: the terrain gives, the debris, the feedback (on PlayStation,
  the pad's vibration scales with the work) ([PlayStation blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=359486);
  reviews).
- **Tiredness in the body.** *Kingdom Come: Deliverance*: exhausted, its
  hero's movements are sluggish and his attacks weaker and slower
  (guides; whether aim suffers is not documented). *Arma 3*: fatigue
  adds to the weapon's sway and shortens how long breath can be held,
  which its developers wanted to be like real handling
  ([Bohemia, weapon sway and fatigue](https://dev.arma3.com/post/oprep-weapon-sway-fatigue)).
  *Dwarf Fortress*: tired dwarves work more slowly and do poorer work
  ([wiki](https://www.dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Sleep)).
- **Workers watched from above.** *Going Medieval*'s developers found idle
  settlers only scratching their heads and stretching, and planned for
  them to lean on walls, sit, talk ([devlog, from a summary](https://itch.io/devlog/1103666/upcoming-in-045.amp));
  *Manor Lords* animates its villagers' work piece by piece (reviews). A
  small mining game describes its swing in four beats (wind-up, strike,
  impact, recovery) with a light breathing in between so that it is never
  still.

## 4. How film and animation show it

- **As rhythm and song.** In Disney's *Snow White* (1937) the dwarfs dig
  in their mine to "Dig Dig Dig" and walk home to "Heigh-Ho": the work is
  cheerful and on the beat ([Snow White museum](https://snowwhitemuseum.com/images/production-art/sequence-4a-dwarfs-at-mine/)).
  In *Princess Mononoke* the women of Irontown work the great bellows
  together, with a work song whose words Miyazaki wrote
  ([Ghibli wiki](https://ghibli.fandom.com/wiki/Toki_(Princess_Mononoke))).
  The chain gang's hammer song is in films as well (*O Brother, Where Art
  Thou?* opens with one †).
- **As the toll.** Claude Berri's *Germinal* (1993) rebuilt a working
  mine above and below ground, and did not gloss over what working
  underground did to people ([Roger Ebert](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/germinal-1994);
  [ReelViews](https://www.reelviews.net/reelviews/germinal)).
- **Weight through timing** (the research of the morning, section 3):
  uneven timing reads as heavy, even timing as purposeful; a breath, made
  a little larger than life, can read as more real than life.

## 5. How a tired body reads

Several things at once, never one alone
([acting notes and guides](https://medium.com/@nova.salvi/what-work-does-to-the-body-e15ae035e5ba), and †):
its posture sags (shoulders forward, back rounded); its movements slow
and get clumsier; **its breath shows**, the shoulders rising and falling;
**it leans** on what is near (for a miner, its pickaxe); its hands go to
its knees or thighs to take its weight; it wipes its brow; and, after
the hardest work, it stands a moment before it does anything. A slouch
alone could mean anything.

## 6. What we hold the work to

| | Held to | From |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | **A rhythm of its own**: each miner swings at a steady pace its body sets (Round heavier and slower, Small lighter and quicker), the same blow after blow while it is fresh | Hand drillers; work songs; G7 |
| W2 | **The breath goes with the blow**: drawn in on the lift, let out on the strike | Work songs; the breath already drawn on the miners |
| W3 | **Short pauses, taken as it needs them**: every so often it straightens, breathes, shifts its grip, for a second or two, more often as it tires; not on a clock, and never all three miners at once | Short breaks; self-pacing; Luis: no switch at a threshold |
| W4 | **A real rest when it is spent**: it stands, breathes hard, leans on its pickaxe or puts a hand to its knee, and goes on when it has its strength back; the rest long enough that most of its strength comes back, which is much in the first moments and less after | The rest allowances; how strength comes back |
| W5 | **Tired, it is weaker**: blows slower and lighter, its hand further up the handle (as it is already) | Strength under fatigue |
| W6 | **Tired, it is less sure**: its blows wander more from where they were aimed, from the shoulder most, because it holds its joints less steadily; it partly makes up for it by slowing and settling before it swings | Precision under fatigue |
| W7 | **Its tiredness shows in its whole body**: posture, breath, pace and the pauses together | How a tired body reads |
| W8 | **The work itself stays a pleasure to watch**: each blow lands with its weight, its sound, its debris; tiredness slows and colours the work, it does not stop it for long or make it tedious | Players on Valheim and Deep Rock Galactic |

**The balance I set from this, for the game** (a figure to judge, not
law; a game's day is shorter than a real one):

- **A miner works about two thirds to three quarters of the time and
  rests the rest**, heavier work and a weaker body resting more. (A real
  day of such work rests two fifths; the game's rhythm is quicker so that
  the rest is seen often enough to read, and short enough not to bore.)
- **A stretch of work of about forty seconds to a minute and a half**
  before a real rest, with short pauses in it, and **a real rest of about
  ten to twenty seconds**, in which most of its strength comes back.
- With the stronger arms, Round now first rests at swing 48 (about a
  minute and a half of swinging), which is at the long end of that; the
  short pauses (W3), and tiredness taking precision too (W6), make the
  stretch read as work without making it shorter by decree. **I leave its
  arms' tiring as the physics gives it**, and measure the whole cycle
  again once W3 and W6 are built.

## 8. What was built from it (the same day; work branch, not in a build)

- **W6, tiredness takes precision** (Luis: "tiredness should also
  influence strength and precision"; strength it did already). Tired, a
  miner holds its tool less steadily (it closes on where the tool is
  meant to be less briskly: by half for arms wholly spent) and less
  evenly (what its arms give wavers: 2% fresh, 35% more for arms wholly
  spent). Round, 64 swings: its fresh blows (6, under a tenth spent)
  spread 10 mm, its tired ones (24, over three tenths) 26 mm, and came
  down 8% slower (6.30 against 5.81 m/s through upright).
- **W3, breaths between blows.** Now and then it lets its pick's head
  rest where the blow left it, straightens a little and breathes, 0.9 to
  2.2 s: none while fresh, then more often the more spent it is, up to
  three blows in ten when nearly spent enough to rest; each miner on its
  own chances. With them Round first rests at swing 55 (49 without).
- **The pace** (R1, left to me): the arms' tiring is left as the physics
  gives it.
- **Not yet:** W2 (the breath with the blow), W4 (leaning on the pickaxe,
  a hand to the knee in a rest), W7 as a whole; and none of it looked at
  in pictures or played.
